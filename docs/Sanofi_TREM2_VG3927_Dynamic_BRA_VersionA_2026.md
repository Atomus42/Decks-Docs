## Slide 1: The Benefit-Risk Challenge for TREM2 Agonism After INVOKE-2

**Headline:** The INVOKE-2 Failure Has Made Dynamic, Continuously Updated Benefit-Risk Assessment a Prerequisite — Not an Option — for VG-3927's Clinical Development

**Content:**

**The INVOKE-2 precedent has fundamentally altered the benefit-risk landscape for TREM2 agonism:**

AL002 (Alector/AbbVie), the first TREM2 agonist to reach Phase 2, failed comprehensively in INVOKE-2: 381 early AD patients, 48-96 weeks of treatment, no benefit on the CDR-SB primary endpoint, no improvement on any secondary endpoint (ADAS-Cog14, ADCS-ADL-MCI), no amyloid PET reduction — despite confirmed pharmacodynamic target engagement. Simultaneously, ARIA-E occurred in 29% and ARIA-H in 27% of treated patients, at rates comparable to anti-amyloid antibodies but without the corresponding amyloid clearance (INVOKE-2 analysis, *Front Aging Neurosci*, 2025). Denali/Takeda subsequently discontinued DNL919, citing a "narrow therapeutic window."

**VG-3927 enters this landscape with genuine pharmacological differentiation — but against a complex and evolving competitive field:**

| Axis | VG-3927 (Sanofi) | AL002 (Alector/AbbVie) | VHB937 (Novartis) | Anti-amyloid Abs (lecanemab, donanemab) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modality | Oral small molecule | IV monoclonal antibody | IV monoclonal antibody | IV monoclonal antibodies |
| Status | Phase 2 planned | Phase 2 FAILED (Nov 2024) | Phase 2 recruiting | Approved / submitted |
| TREM2 mechanism | Molecular glue; potentiates endogenous signaling | Direct agonism; promotes receptor internalization | IgSF-domain binder; stabilizes surface TREM2 | Not TREM2-targeting |
| Fc domain | None | Yes — engages perivascular macrophages | Yes | Yes |
| ARIA profile | No Fc = lower expected ARIA risk | 29% ARIA-E, 27% ARIA-H | To be determined | 12.6-24% ARIA-E |

**Why a static, one-time benefit-risk assessment is insufficient for VG-3927:**

The benefit-risk landscape that VG-3927 must navigate is not fixed — it is evolving on multiple fronts simultaneously:

1. **INVOKE-2 post-hoc analyses** continue to be published, generating new hypotheses about TREM2 agonist failure modes, APOE4-stratified safety data, and biomarker insights that directly affect VG-3927's positioning
2. **VHB937 (Novartis)** is recruiting for Phase 2 — its readout will redefine the competitive landscape for TREM2 agonism. VHB937's biomarker profile (dose-dependent SPP1, sCSF1R, and CCL3 reduction) will provide critical mechanistic comparator data
3. **Lecanemab and donanemab long-term safety data** are accumulating in the post-marketing setting, reshaping the AD treatment landscape into which VG-3927 must integrate — including as a potential combination partner
4. **TREM2 biology publications** continue at pace — new evidence on stage-dependent microglial activation (Ulland & Colonna, *Nat Rev Neurol*, 2018), expression-level-dependent efficacy (*Mol Neurodegeneration*, 2025 review), and APOE-TREM2 interactions (Yeh et al., *Neuron*, 2016) will recalibrate the benefit-risk calculus
5. **APOE4 genotype safety stratification** is critical and unresolved — INVOKE-2 excluded APOE4 homozygotes mid-trial after severe ARIA events, a reactive decision that VG-3927's Phase 2 must address proactively

**Risk minimization strategies must be designed now, before Phase 2 enrollment, and updated continuously as data accrues.** The benefit-risk rationale that justifies Phase 2 entry will not be the same rationale that supports regulatory submission. Between those two points, every new publication, every competitor readout, every safety signal changes the evidence base. This is exactly what a prospective, dynamic benefit-risk assessment platform is designed to manage.
