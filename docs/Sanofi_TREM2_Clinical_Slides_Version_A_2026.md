## Slide 1: TREM2 Agonism in Alzheimer's Disease — Mechanistic Complexity Demands Multi-Dimensional Benefit-Risk Assessment

**Headline:** The TREM2/DAP12 Signaling Axis Presents a Benefit-Risk Profile of Unusual Mechanistic Complexity

**Content:**

TREM2 (Triggering Receptor Expressed on Myeloid Cells 2) is a type I transmembrane glycoprotein signaling through the DAP12/TYROBP adaptor. In the CNS, expression is restricted almost exclusively to microglia. Genetic evidence for TREM2 as a therapeutic target in AD is among the strongest in the neurodegeneration field:

- **R47H heterozygous variant:** OR = 2.9-4.1 for late-onset AD (Jonsson et al., *NEJM*, 2013; Guerreiro et al., *NEJM*, 2013)
- **R47H homozygous variant:** OR = 97.1 (Bellenguez et al., *NEJM*, 2024)
- **Biallelic TREM2 loss-of-function (Nasu-Hakola disease):** Fatal early-onset frontotemporal dementia with leukoencephalopathy
- **TREM2 required for Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM) Stage 2 activation:** Microglia cannot transition to the neuroprotective DAM phenotype without functional TREM2 (Keren-Shaul et al., *Cell*, 2017)
- **Higher CSF sTREM2 correlates with reduced cognitive decline** in amyloid-positive individuals (Ewers et al., *Sci Transl Med*, 2019)
- **TREM2 binds APOE, facilitating microglial uptake of amyloid-beta** (Yeh et al., *Neuron*, 2016)

However, this strong genetic rationale is matched by equally significant mechanistic complexities that create a narrow and context-dependent therapeutic window:

1. **Stage-dependent directionality:** TREM2 agonism appears protective during amyloid-dominant early AD but potentially harmful when tau pathology predominates. Preclinical data show AL002a enhanced tau seeding and spreading in tau-dominant models.
2. **Peripheral myeloid activation:** TREM2 is not CNS-exclusive. Systemic agonism risks activating macrophages, osteoclasts, and dendritic cells, with potential consequences for bone remodeling, inflammation, and immune surveillance.
3. **Infection susceptibility:** TREM2 activation can exacerbate tissue damage during respiratory viral and hepatic infections — a material concern in elderly AD patients with baseline immunosenescence.
4. **Expression-level-dependent efficacy:** TREM2 expression level determines microglial state and therapeutic response (*Nature Communications*, 2026), meaning patient-level TREM2 expression heterogeneity directly modulates treatment effect.
5. **Demyelination risk:** TREM2 agonist antibodies disrupted injury resolution in cuprizone demyelination models (*J Neuroscience*, 2024).
6. **ARIA-like MRI changes:** Most frequent adverse event in the INVOKE-2 trial; incidence higher in APOE-epsilon-4 homozygotes.

**Relevance:** This slide establishes the scientific premise for the entire presentation. Sanofi's scientists know this biology; the point is to frame TREM2 agonism as a target class where standard benefit-risk assessment methods — designed for more straightforward pharmacology — are insufficient. The multi-dimensional mechanistic complexity (stage-dependence, genotype-dependence, expression-level-dependence, peripheral effects, infection interactions) demands a correspondingly multi-dimensional benefit-risk prediction framework.

## Slide 2: VG-3927 in the Post-INVOKE-2 Landscape — A Differentiated Mechanism Facing an Undifferentiated Evidentiary Challenge

**Headline:** VG-3927's Molecular Glue Mechanism Provides Pharmacological Differentiation from AL002, but the Benefit-Risk Evidence Landscape Remains Fragmented and Under-Synthesized

**Content:**

**The INVOKE-2 precedent and its implications for VG-3927:**

AL002 (anti-TREM2 monoclonal antibody) failed in Phase 2 (INVOKE-2: 381 patients, 76-week treatment; failed CDR-SB primary endpoint and all secondary endpoints; published *Nature Medicine*, 2026). This is the defining dataset for TREM2 agonism clinical development. Denali/Takeda subsequently cited a "narrow therapeutic window" as the basis for discontinuing DNL919 (anti-TREM2 antibody).

**VG-3927's pharmacological differentiation (Phase 1 data, 115 participants):**

| Parameter | VG-3927 | AL002 |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| Modality | Oral small molecule | IV monoclonal antibody |
| Mechanism | Molecular glue — potentiates TREM2 signaling in response to natural damage ligands | Direct receptor agonism |
| TREM2 binding preference | Preferentially binds membrane TREM2 over sTREM2 | Binds both |
| BBB penetration | CSF-to-unbound-plasma ratio 0.91 | Limited CNS penetration (typical of mAbs) |
| Dosing | Once-daily oral, dose-dependent sTREM2 reduction (~50% at 25mg) | IV infusion |
| Tolerability (Phase 1) | Well tolerated (n=115) | ARIA-like MRI changes as most frequent AE |

These are genuine mechanistic differentiators. The molecular glue mechanism — amplifying endogenous TREM2 signaling at sites of damage ligand accumulation rather than constitutive receptor activation — provides a plausible pharmacological basis for a more physiological, spatially restricted agonism.

**The unresolved evidentiary challenge:**

The differentiation argument is necessary but not sufficient for Phase 2 design. The benefit-risk evidence base for TREM2 agonism is:

- **Fragmented across literature, preclinical datasets, INVOKE-2 clinical data, and multiple pharmacovigilance databases** — no integrated synthesis exists
- **Genotype-stratified only in retrospective analyses** — TREM2 R47H, APOE-epsilon-4, and compound genotype interactions are incompletely characterized for safety
- **Stage-dependent in a way that preclinical models poorly predict** — the amyloid-vs-tau directionality problem has not been resolved clinically
- **Complicated by multi-dimensional patient heterogeneity** — TREM2 genotype x APOE genotype x disease stage x baseline TREM2 expression x baseline microglial activation state

**The Phase 2 design question:** How does Sanofi define enrichment criteria, stratification variables, safety monitoring thresholds, and stopping rules when the benefit-risk landscape is this multi-dimensional and the primary clinical precedent (INVOKE-2) failed?

**Relevance:** This slide reframes the challenge from "VG-3927 is different from AL002" (which Sanofi's scientists already know) to "the evidentiary infrastructure for benefit-risk prediction in this mechanism class is fragmented in ways that affect Phase 2 design decisions." It sets up the need for systematic, multi-source evidence synthesis — not as a general capability, but as a specific requirement for this program.

## Slide 4: Post-INVOKE-2 Evidence Framework for VG-3927 Phase 2 Design — How Systematic Multi-Source Synthesis Supports Regulatory and Scientific Rigor

**Headline:** A Structured, Auditable Evidence Framework to Support VG-3927 Phase 2 Protocol Design, Enrichment Strategy, and Regulatory Justification

**Content:**

**The regulatory challenge:** Sanofi must justify VG-3927 Phase 2 design to regulators in a mechanism class where the lead clinical candidate (AL002) has failed. The differentiation argument (oral molecular glue vs. IV mAb, preferential membrane binding, superior CNS penetration) is pharmacologically sound but must be supported by a comprehensive, structured evidence package that:

1. Systematically characterizes the INVOKE-2 failure mode to demonstrate that VG-3927's mechanism addresses identified limitations
2. Provides subpopulation-specific safety predictions to inform enrichment and monitoring
3. Demonstrates the evidentiary basis for proposed biomarker stratification
4. Satisfies BRAT/CIOMS XII methodology requirements for structured benefit-risk evaluation

**What ArcaScience's platform produces for this program:**

**A. Multi-source TREM2 evidence synthesis**
- Automated extraction and structuring across the fragmented TREM2 literature (PubMed/MEDLINE, 36M+ indexed citations)
- Integration of INVOKE-2 published results (*Nature Medicine*, 2026), VG-3927 Phase 1 data, and preclinical evidence
- Cross-referencing with pharmacovigilance databases (FAERS, EudraVigilance, VigiBase, JADER) for analogous mechanism safety signals
- Output: structured, source-linked evidence map of TREM2 agonism benefit-risk landscape with per-finding traceability

**B. Subpopulation-specific B/R modeling**
- Stratified analysis: TREM2 genotype (R47H heterozygous, R47H homozygous, common variant) x APOE genotype (epsilon-2/3/4) x disease stage (preclinical, prodromal, mild, moderate)
- Safety signal prediction by subpopulation: ARIA probability, infection susceptibility, peripheral activation risk
- Enrichment threshold identification: which patient subpopulations have a favorable predicted B/R ratio for VG-3927?
- Output: subpopulation-specific B/R matrices with supporting evidence packages for each cell

**C. Combination therapy B/R assessment**
- TREM2 agonist + anti-amyloid antibody (lecanemab, donanemab) combination rationale is scientifically compelling (TREM2-mediated microglial phagocytosis of antibody-opsonized amyloid) but introduces additive ARIA risk
- Multi-source safety modeling: cumulative ARIA probability, infection risk, peripheral myeloid effects under combination regimens
- Output: structured combination B/R assessment with evidence-graded risk estimates

**D. Regulatory-ready structured outputs**
- BRAT framework implementation for VG-3927 benefit-risk assessment
- Pre-populated CTD Module 2.5 benefit-risk sections with source-linked evidence
- Gap analysis: what evidence exists, what is missing, what requires prospective collection in Phase 2
- All outputs auditable at per-element level (92% precision on AE extraction vs. 67% GPT-4; Chen et al., *AI in Medicine*, 2025; 94% F1 score on NLP AE extraction; Rodriguez et al., *BMC Medical Informatics*, 2024)
