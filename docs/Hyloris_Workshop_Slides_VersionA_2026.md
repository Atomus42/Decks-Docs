## Slide 1: The 505(b)(2) Evidence Burden — What Regulators Actually Require, and Where Repurposing Candidates Fail

*Duration: ~7 minutes (3 min presentation, 4 min guided discussion)*

**Headline:** A 505(b)(2) Applicant Must Demonstrate an Evidence-Based Benefit-Risk Comparison Against the Reference Listed Drug — Most Repurposing Candidates Fail Not on Science, But on Evidence Assembly

---

**Content:**

**The regulatory architecture of the 505(b)(2) pathway:**

The 505(b)(2) new drug application, codified under Section 505(b)(2) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. 355(b)(2)), permits an applicant to rely in part on FDA's prior findings of safety and efficacy for a Reference Listed Drug (RLD) — but it does not eliminate the obligation to present a complete, structured benefit-risk evaluation of the proposed product. FDA Guidance for Industry, "Applications Covered by Section 505(b)(2)" (October 1999, updated 2019), explicitly requires that the applicant provide:

1. **A complete Clinical Overview (eCTD Module 2.5)** — including Section 2.5.6, the Benefit-Risk Assessment, which must be structured to address:
   - 2.5.6.1 Therapeutic Context — the disease, its burden, and current treatment options
   - 2.5.6.2 Benefits — the clinical benefits of the proposed product, with comparison to the RLD
   - 2.5.6.3 Risks — the identified and potential risks, risk management strategies, and comparison to the RLD safety profile
   - 2.5.6.4 Benefit-Risk Assessment — the integrated assessment and conclusion

2. **Bridging evidence** — demonstrating that the applicant's product can rely on the RLD's data while also establishing the specific modifications (new formulation, new route, new indication) and their impact on the benefit-risk profile

3. **A structured safety comparison** — under ICH M4E(R2) ("The Common Technical Document for the Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use: Efficacy," Module 2.5), the Clinical Overview must present the safety profile of the proposed product in the context of the known safety profile of the RLD

**The evidence gap that kills 505(b)(2) reformulation candidates:**

FDA's Refuse to File (RTF) and Complete Response Letters (CRLs) for 505(b)(2) applications frequently cite deficiencies not in the clinical data themselves, but in the structure, completeness, and regulatory alignment of the benefit-risk narrative. Common deficiency categories include:

| Deficiency Type | Regulatory Citation | What Goes Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete therapeutic context | eCTD 2.5.6.1, ICH M4E(R2) | Disease landscape and RLD positioning described qualitatively rather than with structured, source-linked evidence |
| Benefit claims unsupported by comparative data | eCTD 2.5.6.2, ICH E1/E9 | Reformulation benefit (e.g., reduced GI AEs, improved bioavailability) asserted without quantitative comparison to RLD |
| Safety comparison not structured to CIOMS/BRAT standards | eCTD 2.5.6.3, CIOMS XII, ICH E2C(R2) | RLD safety profile presented as a narrative summary rather than a structured, normalised adverse event comparison |
| Benefit-risk conclusion not integrated | eCTD 2.5.6.4, FDA BRAD guidance (2018) | Benefits and risks discussed separately without an integrated framework — no value tree, no weighting, no explicit B-R comparison |
| Missing risk management for formulation-specific risks | ICH E2E, ICH E2C(R2) | New excipient risks, route-change risks, or dose-proportionality risks not prospectively addressed |

**The structural problem for lean specialty pharma:**

Large pharmaceutical companies maintain 20-50 person regulatory writing teams that spend 6-18 months assembling eCTD Module 2.5 submissions. For a 50-person company like Hyloris, targeting sub-EUR 7M total development cost per product, this evidence assembly burden is the rate-limiting step — not the clinical trial itself. The PK bridging study for a 505(b)(2) reformulation may take 6-9 months and cost EUR 1-3M. The evidence assembly for the benefit-risk sections of the NDA may take an equivalent period and consume an equivalent share of the development budget when outsourced to CROs or regulatory consultancies.

**The thesis of this workshop:**

What if the evidence base that informed the decision to pursue a reformulation candidate — the disease analysis, the adverse event comparison, the clinical endpoint mapping, the benefit-risk assessment — was already structured in the format required for the 505(b)(2) filing? What if scouting evidence and filing evidence were a single, continuous thread?

**The glioblastoma proof point — this methodology is already validated:**

ArcaScience has already demonstrated the upstream screening methodology that powers Phase 1 of the evidence thread. In collaboration with the Paris Brain Institute (not a large pharmaceutical company), the platform screened 100 marketed drugs for glioblastoma repurposing potential:

- **Method:** Used adverse event data as a pharmacological proxy signal — drugs causing CNS-related AEs (visual disturbance, headache, hearing impairment) must cross the blood-brain barrier, a prerequisite for glioblastoma efficacy. AE profiles were extracted at 92% precision, normalised to MedDRA, and structured by frequency and severity.
- **Result:** 3 drugs identified as compatible with glioblastoma. 2 are now in Phase 2 clinical trials.
- **Structural parallel to Hyloris:** The same AE-as-signal methodology applies to reformulation screening. A drug with high rates of injection-site reactions signals an IV-to-oral or RTU reformulation opportunity. A drug with dose-dependent GI toxicity signals an extended-release opportunity. A drug with peak-concentration-related CNS effects signals a modified-release opportunity. The adverse event profile becomes a map of reformulation potential.
