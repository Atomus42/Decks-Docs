**The strategic pivot that changes the analytical equation:**

Hyloris's first wave of portfolio building relied heavily on in-licensing: identifying pre-characterized candidates from external sources (RedHill, AFT Pharmaceuticals, academic groups) and applying Hyloris's 505(b)(2) regulatory and formulation expertise to develop them. This is capital-efficient but dependent on deal flow --- the pipeline grows only as fast as licensing opportunities appear.

Stijn Van Rompay's strategic direction is clear: shift toward proprietary, internally-originated, patent-protected reformulated and repurposed products. This shift moves Hyloris up the value chain --- from executing others' reformulation ideas to generating its own --- but it transfers the analytical burden of candidate identification from external licensors to Hyloris's internal BD team.

**The arithmetic of manual scouting at portfolio-building velocity:**

| Dimension | Current Manual Process | Required at 30-Asset Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Candidates evaluated per year** | ~15-20 (estimated, across all therapeutic areas) | 30-50+ (to maintain pipeline velocity after attrition) |
| **Time per candidate evaluation** | 2-4 weeks (KOL polling, desk research, literature review, label comparison) | Same 2-4 weeks --- linear scaling with headcount |
| **Evidence sources per evaluation** | PubMed search, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA labels, market reports | Same sources --- no systematic AE analysis, no structured B/R framework |
| **Output format** | Unstructured PowerPoint/Word for Product Selection Committee | Same format --- no regulatory pre-alignment, no eCTD 2.5.6 structure |
| **Team capacity** | BD team of ~3-5 people (estimated for a 50-person company) | Would require doubling BD headcount to double throughput |
| **Therapeutic area coverage** | 2-3 areas per year at depth | 6+ areas required to meet diversification targets |

**The gap:** Hyloris's Product Selection Committee requires structured, evidence-based rationale for candidate selection decisions. The manual process produces qualitative assessments that are analyst-dependent, not reproducible, and disconnected from the eventual 505(b)(2) filing requirements. Scaling proprietary identification at the velocity Hyloris requires means either (a) hiring 5-10 additional BD analysts, or (b) deploying a platform that compresses evaluation time from weeks to days while simultaneously improving evidence quality and regulatory alignment.

**Phase 3: Deep-Dive Benefit-Risk Assessment (Platform delivery: 2-3 weeks per candidate)**

**What happens:**
- For each of the 3-5 priority candidates, ArcaScience produces a full Benefit-Risk Assessment:
  - Benefits of existing formulation (the RLD): established efficacy, physician familiarity, formulary positioning, cost
  - Risks of existing formulation: the formulation-dependent AEs identified in Phase 1, quantified with frequency, severity, and treatment discontinuation rates
  - Projected benefits of reformulated product: improved bioavailability, reduced AEs, improved compliance, nursing time reduction, hospital stay reduction
  - Projected risks of reformulated product: new excipient risks, bioequivalence uncertainty, formulation stability, new route-specific AEs
  - Net benefit-risk delta: the quantitative, evidence-based case for why this reformulation is worth pursuing
- Output structured as a BRAT (Benefit-Risk Action Team) value tree with configurable weighting, aligned to eCTD Module 2.5.6
