## 1. Strategic Priorities and Transformation Agenda (2024-2026)

### Corporate Plan 2023-2026: Four Strategic Priorities

The MHRA's Corporate Plan, covering 2023-2026, organises the agency's ambitions around four strategic priorities:

1. **Maintain public trust** through transparency and proactive communication
2. **Enable healthcare access** to safe and effective medical products
3. **Deliver scientific and regulatory excellence** through strategic partnerships
4. **Become an agency where people flourish** alongside a responsive customer service culture

Source: [MHRA Corporate Plan 2023 to 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mhra-corporate-plan-2023-to-2026)

### Business Plan 2025-2026 (Final Year)

The final Business Plan for the 2023-2026 Corporate Plan period was published in mid-2025, with four refined pillars:

1. Protecting public safety and maintaining public trust
2. Delivering efficient, predictable services through regulatory excellence
3. Being an agile organisation that drives innovation
4. Being a great place to work and providing excellent customer service

Source: [MHRA Business Plan 2025-2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/685aa804e9509f1a908eb121/Business-plan-Final.pdf)

### Data Strategy 2024-2027

The MHRA launched its **first-ever Data Strategy** in 2024, built around five core themes:

- Enable effective, timely, and proportionate regulatory decision-making through Real-World Evidence
- Develop, extend, and integrate capabilities in data and digital technologies
- Establish, embed, and expand synergistic partnerships across the data ecosystem
- Safely and responsibly harness the potential of AI and advanced analytics throughout the product lifecycle
- Proactively address legacy systems and technical debt through investment in legacy eradication and architecture development

The **RegulatoryConnect** system was intended as the centrepiece digital platform -- a "single point of truth" to minimise duplication and prevent siloing. It launched in March 2024 but was **shut down in November 2025**, less than two years after launch, because "the cost to complete the programme was considered too high for a solution which did not enable delivery of the aspirations of the agency."

The MHRA is now developing a **new multi-year strategy through 2030**, to be launched in early 2026, which will revisit technological needs and embed principles of AI.

Source: [MHRA Data Strategy 2024-2027](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mhra-data-strategy-2024-2027/mhra-data-strategy-2024-2027)

### Key Transformation Deliverables

| Pre-Market Devices SI | Being developed | Expected Parliament late 2025, force 2026 |

### Financial Self-Sufficiency Goal

The MHRA's ambition is to become **financially self-sufficient from the DHSC** for all day-to-day operational responsibilities during the Corporate Plan period. However, it cannot use fee income for capital investment and will continue to rely on the Department for capital funding and new government priorities.

**Pitch relevance:** The MHRA is in a period of deep transformation with significant technology failures (RegulatoryConnect cancellation), modernisation needs, and a new CEO. The new 2030 strategy currently being developed presents a window of opportunity for technology partnerships. The explicit emphasis on data, AI, and real-world evidence in the Data Strategy aligns directly with ArcaScience's capabilities.

## 2. ILAP: Innovative Licensing and Access Pathway

### First Iteration (2021-2024)

- Launched **January 2021** as the world's only end-to-end access pathway
- **166 Innovation Passports** awarded
- Acceptance rate: approximately **50%** of applications
- Therapeutic breakdown: **oncology 39%**, rare genetic disorders 22%, metabolic disorders 6%
- Most products entered at early stage (Phase 1 or 2)
- Of 90 ILAP participants evaluated: **13 recommended by NICE**, 2 discontinued, 1 withdrawn
- **32%** of ILAP awardees received FDA Fast Track designation

### Suspension and Criticism

ILAP was **paused in November 2024** based on feedback that:
- "The offer was not clear enough"
- "The entry criteria were overly permissive leading to the pathway becoming unsustainable"
- "There was a lack of direct NHS involvement to support timely adoption of ILAP products"
- ISPOR Europe 2024 finding: "lack of transparency on the ILAP, as there are no official publicly available resources to identify innovation passport awardees"

### Relaunch (January 2025)

On **30 January 2025**, the government relaunched ILAP with major changes:
- More selective entry criteria
- NHS England now a core partner (focus on operational planning and system preparedness)
- Limited to medicines that have **not yet entered confirmatory trials**
- Expanded to include **drug-device combinations**
- First application window opened **31 March 2025**
- Innovation Passport fee: **GBP 3,624** (non-refundable)

Partners: MHRA, NHS England, NICE, SMC, AWTTC, DHSC, Office for Life Sciences

**Complication:** The UK Government has since **abolished the administrative body of NHS England**, raising questions about the future of NHS involvement in ILAP.

Sources: [ILAP GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/innovative-licensing-and-access-pathway-ilap), [ILAP Relaunch](https://www.insideeulifesciences.com/2025/02/14/re-launch-of-uks-innovative-licensing-and-access-pathway-ilap/)

**Pitch relevance:** ILAP is the flagship "innovation" pathway but has struggled with implementation. The MHRA needs tools that can support more rigorous evidence assembly for ILAP products, particularly in the post-authorisation evidence generation phase. The relaunch's emphasis on "bespoke" pathways and "more predictable timelines" signals a need for technology-enabled efficiency.

## 3. AI/ML in Drug Regulation and Pharmacovigilance

### MHRA Strategic Approach to AI (April 2024)

On **30 April 2024**, the MHRA published "Impact of AI on the regulation of medical products," its response to the UK Government's pro-innovation AI white paper. Built around five UK AI White Paper principles:

1. Safety, security and robustness
2. Appropriate transparency and explainability
3. Fairness
4. Accountability and governance
5. Contestability and redress

Key positions:
- Existing pharmacovigilance regulations do not specifically reference AI, but quality management system requirements apply
- AI may change the pace of drug development, impact clinical trial design, and enable personalised medicines
- The fundamental questions regulators ask "do not change when the nature of the evidence changes"

Source: [MHRA AI Strategic Approach](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/662fce1e9e82181baa98a988/MHRA_Impact-of-AI-on-the-regulation-of-medical-products.pdf)

### AI Airlock Regulatory Sandbox

- Launched **May 2024** -- a world-first regulatory "sandbox" for AI-powered medical devices
- **Five technologies selected** for the pilot scheme
- Pilot ran until **April 2025**
- Findings intended to inform future AI Airlock projects and influence UK AI medical device guidance

Source: [MHRA AI Airlock](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2024/12/mhra-selects-five-ai-powered-medical-devices-for-regulatory-pilot/)

### National Commission on AI in Healthcare (September 2025)

- Launched September 2025 to develop recommendations for a "world-leading framework for the regulation of AI in healthcare"
- Call for evidence: **18 December 2025 to 2 February 2026** -- seeking input from public, clinicians, industry, and healthcare providers
- Recommendations expected for the MHRA

Source: [MHRA AI Consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhra-seeks-input-on-ai-regulation-at-pivotal-moment-for-healthcare)

### International Collaboration: Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP)

The **FDA, Health Canada, and MHRA** jointly identified **10 guiding principles** for GMLP, including:
- Multi-disciplinary expertise throughout the product lifecycle
- Integration of AI/ML models into clinical workflows
- Meeting patient safety and clinical needs

The **IMDRF GMLP document (N88, 2025)** establishes 10 guiding principles for safe, effective, and clinically relevant AI-powered medical devices.

### SafetyConnect: Pharmacovigilance Modernisation

The MHRA's pharmacovigilance modernisation programme, **SafetyConnect**, partners with **Insife** using the **HALO platform**:

- Background: MHRA's legacy PV system was "no longer fit for purpose" -- PV, medical devices, and bloods were on three separate systems (old legacy system, Lotus Notes, and Excel spreadsheets)
- Solution: Insife's HALO platform performs case management and operational reporting with AI-powered automation
- Yellow Card reports now integrated with HALO, flowing directly into signal detection software
- Signal detection via **Commonwealth Vigilance Workbench (CVW)** -- an integrated cloud-based signal management system
- Medical devices section went live **end of 2022**
- Medicines and vaccines section due to go live **mid-2024**

HALO platform features:
- 19 fully integrated PV modules
- AI-enabled capabilities: automated translation, content creation, data extraction from structured and unstructured documents
- Single source of truth (SSOT) for safety data

Sources: [Insife MHRA Partnership](https://www.insife.com/articles-mhra), [HaloPV Platform](https://www.halopv.com/)

**Pitch relevance:** This is critical intelligence. The MHRA is already investing in AI for pharmacovigilance through SafetyConnect/HALO, but this focuses on case management and signal detection workflow. ArcaScience's evidence structuring capability addresses a different layer -- the literature and evidence assembly phase that feeds into the benefit-risk assessment. The MHRA is clearly receptive to AI tools but demands auditability and integration into existing workflows.

## 4. International Recognition Framework and Reliance Pathways

### Medicines: International Recognition Procedure (IRP)

Operational since **1 January 2024**, replacing the EC Decision Reliance Procedure (ECDRP).

**Seven Reference Regulators:** EMA, FDA, Health Canada, Swissmedic, PMDA (Japan), TGA (Australia), HSA (Singapore)

**Two Routes:**
| Route | Review Time | Typical Use |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Route A | 60 days | EMA-approved dossiers |
| Route B | 110 days | Other reference regulator approvals |

**Key Performance Data:**
- Up to **40% of UK marketing authorisations** now go through the IRP (August 2025 data)
- First IRP approval: denosumab pre-filled syringe (Xgeva, Amgen), 29 February 2024
- By January 2025: **453 IRP submissions** (273 Type IB, 180 Type II) approved
- **95-97%** granted within statutory timeframe

Source: [MHRA IRP](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-recognition-procedure/international-recognition-procedure)

### Medical Devices: International Reliance Framework

- Policy proposal first published **February 2025**, updated October 2025
- Four countries identified with comparable systems: USA, Canada, EU, Australia
- MHRA proposes to rely on approvals from **USA, Canada, and Australia**
- Framework provides a **certificate of international reliance** (not UKCA marking)
- **FDA-MHRA formal collaboration** announced October 2025 to align regulatory processes for medical devices
- Expected to become **law in 2026**, with reliance routes active from **2027**

### Windsor Framework (1 January 2025)

- All applications must now be for **UK-wide MAs** (GB-only MAs no longer possible)
- Simplifies medicine supply to Northern Ireland
- But EU changes applying to Northern Ireland will deepen regulatory differences with Great Britain

Sources: [MHRA IRP Procedure](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-recognition-procedure/international-recognition-procedure), [Pharmaphorum on MHRA Reliance](https://pharmaphorum.com/market-access/looking-ahead-2025-and-mhra-reliance)

**Pitch relevance:** The IRP is a pragmatic response to post-Brexit capacity constraints. The fact that 40% of UK MAs now go through reliance pathways signals that the MHRA cannot independently assess all products with existing resources. ArcaScience could position itself as helping MHRA maintain quality in its independent sovereign assessments by accelerating evidence assembly.

### Regulatory Backlogs

**January 2024 performance data (Pharmaceutical Journal):**
- National route: average **333 days** vs. 210-day statutory target (**123 days over**)
- Reliance routes: average **195 days** vs. 67-day statutory target (**128 days over**)
- December 2023: Established medicines: **496 days** (286 days over target)
- As of 4 January 2024: **1,167 individual product licences** exceeding the 210-day approval limit

**Recovery actions:**
- From September 2023: triaging trial applications, reallocating staff, contracting additional assessors
- March 2024: new process requiring incomplete applications to be refused immediately
- By March 2025: MHRA reported **all statutory backlogs cleared**

**Clinical trial recovery:**
- **35.7% increase** in trial initiations in 2024 (578 trials, up from 426 in 2023)
- UK rose from **8th to 6th place** globally for initiation of Phase III trials

Source: [Pharmaceutical Journal: MHRA Approvals](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/mhra-approvals-miss-targets-by-more-than-100-days-data-show)

### Industry Confidence Erosion

The ABPI published *Enhancing the role of UK medicine regulation* on **4 December 2024**, finding:
- **80% of respondents** felt MHRA's lack of capacity was undermining industry trust and deterring domestic investment
- Flagged "capacity and predictability" as top concerns
- Described MHRA as having a "global reputation" but raised concerns about transparency, funding, and timeline predictability
- Called for regulators to be "sufficiently resourced to clear the current backlogs and co-develop new regulatory guidance"

### Parliamentary Criticism (January 2025)

A major Parliamentary debate on MHRA performance was held **16 January 2025**, covering:

- Yellow Card system failures and safety monitoring inadequacies
- MHRA funding and pharmaceutical industry influence
- Implementation of IMMDS ("First Do No Harm") review recommendations
- MPs stated: "We should not have to wait until media coverage publicly shames health scandals"
- Concerns about pelvic mesh, Primodos, and sodium valproate
- Question raised: "Who regulates the regulators?"

Source: [Hansard: MHRA Debate January 2025](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2025-01-16/debates/4BF8018B-9662-427B-A580-2EBB7770D164/MedicinesAndHealthcareProductsRegulatoryAgency)

### Post-Brexit Sovereignty Paradox

The MHRA faces a fundamental tension:
- **Sovereignty narrative:** UK aims for an independent, innovative regulatory system post-Brexit
- **Pragmatic reality:** 40% of UK MAs now go through international reliance pathways
- **Criticism:** Whether reliance is consistent with "Brexit freedoms" or merely ties the MHRA to other agencies' decisions
- **Academic analysis:** "Brexit is unlikely to eliminate market-based pressures for the UK's regulatory alignment with EU rules"

The medical devices regulatory reform, launched via consultation in **September 2021**, has been repeatedly delayed. The new substantive regime is described as "long-awaited."

Sources: [Parliamentary Briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CDP-2024-0172/CDP-2024-0172.pdf), [Pharmaphorum: MHRA Reliance](https://pharmaphorum.com/market-access/looking-ahead-2025-and-mhra-reliance)

**Pitch relevance:** These pain points are the core of ArcaScience's value proposition. With ~20% vacancy rate, 40% historical staff loss, 80+ concurrent safety investigations across 600+ drugs, and only ~100 benefit-risk assessors, the MHRA is structurally under-resourced. Tools that reduce the manual effort of evidence assembly directly address the capacity crisis without requiring new headcount.

## 6. Yellow Card Scheme and ADR Reporting Statistics

### Scale of Operation

| Metric | Value | Period |
|--------|-------|--------|
| ADR reports assessed (annual) | **>100,000** | 2024-25 |
| Cumulative ADR reports (total) | **1,345,712** | 1963-2024 |
| Fatal reports | **61,944 (5%)** | Cumulative |
| Serious but non-fatal reports | **874,564 (65%)** | Cumulative |
| Reports with pharmacogenomic relevance | **115,789 (9%)** | Cumulative |
| Northern Ireland reports | **797** | 2023/24 (up 32%) |

### Under-Reporting Problem

This is a critical systemic issue:
- Estimated that only **10% of serious reactions** are reported
- Only **2-4% of non-serious reactions** are reported
- Median under-reporting rate across 37 studies: **94%** (IQR 82-98%)
- The IMMDS 2020 review concluded: "the Yellow Card system is **too complex and too diffuse to allow early signal detection**"
- Under-reporting "makes it difficult to spot safety signals and assign causation... this translates into unnecessary harm or death, with devastating side effects from treatment going unnoticed for years, months or even decades"
- January 2025 Parliamentary debate: "the yellow card system for reporting suspected adverse drug reactions is failing, with no process for following up on serious or fatal reactions"

### Data Access

- Interactive Drug Analysis Profiles (iDAPs) available at [yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/idaps](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/idaps)
- Time delay of approximately **one month** from receipt to appearance in summary
- Data sources: healthcare professionals, members of the public, and pharmaceutical companies

Sources: [MHRA Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhras-2024-25-annual-report-and-accounts-and-impact-report-show-progress-on-safety-innovation-and-regulatory-excellence), [PLOS Medicine Study 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1004565)

**Pitch relevance:** With 94% under-reporting and the Yellow Card system criticised as "too complex and too diffuse to allow early signal detection," the MHRA needs tools that can compensate by extracting safety signals from published literature and public databases. ArcaScience's evidence structuring from published sources (PubMed, FAERS, EudraVigilance public data) directly addresses this gap -- supplementing the under-reported spontaneous reporting data with systematic literature-derived evidence.

## 7. Real-World Evidence and Adaptive Licensing

### Pilot RWE Dialogue Programme (January 2025)

Launched **13 January 2025**, running throughout 2025:
- Creates a "safe harbour environment" for commercially sensitive discussions between applicants and MHRA
- Aims to increase clarity of regulatory and HTA expectations for data sources, datasets, methodologies, and outcome measurements
- Learning will inform future MHRA-NICE-industry collaborations
- Part of the wider Data Strategy 2024-2027 and the **Pro-innovation Regulations of Technologies review** (2023)

Source: [Ropes & Gray: MHRA RWE Programme](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102jtqu/mhra-launches-the-2025-pilot-real-world-evidence-dialogue-programme-implementin)

### Rare Disease Regulatory Reform (November 2025)

A landmark policy paper released **2 November 2025** proposed sweeping reforms:

- **Investigational Marketing Authorization:** Flexible licensing for rare disease therapies with adaptive trial design
- **Early approvals** based on limited but scientifically grounded evidence (e.g., plausible mechanism of action), with ongoing reassessment
- **Flexible evidence:** Surrogate endpoints, natural history comparators, and real-world data accepted when traditional trials are not feasible
- **Pre-Designation Dialogue** required before entering the pathway
- Supported by the newly formed **Rare Disease Consortium** (MHRA, NICE, DHSC, NHS England, patients, academia, industry)
- Draft framework expected **Spring 2026**, public consultation in 2026

Source: [Morgan Lewis: UK Rare Disease Regulation](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2025/11/major-overhaul-of-uk-rare-disease-regulation-mhra-proposes-enhanced-framework)

### CPRD: Clinical Practice Research Datalink

The MHRA operates the **Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD)**:
- **65 million patients** from GP practices across the UK (updated from 30 million)
- Anonymised real-world data
- Critical to MHRA's post-authorisation work
- The MHRA plans to evaluate **common data models and federated analytics** to generate scientifically robust RWE for regulatory decisions

### International Harmonisation

The MHRA is working towards international RWE harmonisation through:
- ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)
- IMDRF (International Medical Device Regulators Forum)
- Collaboration with NICE on data standards and terminology

**Pitch relevance:** The MHRA is aggressively expanding its use of real-world evidence but acknowledges challenges in data quality, methodology standards, and evidence integration. ArcaScience's ability to structure and cross-reference evidence from diverse published sources -- including observational studies, registries, and real-world data publications -- supports the MHRA's stated RWE ambitions. The rare disease adaptive licensing framework creates an entirely new category of evidence-intensive regulatory work that could benefit from automated evidence structuring.

## 11. MHRA vs. EMA vs. FDA: Comparative Analysis

### Novel Drug Approvals (2024)

In 2024, the three agencies collectively authorised **53 novel drugs**.
- **FDA CDER:** 50 novel approvals (ten-year rolling average: 46.5)
- **28 out of 53 (53%)** were first-in-class
- **52% of FDA 2024 approvals** targeted orphan diseases

### Approval Volume and Sequencing

A retrospective study of **154 innovative medicines** from NICE technology briefings (2020-2023) found:

| Metric | FDA | EMA | MHRA |
|--------|-----|-----|------|
| Total approved (of 154) | **84 (55%)** | **80 (52%)** | **71 (46%)** |
| First approvals achieved | **70** | **17** | **1** |
| Average days faster than MHRA | **360 days** | **86 days** | -- |
| Use of expedited pathways | **64%** | Not reported | **11%** |

The MHRA achieved only **1 first approval** out of 154 medicines studied, compared to the FDA's 70.

Source: [PMC: International Comparison of Medicines Approvals](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12587917/)

### Median Review Times (Working Days)

| Agency | Median Review Time | N | IQR |
|--------|-------------------|---|-----|
| **MHRA** | **170 days** | 7 | 162-236 |
| **FDA** | **173 days** | 82 | 129-218 |
| **EMA** | **296 days** | 77 | 247-341 |

Note: MHRA had the lowest median review time but based on only 7 eligible medicines (very small sample).

### Statutory vs. Actual Performance (MHRA)

| Route | Statutory Target | Actual Performance (Jan 2024) | Overage |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------------------|---------|
| National route | 210 days | **333 days** | +123 days |
| Reliance route | 67 days | **195 days** | +128 days |
| IRP Route A | 60 days | Operating | Per target |
| IRP Route B | 110 days | Operating | Per target |

### Expedited Pathway Comparison

| Pathway | Agency | Key Details |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Priority Review / Fast Track / Breakthrough / Accelerated | FDA | 57% of 2024 approvals used expedited pathways; 94% PDUFA goal compliance |
| PRIME / Accelerated Assessment | EMA | 210-day clock shortened to 150 days |
| IRP / ILAP / Rolling Reviews / Project Orbis | MHRA | IRP Route A: 60 days; ILAP relaunched 2025 |

### Key Divergence Example: Lecanemab (Alzheimer's)

- **FDA:** Approved 2023
- **MHRA:** Approved 2024 (first therapy clinically proven to slow progressive decline)
- **EMA:** Advisory committee issued a **negative opinion** -- same data, different regulatory outcome

Source: [British Journal of Pharmacology: Novel Drugs 2024 Review](https://bpspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bph.17458)

**Pitch relevance:** The data shows MHRA is significantly slower than the FDA at getting medicines to market (360 days slower on average) and achieved only 1 first approval out of 154 medicines. The 11% use of expedited pathways vs. FDA's 64% suggests under-utilisation of accelerated routes. This is directly relevant: ArcaScience can position itself as helping the MHRA close the gap with FDA by accelerating the evidence assembly phase of independent assessments, making it faster and more practical for the MHRA to conduct sovereign reviews rather than relying on other agencies' work.

## 12. Software as Medical Device (SaMD) Regulation

### Regulatory Roadmap

- January 2024: MHRA released initial roadmap for future medical devices regulatory framework
- December 2024: **Revised Roadmap (v2.0)** published with updated timelines
- CE marking recognition extended to **30 June 2030** (transitional period)
- After June 2030: UKCA mark required for all medical devices including SaMDs

### Classification Changes

The MHRA has confirmed adoption of the **IMDRF SaMD risk categorisation framework**:
- Most SaMDs will be classified as **Class IIa** (up from current UK classification)
- Aligns UK with comparable international regulatory contexts
- Increased regulatory requirements for conformity assessment

### Planned Guidance (2024-2025)

The MHRA intended to publish at least three SaMD guidance documents:
1. **Good Machine Learning Practice** for medical device development mapping
2. **AIaMD development and deployment** best practice
3. **Data-driven SaMD** research, development and governance
4. **Cybersecurity guidance** for SaMD -- planned for Q2 2025

### AI and Digital Regulations Service (AIDRS)

Launched **April 2024** -- a collaborative initiative by:
- NICE
- MHRA
- Health Research Authority
- Care Quality Commission

The AIDRS website serves as a comprehensive guide for developers and adopters navigating regulatory pathways for SaMD, including AI.

### Key Legislation Timeline

| Milestone | Expected Date |

| Pre-market draft SI on WTO website | Mid-2025 |
| Pre-market SI introduced to Parliament | Late 2025 |
| Pre-market SI in force | 2026 |
| International reliance for devices SI | 2026 |
| Device reliance routes active | 2027 |
| CE marking extension expires | 30 June 2030 |

Sources: [Pennington Law: SaMD Update](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/update-on-regulatory-changes-for-software-based-medical-devices/), [Emergo: MHRA Roadmap](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/mhra-publishes-revised-roadmap-future-regulatory-framework-medical-devices)

**Pitch relevance:** While ArcaScience is not a medical device, the MHRA's increasing sophistication in AI/SaMD regulation signals growing internal expertise with AI concepts and an organisational openness to AI tools. The AIDRS platform shows the MHRA wants to help developers navigate regulatory pathways -- this collaborative stance extends to regulatory science tools.

## 13. Key Data Points for the Pitch

### The Capacity Crisis (Numbers to Quote)

- **~100 assessors** handling **80 concurrent safety investigations** across **600+ drugs**
- **19.7% vacancy rate** and **16.5% annual voluntary turnover**
- **~40% of personnel** lost in 2020 reorganisation
- **80% of ABPI respondents** felt MHRA capacity was undermining trust
- **1,167 product licences** were over the 210-day limit as of January 2024
- National route approvals taking **333 days** vs. 210-day target (123 days over)
- **Only 1 first approval** out of 154 innovative medicines (vs. FDA's 70)

### The Under-Reporting Problem (Numbers to Quote)

- **94% median under-reporting rate** for adverse drug reactions
- Only **10% of serious reactions** and **2-4% of non-serious reactions** reported
- Yellow Card system described as "too complex and too diffuse to allow early signal detection"
- **1.3 million+ cumulative reports** since 1963, but majority of real ADRs never reported

### The Technology Gap (Numbers to Quote)

- RegulatoryConnect digital platform **cancelled after <2 years** (launched April 2024, shut November 2025)
- Legacy PV systems included **Lotus Notes** and **Excel spreadsheets**
- SafetyConnect/HALO modernisation ongoing but focused on case management, not evidence structuring
- New CEO's quote: "our ability to meet that demand with humans is finite"

### The Strategic Alignment (Numbers to Quote)

- MHRA Data Strategy 2024-2027 explicitly calls for leveraging **AI and advanced analytics throughout the product lifecycle**
- Pilot **RWE Dialogue Programme** launched January 2025
- **AI Airlock** (world-first regulatory sandbox) demonstrates willingness to experiment with AI
- National Commission on AI in Healthcare launched September 2025
- **GBP 7 million+** in regulatory science research grants (2024-25)
- New MHRA 2030 strategy being developed, launching early 2026 -- prime window for partnerships

### The Positioning Opportunity

The MHRA is:
- Under-resourced but ambitious
- Technologically behind but investing in modernisation
- Philosophically open to AI but demanding transparency and auditability
- Criticised by Parliament and industry but eager to improve
- Building a new 2030 strategy that will define technology partnerships

ArcaScience's evidence structuring platform addresses the specific bottleneck identified in the meeting: the manual evidence assembly phase (Step 3) of post-authorisation safety assessment, where ~100 assessors manually gather, read, and cross-reference published literature across 80+ concurrent safety investigations.
