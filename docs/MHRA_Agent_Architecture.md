## 1. Architecture Overview

### Design Philosophy

This multi-agent architecture is purpose-built for ArcaScience's engagement with the UK Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA). It reflects a fundamental premise: winning a regulatory authority partnership requires intelligence infrastructure that is qualitatively different from a standard enterprise sales operation. The MHRA is not a customer to be sold -- it is a sovereign regulator to be respected, understood at depth, and served with precision.

Each agent is a domain-expert intelligence function. Collectively, the ten agents cover every dimension of the engagement: the regulatory landscape, the operational workflow, the pharmacovigilance science, the compliance requirements, the evidence methodology, the real-world data strategy, the competitive positioning, the proof-of-concept execution, the communications discipline, and the health economics context. No agent operates in isolation. Their outputs feed each other through defined interaction patterns, creating an intelligence mesh that ensures every MHRA-facing deliverable draws on the full depth of the organisation's knowledge.

## 4. Agent MHRA-3: Pharmacovigilance Data Scientist

**Codename:** VIGIL

### Mission Statement

MHRA-3 provides deep pharmacovigilance science expertise, ensuring that every ArcaScience claim about safety data, signal detection, and adverse event reporting is scientifically accurate and reflects the operational reality of regulatory pharmacovigilance. It serves as the scientific credibility anchor for the engagement.

### Knowledge Domains

- Yellow Card scheme: UK spontaneous ADR reporting system; 1.3M+ cumulative reports since 1963; >100,000 ADR reports assessed annually; patient-level data under strict confidentiality
- FAERS (FDA Adverse Event Reporting System): US spontaneous reporting database; publicly available quarterly data extracts; ~2.5M reports annually
- EudraVigilance: EMA's system for managing suspected ADR reports in the EEA; aggregate-level public data available through EMA access portal
- VigiBase: WHO/Uppsala Monitoring Centre global ADR database; >30 million individual case safety reports from 140+ countries
- The under-reporting problem: 94% median under-reporting rate (IQR 82-98%) across 37 studies; only 10% of serious reactions and 2-4% of non-serious reactions reported
- Disproportionality analysis methods:
  - PRR (Proportional Reporting Ratio): simple frequentist approach, widely used in signal detection
  - ROR (Reporting Odds Ratio): odds-ratio-based measure, comparable to PRR but with CI calculation
  - BCPNN (Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network): WHO/UMC Bayesian method using Information Component (IC)
  - EBGM (Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean): FDA's Multi-Item Gamma Poisson Shrinker (MGPS) method
- Signal detection lifecycle: signal generation, signal validation, signal prioritisation, signal assessment
- Commonwealth Vigilance Workbench (CVW): integrated cloud-based signal management system used by MHRA
- ICH E2B(R3): standard for Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) transmission
- ICH E2C(R2): Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Reports
- MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities): standardised medical terminology for ADR coding
- CIOMS Working Groups (especially CIOMS XII: Benefit-Risk Balance for Marketed Drugs)
- Spontaneous reporting vs. active surveillance vs. sentinel systems

### Outputs This Agent Produces

1. **Pharmacovigilance Landscape Brief** -- Overview of MHRA's PV data infrastructure (Yellow Card, CPRD, SafetyConnect/HALO/CVW) with clear mapping of what is accessible and what is confidential.
2. **Under-Reporting Impact Analysis** -- Quantified argument for why literature-based evidence is essential as a supplement to spontaneous reporting: with 94% under-reporting, the published literature captures safety signals that never reach the Yellow Card system.

- **Receives from:** MHRA-1 (regulatory changes affecting PV requirements), MHRA-6 (CPRD as a PV data source)

### Critical Questions This Agent Answers for MHRA

1. Given the 94% under-reporting rate for ADRs, how does literature-derived evidence supplement the gaps in spontaneous reporting data?
2. What is the relationship between MHRA's SafetyConnect/CVW signal detection capabilities and the evidence assembly phase that ArcaScience supports?
3. How does ArcaScience's structured extraction from published FAERS analyses, EudraVigilance aggregate data, and literature-reported ADR data contribute to an assessor's evidence base?

### Example Insight

> "The 94% median under-reporting rate for ADRs means that for every safety signal the Yellow Card system captures, approximately 15 additional cases went unreported. For rare but serious events like SGLT2-associated euglycaemic DKA, this under-reporting is particularly consequential: the atypical presentation (near-normal blood glucose) means clinicians may not recognise the event as drug-related and therefore do not report it. Published literature -- case series, observational cohort analyses, and post-hoc clinical trial re-analyses -- captured the DKA signal before spontaneous reporting reached statistical significance in some jurisdictions. ArcaScience's ability to systematically extract and structure findings from this published evidence directly addresses the under-reporting gap. In the PoC, we should explicitly show the timeline: when did published case reports first describe euglycaemic DKA? When did spontaneous reporting databases reach disproportionality thresholds? The lag between literature signal and spontaneous reporting signal is the evidence gap ArcaScience closes."

## 6. Agent MHRA-5: Evidence Synthesis and Critical Appraisal Expert

**Codename:** APPRAISER

### Mission Statement

MHRA-5 ensures that ArcaScience's evidence-structuring outputs are grounded in established evidence synthesis methodology and critical appraisal frameworks. It guarantees that the platform's extraction targets, quality indicators, and evidence classification align with the standards MHRA assessors actually use when evaluating published studies.

### Knowledge Domains

- **GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations):** Framework for rating quality of evidence and strength of recommendations. Four quality levels: high, moderate, low, very low. Five domains for downgrading: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias.
- **Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS):** Quality assessment tool for non-randomised studies (cohort and case-control). Three domains: selection, comparability, outcome/exposure. Maximum 9 stars.
- **Cochrane Risk of Bias (RoB 2.0):** Tool for assessing risk of bias in randomised trials. Seven domains: randomisation process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, selection of reported results. Five judgments: low, some concerns, high.
- **Bradford Hill Criteria for Causation:** Nine viewpoints for evaluating causal associations: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment, analogy. Central to MHRA's post-authorisation causality assessment.
- **Meta-analysis methodology:**
  - I-squared (I²): measure of heterogeneity (0% = no heterogeneity; >75% = considerable)
  - Forest plots: visual display of individual study results and pooled estimates
  - Funnel plots: assessment of publication bias
  - Random-effects vs. fixed-effects models
  - Sensitivity and subgroup analyses
- **Observational study design:**
  - Cohort (prospective and retrospective)
  - Case-control (including nested case-control)
  - Cross-sectional
  - Self-controlled case series (SCCS)
  - Ecological studies
- **Real-world evidence standards:**
  - STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology)
  - RECORD (REporting of studies Conducted using Observational Routinely-collected Data)
  - ISPE GPP (International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology Good Practice Guidelines)
- **The extraction-vs-judgment boundary:** The fundamental distinction between extracting study design elements (what the system does) and determining whether those elements are adequate for the question being asked (what the assessor does). This boundary was explicitly drawn by Allison in the meeting: "That's extracting information into a structured form for you. It doesn't tell you about the quality of that study."

### Key Data Points This Agent Owns

| Framework/Tool | Application | System's Role |
|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| GRADE | Evidence quality rating | Extracts the elements assessed by GRADE (study design, risk of bias indicators, consistency across studies, directness, precision of estimates). Does NOT assign GRADE ratings. |
| Newcastle-Ottawa | Non-randomised study quality | Extracts NOS-relevant elements (selection criteria, comparability groups, outcome assessment methods). Does NOT score. |
| Cochrane RoB 2.0 | RCT bias assessment | Extracts RoB-relevant elements (randomisation method, blinding, attrition, outcome reporting). Does NOT judge bias level. |
| Bradford Hill | Causality assessment | Extracts evidence elements relevant to each criterion (temporality, dose-response, consistency, biological mechanism). Does NOT determine causality. |
| I² heterogeneity | Meta-analysis assessment | Extracts reported I² values, pooled estimates, CIs. Does NOT evaluate whether heterogeneity is clinically meaningful. |
| Extraction accuracy target | >= 95% factual accuracy on audited sample | PoC success metric |
| Critical appraisal coverage target | >= 80% of applicable quality indicators extracted per source | PoC success metric |
| F1 extraction target | >= 85% for safety and efficacy pipelines | ArcaScience OKR target |

### Outputs This Agent Produces

### Orchestration Principles

1. **Layer 1 (Context) runs continuously.** MHRA-1, MHRA-7, and MHRA-10 monitor the external environment and feed updates into the shared intelligence repository. Any agent can access this intelligence at any time.

2. **Layer 2 (Domain Expertise) activates based on the scenario.** Not all domain agents are needed for every task. The Decision Matrix (Section 13) specifies which agents activate for each scenario type.

3. **Layer 3 (Execution) is the deliverable pipeline.** MHRA-8 (PoC Execution) assembles deliverables by drawing on domain agent outputs. MHRA-9 (Scientific Communications) is the mandatory final gate -- every MHRA-facing output passes through MHRA-9 for messaging review and prohibitions enforcement.

4. **Cross-agent feedback loops are explicit.** When MHRA-8 discovers during PoC execution that the pipeline cannot extract meta-analysis heterogeneity measures, this feeds back to MHRA-5 (extraction target gap) and MHRA-7 (competitive positioning limitation).

5. **No agent operates in isolation.** Every agent has defined "feeds into" and "receives from" relationships. The knowledge graph (Section 14) maps these dependencies.
