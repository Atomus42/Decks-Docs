## Slide 1: The Microglial Paradox — Why TREM2 Agonism Is Not Simply "More Activation = Better"

**Headline:** TREM2-Mediated Microglial Activation Is Stage-Dependent, Expression-Level-Dependent, and Mechanism-Dependent — the INVOKE-2 Failure Demonstrates That Target Engagement Without Biological Precision Produces Risk Without Benefit

**Content:**

**The two-stage DAM transition and its implications for TREM2 agonism:**

Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM) represent a transcriptionally distinct microglial state identified in amyloid-bearing mouse models and confirmed in human AD brain tissue. The transition from homeostatic microglia to DAM is a two-stage process (Keren-Shaul et al., *Cell*, 2017):

- **Stage 1 (TREM2-independent):** Downregulation of homeostatic genes (P2RY12, CX3CR1, TMEM119); upregulation of TYROBP, APOE, and B2M. This transition occurs in response to amyloid proximity and does not require functional TREM2.
- **Stage 2 (TREM2-dependent):** Upregulation of phagocytic and lipid-metabolic genes (LPL, CST7, CD9, SPP1); acquisition of the full neuroprotective phenotype — amyloid phagocytosis, plaque compaction, neuronal barrier formation. This transition is blocked in TREM2-deficient microglia.

The therapeutic premise of TREM2 agonism rests on promoting the Stage 1-to-Stage 2 transition. But DAM are not simply "good microglia." Their function is context-dependent across at least three dimensions:

**1. Disease stage determines whether TREM2 agonism is protective or harmful:**

- **In early amyloid-dominant pathology:** TREM2-mediated DAM activation promotes plaque compaction and barrier formation around dense-core plaques, limiting diffuse amyloid toxicity and protecting nearby neurites. TREM2 deficiency at this stage *reduces* amyloid deposition — suggesting TREM2-competent microglia may paradoxically facilitate early plaque seeding while simultaneously limiting plaque-associated damage (Ulland & Colonna, *Nat Rev Neurol*, 2018).
- **In established tau pathology:** Microglial activation exacerbates tau seeding and spreading. TREM2-dependent microglial responses amplify neuroinflammatory cascades (NLRP3 inflammasome, IL-1beta, TNF-alpha) that drive tau phosphorylation and propagation. TREM2 deficiency at this stage *increases* amyloid but may *reduce* tau-mediated neurodegeneration.
- **Implication for VG-3927:** The benefit-risk ratio of TREM2 agonism inverts as a function of disease stage. A drug that is neuroprotective in amyloid-positive, tau-negative patients may be neurotoxic in patients with mixed amyloid-tau pathology.

**2. TREM2 expression level determines the microglial response to agonism:**

TREM2 expression level is not binary. It varies across patients, across brain regions, and across disease stages. A 2026 study (*Nature Communications*, 2026) demonstrated that TREM2 expression level is the critical determinant of whether agonism produces a neuroprotective or neuroinflammatory microglial phenotype. "Too much" TREM2 activation — exceeding the physiological range — drives microglia toward a pro-inflammatory, tissue-damaging state rather than the intended phagocytic, neuroprotective DAM phenotype. This creates a narrow therapeutic window that is patient-specific and cannot be predicted from population-level dosing data alone.

**3. Mechanism of agonism determines which microglial state is induced:**

Not all TREM2 agonists activate microglia in the same way. The critical distinction, revealed by comparing antibody-based and small-molecule approaches, is whether the agonist stabilizes surface TREM2 or promotes its internalization:

| Agent | Binding Site | Effect on Surface TREM2 | Microglial Activation Profile | Biomarker Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AL002** (Alector/AbbVie) | Stalk domain | Promotes receptor internalization and shedding | Pro-inflammatory: increased SPP1, sCSF1R, CCL3; consistent with inflammatory microglial polarization | SPP1 up, sCSF1R up, CCL3 up |
| **VHB937** (Novartis) | IgSF domain | Stabilizes surface TREM2 | Anti-inflammatory: decreased SPP1, sCSF1R, CCL3; consistent with homeostatic-to-DAM transition without inflammatory overshoot | SPP1 down, sCSF1R down, CCL3 down |
| **VG-3927** (Sanofi/Vigil) | Small-molecule molecular glue | Does not bind sTREM2; does not promote internalization; no Fc domain | Potentiates endogenous TREM2 signaling in response to natural damage ligands; predicted activation profile is ligand-dependent and spatially restricted | ~50% CSF sTREM2 reduction (Phase 1); downstream profile to be characterized in Phase 2 |

**The INVOKE-2 lesson:**

AL002 confirmed robust target engagement in INVOKE-2 (381 patients, 76 weeks): microglial activation markers were elevated (SPP1, osteopontin), confirming that TREM2 was activated. Yet no clinical benefit was observed on CDR-SB, ADAS-Cog14, or any secondary endpoint. Simultaneously, ARIA-E occurred in 29% and ARIA-H in 27% of treated patients — *without measurable amyloid clearance* (*Front Aging Neurosci*, 2025 analysis; *Nature Medicine*, 2026).

The failure was not a failure of TREM2 biology. It was a failure of *the specific type of microglial activation that AL002 produced*. Stalk-domain binding promoted receptor internalization, reducing surface TREM2 density and driving pro-inflammatory biomarker signatures — the opposite of the intended neuroprotective DAM phenotype. The microglia were activated, but they were activated *in the wrong direction*.

**The central thesis:**

The benefit-risk of TREM2 agonism depends on **which microglial state** is induced, in **which patients** (genotype, expression level), at **which disease stage** (amyloid-dominant vs. tau-dominant). Simplistic models — "activate TREM2, promote phagocytosis, clear amyloid, slow decline" — have been falsified by INVOKE-2. VG-3927's differentiated mechanism (molecular glue, ligand-dependent activation, no Fc domain, no internalization) provides a pharmacological basis for a better microglial activation profile, but this hypothesis must be rigorously modeled across the multi-dimensional patient landscape before Phase 2 can be designed with confidence.

**The evidence architecture reality:**

The above matrix reveals a fundamental asymmetry: TREM2 has exceptionally strong genetic validation — among the top three genetic risk factors for AD after APOE and APP/PSEN — but the clinical evidence base is effectively a single failed trial (INVOKE-2) for a pharmacologically distinct agent. Between genetic validation and clinical proof lies a landscape of critical unknowns in biomarker qualification, patient selection, DDI characterization, and long-term safety.

VG-3927's molecular differentiation from AL002 (oral, small molecule, no Fc, no sTREM2 binding, no receptor internalization, 0.91 CSF/plasma ratio) is genuine and scientifically important. But differentiation *from a failed drug* does not constitute evidence *for* efficacy. It constitutes a hypothesis that must be tested in the context of systematically characterized evidence gaps.

**How ArcaScience's BR-PREDICT platform fills these gaps:**

The evidence gap matrix maps directly to ArcaScience's six work packages:

- **Preclinical efficacy gap** → WP2 (preclinical-to-clinical transposition): quantitative reliability scoring for 5xFAD translation of TREM2 agonist signals
- **Clinical safety gap** → WP5 (knowledge graph): cross-referencing TREM2 signaling pathways with vascular biology to generate ARIA mechanism hypotheses independent of Fc
- **Biomarker qualification gap** → WP5 + WP4: systematic evidence synthesis across candidate PD/response biomarkers with validation status assessment
- **Patient selection gap** → WP3 (pharmacogenomics): APOE4 x TREM2 genotype interaction modeling; WP6 (world model): counterfactual stage-stratified benefit-risk simulation
- **DDI gap** → WP1 (QSAR/QSTR): CYP metabolism prediction; WP4 (RWE): polypharmacy profiling in elderly AD populations
- **Long-term effects gap** → WP5: TREM2-TAM biology evidence synthesis; WP4: cancer registry cross-referencing for chronic immunomodulatory agents in elderly populations

## Slide 3: From Neuroinflammation Biology to Benefit-Risk Prediction — ArcaScience's Multi-Scale Evidence Integration

**Headline:** ArcaScience's Six Work Packages Translate the Biological Complexity of TREM2/Microglial Modulation into Structured, Quantitative Benefit-Risk Predictions — a Specific Pathway Through VG-3927's Evidence Landscape

**Content:**

**The analytical pathway for VG-3927:**

The following traces a specific evidence integration pathway through ArcaScience's BR-PREDICT platform, demonstrating how each work package contributes a distinct analytical layer to VG-3927's benefit-risk prediction:

---

**WP1 (Chemical Structure QSAR/QSTR): Structural analog safety profiling**

VG-3927 is a first-in-class oral molecular glue TREM2 agonist. Its chemical scaffold has no approved precedent compound for safety benchmarking. WP1 addresses this by:

- Identifying structural analogs of VG-3927 across the chemical space using Morgan/ECFP molecular fingerprints and graph neural network (GNN) modeling
- Screening identified analogs for known safety signals — hepatotoxicity, hERG liability, reactive metabolite formation, CYP inhibition/induction profiles
- Generating a structural risk card: predicted liabilities from VG-3927's scaffold benchmarked against the closest known chemotypes
- Predicting CYP metabolism sites (CYP3A4, CYP2D6, CYP2C19) to inform the DDI risk assessment in WP4 — critical for a once-daily oral drug in elderly polypharmacy populations
- **Output:** VG-3927 structural risk profile with predicted off-target liabilities, CYP metabolism predictions, and comparative safety profiles from the closest structural analogs

---

**WP2 (Preclinical-to-Clinical Transposition): Bridging 5xFAD plaque reduction to human predictions**

VG-3927 achieved ~40% reduction in plaques and insoluble Abeta-42 over 6 weeks in 5xFAD mice (*Alzheimers Dement* / PMC, 2024). The same model predicted AL002 would reduce amyloid in humans — it did not. WP2 quantifies the translational reliability:

- Computing a reliability score for the 5xFAD model specifically in the context of microglial-modulating mechanisms — how often has this model predicted outcomes correctly for this mechanism class?
- Applying transposability scoring to each preclinical finding: plaque reduction, DAM gene upregulation, insoluble Abeta-42 reduction, behavioral endpoints
- Assessing cross-species pharmacological differences: mouse TREM2 vs. human TREM2 signaling kinetics, microglial transcriptomic divergence (Srinivasan et al., *Nat Neurosci*, 2020), and the absence of APOE4 in standard murine models
- Calibrating preclinical predictions against VG-3927 Phase 1 human PK data (n=115; CSF/plasma ratio 0.91) to refine the preclinical-to-clinical scaling
- **Output:** Translation confidence matrix for each preclinical endpoint, with reliability-weighted predictions and explicit uncertainty quantification

---

**WP3 (Pharmacogenomic Biomarker Prediction): APOE4 x TREM2 R47H compound interaction modeling**

The APOE4 x TREM2 genotype interaction is a critical but uncharacterized variable in VG-3927's benefit-risk profile:

- **APOE4 and TREM2 directly interact:** TREM2 binds APOE as a ligand; APOE4 has reduced binding affinity compared to APOE3 (Yeh et al., *Neuron*, 2016). APOE4 carriers may have reduced endogenous TREM2 signaling, theoretically making them stronger responders to agonism — but also increasing CAA burden and ARIA susceptibility
- **TREM2 R47H is a partial loss-of-function variant** (OR 2.92; Jonsson et al., *NEJM*, 2013): R47H reduces lipid ligand binding. Patients carrying R47H may represent the most biologically rational enrichment target — they have genetically reduced TREM2 function that an agonist could restore. But they also carry the highest genetic risk for disease severity
- **Compound genotype risk:** APOE4 homozygous + TREM2 R47H heterozygous patients represent a high-genetic-risk subgroup requiring dedicated pharmacogenomic modeling

WP3 integrates data from PharmGKB, ClinVar, gnomAD, and the published TREM2 genetic epidemiology to model:
- Predicted efficacy heterogeneity: differential microglial activation capacity by APOE x TREM2 genotype combination
- Predicted safety heterogeneity: ARIA susceptibility, infection risk, and peripheral myeloid activation by genotype
- Enrichment threshold recommendations: which genotype combinations have favorable predicted benefit-risk ratios?
- **Output:** Genotype-stratified benefit-risk matrix with evidence-graded predictions for each APOE x TREM2 combination

---

**WP4 (Real-World Evidence Modeling): Elderly AD population characterization and polypharmacy landscape**

Phase 2 will enroll elderly patients (65-85 years) with early-to-mild AD — a population defined by comorbidity burden, polypharmacy, and immunosenescence:

- Multi-database integration (CPRD, Optum, MarketScan, FAERS, EudraVigilance, VigiBase, JADER) to characterize:
  - Baseline comorbidity profiles (cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, metabolic, infectious disease)
  - Co-medication landscapes (cholinesterase inhibitors in >60% of patients; increasingly, anti-amyloid antibodies)
  - Background event rates for safety endpoints (ARIA-relevant cerebrovascular events, infections, malignancies in elderly AD cohorts)
- Drug-drug interaction modeling: VG-3927's predicted CYP metabolism profile (from WP1) against the top 50 co-prescribed medications in elderly AD populations
- Infection susceptibility profiling: baseline infection rates in the target population, calibrated for immunosenescence, providing the denominator against which TREM2-mediated immune effects must be assessed
- **Output:** Population characterization report with comorbidity-adjusted baseline event rates, DDI risk rankings, and infection susceptibility estimates

---

**WP5 (Knowledge Graph Ontology): TREM2 pathway biology connected to cross-mechanism safety signals**

The BR-PREDICT knowledge graph (100+ billion data-point relations, Neo4j + RDF architecture, SPARQL-queryable) connects TREM2 biology to the broader landscape of microglial-modulating agents:

- Cross-referencing safety signals from all known agents that modulate microglial function:
  - **TREM2 agonists:** AL002, VHB937 (Novartis), MNA-001 (Muna), DNL919 (Denali/Takeda — discontinued)
  - **CSF1R inhibitors:** Pexidartinib (approved in TGCT; FDA black box for hepatotoxicity), PLX-3397 (elective microglial depletion — demonstrates risks of extreme microglial modulation)
  - **Complement pathway modulators:** Agents modulating C1q/C3 at the microglial-vascular interface
- Mapping the TREM2/DAP12/SYK signaling cascade to downstream biological processes: phagocytosis, lipid metabolism, complement activation, cytokine production, vascular integrity — identifying which downstream nodes carry safety signals across the class
- Identifying non-obvious connections: TREM2 pathway nodes that overlap with pathways implicated in ARIA from anti-amyloid antibodies, hepatotoxicity from CSF1R inhibitors, or immunosuppressive effects from other myeloid-targeting agents
- **Output:** Mechanism-class safety landscape map with evidence-weighted risk signals at each pathway node

**Anchoring precedent — the Sanofi collaboration:**

This cross-mechanism, multi-source approach has direct precedent within the ArcaScience-Sanofi partnership. In a prior collaboration, ArcaScience's platform identified thromboembolic risks from **9x more evidence sources** than Sanofi's internal review, leading to the redirection of development investment pre-Phase III. The TREM2 evidence landscape — scattered across neuroscience, immunology, vascular biology, and immuno-oncology journals, plus multiple pharmacovigilance databases — presents an analogous challenge: critical safety-relevant evidence exists but is dispersed across domains that no single expert or manual review can efficiently synthesize.

---

**WP6 (World Model Integration): Scenario-specific benefit-risk profiles**

The WP6 structural causal model (SCM) consumes outputs from all upstream work packages and integrates them into patient-subgroup-specific, scenario-specific benefit-risk simulations:

- **Counterfactual scenario modeling:** "What is VG-3927's projected benefit-risk profile in APOE4 heterozygotes with early amyloid-positive, tau-negative AD?" vs. "What is the projected profile in APOE4 non-carriers with mixed amyloid-tau pathology?"
- **Combination therapy simulation:** Modeling VG-3927 + lecanemab combination B-R profiles, including additive ARIA risk estimation — scientifically compelling combination (TREM2-mediated phagocytosis of antibody-opsonized amyloid) but with uncharacterized compounding safety risk
- **Dose-response B-R optimization:** Mapping the therapeutic window across patient subpopulations, integrating WP1 structural predictions, WP2 preclinical translation scores, and WP3 pharmacogenomic modifiers
- Each simulation produces uncertainty-quantified predictions (deep ensembles) with Mental Map visualization for clinical team interpretation
- **Output:** Scenario-specific B-R profiles with key drivers, sensitivity analyses, and explicit identification of assumptions — designed as decision-support inputs for Sanofi's development governance, not as automated decisions
