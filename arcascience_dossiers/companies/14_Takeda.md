# Takeda

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025 YTD through Q3, ending March 2026):** Revenue declined ~3% at CER year-to-date as VYVANSE generic erosion tapers off; FY2025 full-year revenue guidance revised upward to JPY 4,530B (~$30B); core operating profit guidance raised to JPY 1,150B. Growth & launch products now represent 52% of total revenue, growing 6.7% at CER, partially offsetting the Vyvanse decline.
- **Headquarters:** Tokyo, Japan (with significant operational presence in Cambridge/Boston, MA)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Gastrointestinal & inflammation (Entyvio franchise, $6.1B+ in sales), rare genetics & hematology, neuroscience, oncology (including new Innovent partnership), plasma-derived therapies, vaccines
- **Pipeline Size:** 6 late-stage (Phase III) programs with combined peak revenue potential of $10B-$20B; October 2025 Innovent Biologics partnership adds two late-stage oncology assets (IBI363 PD-1/IL-2 bispecific, IBI343 CLDN18.2 ADC) targeting a combined $40B+ addressable market; additional early/mid-stage programs across four core therapeutic areas
- **Strategic Priorities:** CEO succession (Julie Kim -- first woman and first American CEO in Takeda's history -- succeeding Christophe Weber in June 2026, announced January 2025); multi-year enterprise-wide efficiency restructuring ($900M charge taken in FY2024, driving 100-250 bps/year core operating margin improvement); late-stage pipeline execution with three NDA filings underway (oveporexton, rusfertide filed; zasocitinib filing on track) and launches expected 2026-2027; Entyvio lifecycle management (SC formulation approved in UC and Crohn's, peak sales target $7.5B-$9.0B); Innovent oncology partnership ($1.2B upfront, up to $11.4B total); oncology focus on GI, thoracic, and hematology tumors with cell therapy deprioritized

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Takeda's global R&D organization focuses on three core therapeutic areas (gastrointestinal & inflammation, neuroscience, oncology) and four core modalities. The $900M enterprise restructuring initiated in FY2024 streamlined R&D, concentrating resources on the six late-stage Phase III programs while driving operational efficiencies that have reduced R&D and SG&A expenses year-over-year. As of Q3 FY2025 (December 2025), organizational changes have affected 600+ positions with JPY 27.4B in restructuring costs recognized. The clinical development structure maintains both a Cambridge/Boston-based global R&D hub and a Japan-based R&D organization (JPBU), though the Japan operation underwent significant restructuring in August 2024 as part of the efficiency program. Oncology R&D was refocused on thoracic, GI, and hematology tumors, with cell therapy deprioritized (TAK-007 CAR-NK pivoted from blood cancer to autoimmune conditions). The October 2025 Innovent Biologics partnership ($1.2B upfront, up to $11.4B total value) significantly bolsters the oncology pipeline with IBI363 (PD-1/IL-2 bispecific) and IBI343 (CLDN18.2 ADC), two late-stage assets for solid tumors with a combined $40B+ addressable market. Takeda will co-develop IBI363 globally (60/40 cost split) and holds exclusive rights for IBI343 outside Greater China. Julie Kim, CEO-elect and set to succeed Christophe Weber in June 2026, is currently serving as interim head of the Global Portfolio Division, signaling continuity in R&D governance through the leadership transition.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs is organized as a global function supporting the core therapeutic areas, with particular intensity around the six late-stage programs. As of early 2026, NDAs have been filed with the FDA for oveporexton (narcolepsy type 1) and rusfertide (polycythemia vera), with formal acceptance pending. Zasocitinib (psoriasis) NDA filing is on track following positive Phase 3 readouts in late 2025, including a fully enrolled head-to-head study versus deucravacitinib expected to read out in 2026. All three programs are expected to launch within 18 months (late 2026 through early 2027). The regulatory function coordinates closely with the Japan Pharma Business Unit for PMDA submissions and with the U.S. and EU teams for FDA and EMA filings. Entyvio SC has received marketing authorization in the U.S., EU, and 50+ countries, with geographic launches broadening through 2025-2026 to defend the franchise's $7.5B-$9.0B peak sales target. The Innovent partnership adds regulatory complexity, with IBI363 and IBI343 requiring coordinated global development and filing strategies. Takeda's acquisition-driven portfolio (Shire legacy products) continues to require regulatory management of both innovative and established products globally.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Pharmacovigilance operates as a global function supporting the broad marketed portfolio (including Entyvio at $6.1B+ in sales across IV and SC formulations) and the expanding clinical pipeline. The PV function must cover diverse modalities including small molecules, biologics, plasma-derived therapies, vaccines, and cell therapies -- and now, with the Innovent partnership, bispecific antibody fusion proteins (IBI363) and antibody-drug conjugates (IBI343), each with distinct safety monitoring requirements. Given the Shire legacy, Takeda's PV organization has deep expertise in rare disease safety monitoring, where patient populations are small and signal detection requires specialized approaches. The enterprise restructuring has reduced PV staffing as part of the 600+ position reductions, while the impending launch of three new products (oveporexton, rusfertide, zasocitinib) in 2026-2027 will significantly increase the PV workload. Oveporexton's novel orexin agonist mechanism and rusfertide's hepcidin mimetic profile will both require tailored post-marketing surveillance frameworks.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is managed through R&D portfolio committees that evaluate the six late-stage programs and the newly acquired Innovent oncology assets. With NDAs filed for oveporexton and rusfertide and zasocitinib filing imminent, B/R governance is now shifting from development-phase assessments to launch-readiness and post-marketing frameworks. The CEO succession adds a governance dimension: Julie Kim, as CEO-elect and interim head of the Global Portfolio Division, is already shaping B/R priorities for the post-Weber era. B/R assessments span diverse therapeutic contexts (narcolepsy, psoriasis, polycythemia vera, IgA nephropathy, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, inflammatory conditions) and now extend to oncology (solid tumors via Innovent assets), requiring versatile evaluation frameworks across an even broader range of modalities. The Innovent partnership introduces co-development B/R governance complexity, with Takeda holding 60% of IBI363 costs/profits and needing to align B/R decision-making with a Chinese biotech partner.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Head of Global Regulatory Affairs or CMO -- driven by the unprecedented challenge of three simultaneous NDA filings (oveporexton, rusfertide, zasocitinib) plus launch preparation, compounded by the Innovent oncology partnership adding two new late-stage programs requiring B/R governance frameworks
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO / Head of Biostatistics -- Takeda's data-driven culture requires rigorous validation of probabilistic B/R models; the oveporexton Phase III results (85% of patients reaching normative ESS range) and rusfertide (77% clinical response) provide rich datasets for dynamic BRA demonstration
- **Budget Owner:** CMO / Head of R&D (global R&D budget in mid-teens percent of revenue); Julie Kim as interim head of Global Portfolio Division may influence budget allocation during the transition period
- **Procurement Cycle:** 9-18 months; Japanese corporate governance culture emphasizes consensus-building; dual headquarters (Tokyo/Cambridge) adds complexity; however, the restructuring mandate for efficiency gains and the compressed launch timeline (three products in 18 months) create urgency for B/R decision-support tools
- **Internal Friction Points:** (1) The $900M restructuring, while winding down (lower restructuring expenses in FY2025 vs FY2024), has reduced headcount by 600+ positions and created organizational distraction; (2) CEO succession (Weber to Kim, June 2026) creates a narrow window -- decisions may be deferred until Kim fully takes charge, or accelerated to demonstrate launch readiness; (3) The Innovent co-development model (60/40 cost split) introduces a partner governance layer that complicates new vendor decisions; (4) Diverse portfolio (biologics, plasma, vaccines, small molecules, plus new bispecifics and ADCs) requires platform customization across modalities

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target the convergence of three transformative events at Takeda: (1) the imminent triple-launch of oveporexton, rusfertide, and zasocitinib within 18 months, (2) the Innovent oncology partnership adding two new late-stage assets requiring B/R governance from scratch, and (3) the CEO succession creating a strategic reset moment. The value proposition: Takeda is simultaneously filing NDAs, preparing launches, integrating a major oncology partnership, and transitioning leadership -- this is a B/R governance challenge of unprecedented scale and complexity for the company. ArcaScience's dynamic BRA platform can serve as the unified decision-support layer across all eight late-stage programs (six original plus two Innovent assets). The rare disease angle remains compelling: rusfertide achieved a 77% clinical response rate in Phase III with Breakthrough Therapy Designation, and oveporexton's Phase III data showed 85% of patients reaching normative range on the ESS -- both programs now have NDAs filed and need post-filing B/R support for advisory committee preparation and label negotiations. The Innovent partnership opens a new entry vector: IBI363 (PD-1/IL-2 bispecific) has treated 1,200+ patients and requires sophisticated B/R modeling across multiple solid tumor types, while the 60/40 co-development structure creates a natural need for a shared, transparent B/R platform between Takeda and Innovent. The CEO succession creates a window: Julie Kim, as CEO-elect and interim head of Global Portfolio Division, is building her strategic agenda -- positioning ArcaScience as part of the "Kim era" launch infrastructure could secure long-term commitment. Propose a dual pilot: (1) oveporexton or rusfertide for launch-readiness B/R governance, and (2) IBI363 for partnership-integrated B/R modeling.
