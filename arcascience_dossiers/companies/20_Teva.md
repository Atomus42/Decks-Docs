# Teva Pharmaceutical

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** ~$17.4B (estimated; Q3 2025 quarterly revenues of $4.5B; 11 consecutive quarters of growth); FY2024 revenue was $16.5B
- **Headquarters:** Tel Aviv, Israel (with major operations in Parsippany, NJ, USA)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Generics (world's largest manufacturer, 1,800+ molecules), CNS/neuroscience (AUSTEDO, AJOVY, UZEDY, COPAXONE), immunology (duvakitug anti-TL1A partnership with Sanofi), immuno-oncology (TEV-56278 anti-PD1/IL-2 ATTENUKINE with Fosun Pharma), biosimilars (Simlandi, Selarsdi, Epysqli)
- **Pipeline Size:** ~40-50 pipeline products at various stages; ~150 bioequivalence studies annually; innovative portfolio reached $1B in quarterly revenues for the first time in Q4 2025; key innovative brands generated ~$2.3B+ in 2024 growing ~40% YoY in 2025; late-stage pipeline includes olanzapine LAI (NDA filed December 2025), duvakitug (Phase 3 initiated for UC/Crohn's), anti-IL-15 program
- **Strategic Priorities:** "Pivot to Growth" strategy in Phase 2 ("Accelerate Growth") since mid-2025 -- transitioning from generics dependence to innovative medicines; operating margin expansion to 30% by 2027 (on track; gross margin expanded 310bps to 51.8% in 2025); debt reduction to 2x net leverage (total debt reduced by ~$976M annually, down to ~$16.7B as of Q1 2025); Teva Transformation programs targeting ~$700M net savings through 2027; CNS franchise expansion (AUSTEDO $2.2B in 2025, +35% YoY; UZEDY $190-200M run rate, +120-156% growth; olanzapine LAI NDA filed); immunology investment (duvakitug Phase 3 initiated, $500M milestone from Sanofi); immuno-oncology (TEV-56278 collaboration with Fosun Pharma in Asia); biosimilar launches (Simlandi=Humira interchangeable, Selarsdi=Stelara interchangeable May 2025, Epysqli=Soliris); R&D reallocation toward innovative medicines; API business divestiture in progress (exclusive buyer negotiations terminated November 2025, process continuing); transition to $1.4B net profit in 2025 from $1.6B loss

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Teva's clinical development is organized across three strategic pillars: innovative medicines (neuroscience, immunology), biosimilars, and complex generics. R&D investment was approximately $1.2B in 2024, with increases driven by immunology/immuno-oncology investments and the late-stage neuroscience pipeline. Clinical development in neuroscience focuses on neuropsychiatry, with AUSTEDO ($2.2B revenue in 2025, targeting >$2.5B by 2027 and >$3B peak), AJOVY, and UZEDY as the commercial anchors. AUSTEDO XR (once-daily formulation, FDA-approved May 2024) is protected by 11 Orange Book patents expiring 2031-2041. The olanzapine LAI NDA was filed in December 2025 with zero reported cases of post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome (PDSS), a potential competitive advantage. The immunology pipeline has advanced significantly: duvakitug (anti-TL1A), developed in partnership with Sanofi, reported positive Phase 2b results in Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, initiated Phase 3 in Q4 2025, and triggered a $500M development milestone payment from Sanofi. TEV-56278 (anti-PD1/IL-2 ATTENUKINE) entered an expanded collaboration with Fosun Pharma for Asia-Pacific development in June 2025. Biosimilar development leverages Teva's antibody and device engineering capabilities, with key 2025 launches including SELARSDI (ustekinumab, biosimilar to Stelara, launched February 2025, FDA interchangeable designation May 2025) and EPYSQLI (eculizumab, biosimilar to Soliris). The company operates R&D centers that work on 40-50 pipeline products simultaneously. 2026 is expected to be a milestone-rich year with multiple late-stage pipeline readouts across immunology and neurology, anticipated FDA approval of olanzapine LAI, and important data for duvakitug and anti-IL-15 programs.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs at Teva manages one of the highest-volume filing operations in the industry, covering innovative medicines, biosimilars, and generics across global markets. The regulatory function handles ANDA filings (generics), 505(b)(2) filings (complex generics), BLA/NDA filings (innovative and biosimilars), and international submissions. Key 2025 regulatory milestones include: the olanzapine LAI NDA filing (December 2025); FDA interchangeable designation for SELARSDI (May 2025); SIMLANDI interchangeable biosimilar status; EPYSQLI biosimilar launch; duvakitug Phase 3 IND submissions for UC and Crohn's disease; U.S. and EU submissions for biosimilars to Prolia, Simponi, and Simponi Aria; and UZEDY international launches (regulatory exclusivity until April 2026, with four Orange Book patents expiring 2027-2040). Teva has set targets to increase regulatory submissions by 150% in lower- and middle-income countries across six therapeutic areas. The Anda distribution business was relocated from the U.S. segment to "Other Activities" in 2026, signaling management's intent to focus U.S. segment regulatory resources on innovative drug commercialization.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Teva's PV function manages safety across an extraordinarily broad product portfolio -- 1,800+ generic molecules plus innovative medicines and biosimilars. The PV challenge is unique in its breadth: monitoring safety signals across thousands of products in global markets, each with different regulatory requirements. For innovative products (AUSTEDO/AUSTEDO XR in tardive dyskinesia/Huntington's, AJOVY in migraine), PV requires standard brand-level safety monitoring, with AUSTEDO's rapid growth ($2.2B in 2025) increasing the volume and complexity of safety data. For UZEDY and the forthcoming olanzapine LAI, PV must monitor for long-acting injectable-specific safety signals (notably, the olanzapine LAI's zero PDSS cases in trials will require robust post-marketing confirmation). For the immunology portfolio, duvakitug Phase 3 studies in UC and Crohn's disease introduce new PV requirements for anti-TL1A safety monitoring. For generics, PV focuses on manufacturing quality and post-marketing surveillance. The expanding biosimilar portfolio (Simlandi, Selarsdi, Epysqli) adds immunogenicity monitoring requirements across multiple therapeutic areas. The "Pivot to Growth" strategy's emphasis on innovative medicines is materially increasing the sophistication and resource intensity of the PV function, particularly as the innovative portfolio reached $1B in quarterly revenues for the first time in Q4 2025.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Teva operates at two levels: (1) innovative medicines, where traditional B/R assessment applies to CNS products, biosimilars, and emerging immunology/immuno-oncology assets; and (2) the generics portfolio, where B/R focuses on bioequivalence, manufacturing quality, and substitution safety. The "Pivot to Growth" Phase 2 ("Accelerate Growth") strategy is shifting B/R governance emphasis decisively toward the innovative portfolio, where higher-value B/R decisions support premium pricing and market differentiation. The duvakitug Phase 3 program introduces GI immunology B/R governance requirements (IBD-specific safety monitoring for anti-TL1A), while the immuno-oncology entry (TEV-56278) introduces oncology-grade B/R requirements. The olanzapine LAI program's differentiated safety profile (zero PDSS) creates a B/R governance opportunity to establish safety superiority versus existing LAI antipsychotics. As the innovative portfolio now generates $1B+ per quarter, the volume and strategic importance of innovative B/R decisions has increased substantially.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** CMO/Head of R&D or VP Drug Safety -- driven by the "Accelerate Growth" Phase 2 mandate that elevates the importance of innovative medicine B/R governance as the innovative portfolio surpasses $1B quarterly revenue
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO or VP Biostatistics -- must validate that the platform adds value beyond what Teva's bioequivalence-focused analytical capabilities already provide; duvakitug Phase 3 and olanzapine LAI post-marketing represent near-term validation opportunities
- **Budget Owner:** CMO/Head of R&D ($1.2B+ R&D budget) or CEO (given the strategic nature of the transformation); Teva's return to $1.4B net profitability in 2025 (from $1.6B loss) and 310bps gross margin expansion creates new budget headroom
- **Procurement Cycle:** 6-12 months; Teva is in a transformation environment balancing $700M savings targets with explicit R&D and commercial reinvestment for growth; the transition to profitability improves procurement receptivity
- **Internal Friction Points:** (1) Generics-focused organizational culture is evolving but may still undervalue sophisticated B/R tools designed for innovative medicines; (2) Debt reduction remains a priority (~$16.7B as of early 2025, targeting 2x net leverage by 2027); (3) API divestiture process stalled (exclusive buyer negotiations terminated November 2025), absorbing management attention; (4) Sanofi partnership governance for duvakitug may complicate independent platform procurement decisions

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should frame the value proposition around Teva's "Accelerate Growth" Phase 2 transformation. Teva's transition from a $1.6B net loss to $1.4B net profit in 2025 validates the "Pivot to Growth" strategy, but the next phase demands even more sophisticated B/R governance as the innovative portfolio scales beyond $1B quarterly revenue. The pitch: generics B/R is binary (bioequivalent or not), but innovative medicine B/R is probabilistic and dynamic -- Teva needs ArcaScience to build the B/R infrastructure that matches its new innovative identity. Three immediate entry points present themselves: (1) **Duvakitug Phase 3** -- the anti-TL1A program in UC and Crohn's disease is Teva's highest-value pipeline asset (up to $500M in remaining milestones from Sanofi), and GI immunology B/R governance for IBD requires dynamic, longitudinal safety assessment across complex patient populations; (2) **Olanzapine LAI post-marketing** -- with NDA filed December 2025 and zero PDSS cases in trials, Teva needs robust B/R monitoring to confirm and sustain the safety differentiation claim versus competing LAI antipsychotics; (3) **AUSTEDO franchise management** -- at $2.2B and targeting >$3B peak, AUSTEDO's growing patient population in tardive dyskinesia and Huntington's disease requires increasingly sophisticated real-world B/R monitoring. The biosimilar portfolio (Simlandi, Selarsdi interchangeable, Epysqli) adds volume: demonstrating biosimilar-to-reference product B/R comparability across multiple agencies supports Teva's goal to double biosimilar revenues by 2027. Frame the financial case around the proven transformation ROI: Teva's 310bps gross margin expansion and return to profitability demonstrate that innovative medicine investment generates outsized returns, making B/R platform investment a force multiplier for the portfolio's growth trajectory.
