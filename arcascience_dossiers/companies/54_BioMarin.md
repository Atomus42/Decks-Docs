# BioMarin Pharmaceutical

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $2.85 billion (up 18% YoY)
- **Headquarters:** San Rafael, California, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Rare diseases (skeletal conditions, enzyme replacement therapies), gene therapy (hemophilia A), achondroplasia, phenylketonuria (PKU), Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (AATD)
- **Pipeline Size:** 8 commercial therapies; 3 prioritized clinical-stage programs (BMN 351 for DMD, BMN 349 for AATD, BMN 333 for growth disorders); restructured into 3 business units (Skeletal Conditions, Enzyme Therapies, Roctavian)
- **Strategic Priorities:** Revenue target of $4B by 2027, 11 launches by 2034, VOXZOGO franchise expansion beyond achondroplasia, gene therapy (Roctavian) profitability, business development ($1B in cash for acquisitions in $1.5B range), corporate restructuring, "Core 5" criteria for pipeline prioritization

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
BioMarin recently restructured into three business units: Skeletal Conditions (anchored by VOXZOGO), Enzyme Therapies (ALDURAZYME, BRINEURA, NAGLAZYME, PALYNZIQ, VIMIZIM), and Roctavian (gene therapy for hemophilia A). Clinical development is centralized with a focus on genetically defined populations, targeted therapeutics, assessable endpoints, transformational impact, and commercially competitive profiles (the "Core 5" criteria). The company discontinued 4 programs in 2024 (BMN 331, 255, 355, 365) as part of portfolio prioritization, demonstrating disciplined B/R-driven decision-making.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
BioMarin's regulatory function manages interactions with the FDA, EMA, and global health authorities for its rare disease portfolio. The gene therapy program (Roctavian) has required navigating complex regulatory pathways including conditional approvals and post-marketing commitments. VOXZOGO's expansion into hypochondroplasia and idiopathic short stature involves productive FDA engagement for development program alignment. Rare disease designations, orphan drug status, and accelerated approval pathways are core regulatory strategies.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV/drug safety is particularly critical for BioMarin given the rare disease focus with small patient populations, long-term enzyme replacement therapies, and gene therapy (Roctavian) with extended follow-up requirements. Post-marketing pharmacovigilance for Roctavian is especially complex given the novel gene therapy modality and conditional approvals that require ongoing safety monitoring. The enzyme therapy portfolio requires continuous safety surveillance across pediatric and adult populations globally.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk governance is central to BioMarin's operations. The "Core 5" criteria for pipeline prioritization represent a formalized B/R framework. The decision to discontinue 4 programs in 2024 demonstrates structured B/R evaluation at the portfolio level. For Roctavian, ongoing B/R assessment is critical given the conditional approval status and the need to demonstrate long-term efficacy and safety. Rare disease B/R assessment involves unique considerations including small patient populations, high unmet need, and limited comparator data.
