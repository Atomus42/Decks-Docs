## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Ascendis is organized around its proprietary TransCon technology platform, which enables long-acting prodrug therapies. Clinical development is structured by therapeutic area: Endocrinology Rare Disease (3 programs, all now marketed/approved), Oncology (TransCon IL-2), and emerging areas (ophthalmology via Eyconis subsidiary). All three endocrinology rare disease products have reached market: SKYTROFA fully commercial with label expansion to adult GHD (FDA-approved July 2025) and a Phase 3 basket trial initiated for ISS, SHOX deficiency, Turner syndrome, and SGA; YORVIPATH commercially available in 30+ countries with ongoing PaTHway60 (adults) and PaTHway Adolescent label expansion trials, plus a confirmed once-weekly TransCon PTH product profile; YUVIWEL (navepegritide/TransCon CNP) received FDA accelerated approval in February 2026 as the first and only once-weekly treatment for achondroplasia in children aged 2+ with open epiphyses, with US commercial launch expected early Q2 2026 and an EMA decision anticipated Q4 2026. The COACH trial (TransCon CNP + TransCon hGH combination) continues with Week 78 data expected Q2 2026 and a Phase 3 trial protocol submitted to FDA following an end-of-Phase-2 meeting in Q4 2025; planned expansion into hypochondroplasia. Oncology development (TransCon IL-2 beta/gamma) is advancing with median overall survival data from a 70-patient cohort in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer expected Q2 2026. The Novo Nordisk partnership adds a metabolic disease dimension with TransCon Semaglutide on track to enter the clinic. Eyconis, in which Ascendis holds a 41% equity position, is developing TransCon aVEGF (EYC-0305) for wet AMD and retinal diseases, with clinical entry anticipated in 2026.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages multi-jurisdictional filings across FDA, EMA, NMPA (China), and expanding international markets. The team has now successfully navigated FDA and EMA approvals for SKYTROFA, YORVIPATH, and achieved YUVIWEL FDA accelerated approval (February 2026), which also yielded a Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher (PRV). The SKYTROFA adult GHD label expansion (FDA-approved July 2025) demonstrates lifecycle management capability. EMA MAA decision for YUVIWEL is expected Q4 2026. NMPA is reviewing TransCon hGH BLA for pediatric GHD in China with a decision anticipated Q1 2026. International market expansion involves exclusive licensing agreements with regional partners (Teijin for Japan, Specialised Therapeutics Asia, Er-Kim, Vector Pharma), with YORVIPATH now available in 30+ countries and 10 additional full commercial launches planned by year-end 2026. Rare disease regulatory expertise (orphan drug designations, pediatric exclusivity, accelerated approval pathways) is a core competency with growing complexity as the company manages confirmatory trial obligations for YUVIWEL's accelerated approval.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV monitors safety for three marketed/approved products with distinct profiles: SKYTROFA (pediatric and now adult GHD following July 2025 label expansion), YORVIPATH (hypoparathyroidism, commercially available in 30+ countries), and YUVIWEL (achondroplasia in children, FDA accelerated approval February 2026 — requiring post-marketing confirmatory trial safety monitoring as a condition of continued approval). Pediatric patient populations across both SKYTROFA and YUVIWEL require specialized safety monitoring. YUVIWEL's accelerated approval status creates heightened PV obligations, including ongoing safety surveillance that supports the confirmatory trial framework. The expansion into oncology (TransCon IL-2 beta/gamma) will add immunotherapy safety monitoring requirements. International expansion through partners across 30+ countries and growing adds multi-jurisdictional safety reporting obligations of increasing complexity. The Phase 3 basket trial for SKYTROFA (ISS, SHOX deficiency, Turner syndrome, SGA) introduces new patient populations requiring tailored safety monitoring protocols.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is centralized within a relatively focused organization targeting rare disease blockbuster status for three products. The Vision 2030 strategy requires portfolio-level B/R decisions about expanding from endocrinology rare disease into larger therapeutic areas (oncology, ophthalmology, metabolic). The Novo Nordisk partnership (TransCon technology for GLP-1) represents a major strategic B/R decision about platform value maximization.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
Ascendis's Vision 2030 strategy — three independent blockbuster products plus pipeline expansion into oncology, ophthalmology, and metabolic areas — represents a dramatic scaling challenge for B/R governance. ArcaScience should position the BRA platform as a growth-readiness tool that: (1) supports TransCon CNP NDA/MAA filing strategy with integrated clinical evidence and probabilistic approval modeling for both FDA and EMA, (2) enables systematic B/R assessment as the company expands from 3 rare disease programs into larger therapeutic areas where clinical trial complexity and competitive dynamics are fundamentally different, and (3) integrates real-world evidence from SKYTROFA and YORVIPATH commercial experience back into ongoing development decisions (e.g., COACH combination trial). The rare disease focus is both an advantage (concentrated value per program) and a challenge (limited sample sizes require sophisticated statistical approaches to B/R assessment). A pilot focused on TransCon CNP's NDA filing preparation would demonstrate immediate value at a critical regulatory milestone.
