# Boehringer Ingelheim

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $27.7B (~EUR 29B group net sales; human pharma ~82% of total)
- **Headquarters:** Ingelheim am Rhein, Germany
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Cardiovascular, renal & metabolic diseases (CRM); oncology; respiratory diseases (IPF); immunology; mental health; retinal health
- **Pipeline Size:** ~60 clinical assets across ~90 indications; 70% first-in-class mechanisms; ambition to launch 25 new treatments by 2030; 150+ active research partnerships with 120+ academic institutions
- **Strategic Priorities:** 25 new treatments by 2030; survodutide (MASH/obesity) Phase III advancement; oncology re-entry (zongertinib, ADCs, precision oncology); nerandomilast approval for IPF; iclepertin for mental health; massive R&D investment ($6.7B in 2024, 27.6% of human pharma sales); external innovation (50%+ of pipeline from partnerships); family-owned long-term investment model

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Boehringer Ingelheim's clinical development is organized under the Innovation Unit, led by a Head of Innovation Unit who sits on the Board of Managing Directors. The Innovation Unit encompasses both research and clinical development across all therapeutic areas. Within the Innovation Unit, development is organized by therapeutic area (CRM, oncology, respiratory, immunology, mental health, retinal health) with a Global Head of R&D overseeing clinical operations. The company maintains R&D presence in eight countries with central sites in Germany, the U.S., Austria, Japan, France, and China. More than 11,500 employees work across the two global R&D networks (human pharma and animal health). The company initiated 5 new Phase I, II, or III trials in the first half of 2024 alone across cardiometabolic, mental health, and oncology.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs is part of the broader Medicine Division, which also encompasses medical affairs and related functions. Regional heads of regulatory affairs operate across all major markets. The regulatory function manages an increasingly complex portfolio that includes both established products (Jardiance, Ofev, Spiriva) requiring lifecycle management and novel assets requiring first-time filings. Key regulatory milestones include nerandomilast (JASCAYD) approval in China for IPF, survodutide Phase III advancement, and oncology pipeline progression. As a private, family-owned company, BI does not face the same short-term investor pressure on regulatory timelines as public companies, but the "25 treatments by 2030" target creates internal urgency.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Pharmacovigilance at BI is organized under the Patient Safety and Pharmacovigilance (PSPV) function, led by a Corporate Senior Vice President/Global Head. The PSPV organization includes regional pharmacovigilance teams, an EU-QPPV (Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance) office, and PV operations. The function is responsible for defining quality frameworks, coordinating PV inspections and audits, and developing global PV training strategy. Given BI's large marketed portfolio (Jardiance, Ofev, Spiriva, Trajenta) covering millions of patients globally, the PV workload is substantial. Novel modalities entering the pipeline (ADCs from NBE Therapeutics, precision oncology) will require expanded safety monitoring capabilities.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is embedded within the R&D portfolio management process overseen by the Innovation Unit. The Board of Managing Directors, including the Head of Innovation Unit, provides strategic oversight of B/R decisions for major programs. As a privately held company, BI has the advantage of taking longer-term views on B/R trade-offs without quarterly earnings pressure. The Chief Medical Officer role (Corporate Division Medicine) provides medical oversight of B/R assessments across the portfolio. The company's Venture Fund ($378M+) also makes B/R investment decisions for early-stage external innovation.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Global Head PSPV or CMO -- driven by the scale of managing B/R across 60 assets in 90 indications, or the oncology re-entry requiring new B/R frameworks for novel modalities
- **Scientific Validator:** Head of Innovation Unit or Global Head of R&D -- must confirm scientific rigor of probabilistic BRA approach
- **Budget Owner:** Head of Innovation Unit (Board member) or CMO (Medicine Division)
- **Procurement Cycle:** 12-18 months; family-owned company culture emphasizes thoroughness and long-term value; however, the "25 by 2030" urgency may create faster decision windows; R&D budget of $6.7B provides ample funding capacity
- **Internal Friction Points:** (1) Family-owned company culture may resist external platform dependencies; (2) Strong internal R&D capabilities (11,500+ R&D employees) may lead to "build vs. buy" preference; (3) 50%+ of pipeline from partnerships means B/R data may sit in partner systems, complicating integration; (4) German pharmaceutical company culture tends toward cautious evaluation of new vendors

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should position around BI's unique combination of scale and ambition: 60 assets across 90 indications, with a target of 25 new treatments by 2030, managed by a privately held company that can take a long-term view on R&D investments. The pitch: BI's pipeline scale and ambition demands a platform-level approach to B/R governance -- manual, program-by-program B/R assessment cannot scale to 90 indications. Two high-value entry points: (1) Survodutide in MASH/obesity, where the B/R profile must differentiate from semaglutide and other competitors -- ArcaScience can model the evolving competitive B/R landscape dynamically; (2) The oncology re-entry, where BI is building B/R governance for novel modalities (ADCs, precision oncology) from a relative blank slate -- ArcaScience can help establish best-in-class B/R processes from the start rather than retrofitting. BI's heavy reliance on external partnerships (50%+ of pipeline) creates a data integration challenge that ArcaScience is well-positioned to address, serving as the B/R platform that unifies data from internal programs and partner collaborations. Frame the long-term relationship in terms BI's family-owned culture values: partnership, durability, and shared commitment to patient safety.
