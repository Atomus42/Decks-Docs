# Sanofi

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** EUR 43.6 billion (up 9.9% CER); Business EPS EUR 7.83 (up 15% CER). FY2024: EUR ~47.9B including Opella; Q3 2025: Dupixent exceeded EUR 4B in a single quarter for the first time. 2026 guidance: high single-digit sales growth, EPS growth exceeding sales growth.
- **Headquarters:** Paris, France
- **Corporate Posture (post-April 2025):** Pure-play biopharma following Opella consumer health separation (closed April 30, 2025; 50% controlling stake sold to CD&R; Sanofi retains 48.2%; ~EUR 10B net cash proceeds). Gross margin improved to 77.5% (+1.8pp), reflecting strategic shift toward high-value specialty care. EUR 5B share buyback executed in 2025; additional EUR 1B planned for 2026. Paul Hudson (CEO) is board chair-elect of PhRMA, succeeding Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla in 2026.
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Immunology (Dupixent franchise -- EUR 15.7B in FY2025, +25% YoY; amlitelimab [anti-OX40L], duvakitug [anti-TL1A]), Rare Disease (ALTUVIIIO exceeded EUR 1B in first full year; systemic mastocytosis via Blueprint Medicines -- avapritinib/Ayvakit), Vaccines (EUR 10B+ sales target by 2030), Neurology (selective re-entry via TREM2 agonist; Vigil Neuroscience acquisition closed August 2025), Oncology (selective)
- **Pipeline Size:** 82 clinical-stage projects; ~30 in Phase III or at registration; 8 regulatory submissions accepted in Q3 2025 alone. Looking ahead: 15+ regulatory decisions, 30+ regulatory submissions, and 15+ Phase 3 data readouts expected across 2026-2027.
- **Key 2025 Transactions:** (1) Opella separation closed April 30, 2025 (EUR 10B proceeds); (2) Blueprint Medicines acquisition completed July 18, 2025 ($9.1B upfront + $400M milestone CVRs) -- adds avapritinib (Ayvakit/Ayvakyt, $479M in 2024 revenue, 60%+ YoY growth), elenestinib (next-gen SM), and BLU-808 (wild-type KIT inhibitor for broad inflammatory diseases); (3) Vigil Neuroscience (closed Aug 2025); (4) Vicebio agreement (signed July 2025); (5) DR-0201 asset acquisition from Dren Bio (May 2025); (6) Adagene strategic investment up to $25M (Jan 2026) for masked antibody therapies
- **Strategic Priorities:** Global immunology leadership ("Play to Win" strategy), AI-first pharma ambition ("first biopharma company powered by AI at scale"), pure-play biopharma transformation post-Opella, aggressive pipeline expansion via acquisitions (5 deals in 2025 alone), digital transformation across R&D/manufacturing/supply via three Digital Accelerators, RAISE responsible AI governance framework fully operational, regulatory affairs digital transformation via Accumulus Synergy

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Sanofi's clinical development is organized under the R&D function, with therapeutic area-aligned teams spanning Immunology, Rare Disease, Vaccines, and selective Oncology/Neurology. Post-Opella, the organizational structure has been simplified into a pure-play biopharma with clearer lines of authority and reduced internal complexity -- management attention is no longer divided across consumer health operations. Paul Hudson (CEO) drives the "Play to Win" strategy that has refocused the company on immunology leadership. Brian Foard (Head, Specialty Care) oversees the commercial launch portfolio across immunology, neuro-inflammatory, rare disease, and oncology. R&D investment reached EUR 7.45 billion in 2024 (~17.3% of sales), and the company is pursuing a voluntary R&D restructuring program (2024-2026 GEPP program in France) specifically to reorganize R&D operations for immunology leadership. R&D functions include PreClinical Safety, Laboratory Sciences, Quantitative Pharmacology, Operations & Evidence Generation & Decision Science, Early Development Clinical Operations (EDCO), Regulatory Affairs, Patient Safety & Pharmacovigilance, and Therapeutic Area R&D Groups. The Blueprint Medicines integration (completed July 2025) brings an established presence among allergists, dermatologists, and immunologists, enhancing Sanofi's ability to advance its growing immunology pipeline, and adds a Cambridge, MA-based rare disease R&D hub with deep expertise in KIT-driven biology and safety monitoring for rare immunological conditions.

**Digital Accelerators** are now producing measurable results across three domains: (1) Digital strategy (2022), (2) R&D-focused (early 2024, already identified **7 new therapeutic targets** and deployed **Patient Digital Twin** technology for predictive personalized medicine), and (3) Manufacturing & Supply (digital AI in production). The OpenAI/Formation Bio partnership (May 2024) -- the first collaboration of its kind between pharma, an AI lab, and a tech-bio company -- is producing AI-powered development tools to accelerate drug development timelines. Combined with the internal plai platform, these capabilities have reportedly reduced certain R&D costs by up to 40%. Over 2,000 Sanofi executives have completed AI training through the HEC Paris executive education program, creating unusually high AI literacy across the leadership ranks.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Sanofi has fundamentally **transformed its Regulatory Affairs function** from a compliance-focused back-end role to a strategic partner embedded from day one in drug development. Key elements of this transformation include:
- Regulatory professionals are now embedded at R&D design tables from the earliest stages of development
- **NLP-powered tools**, **automated translations**, and **AI-generated drafts** have been introduced to streamline document preparation
- Sanofi is a founding participant and early adopter of **Accumulus Synergy** -- a cloud-based platform co-developed by industry and regulators for real-time regulatory data exchange, now operating across 50+ countries
- RA professionals are freed from mechanical tasks to focus on strategic judgment, scientific expertise, and health authority relationship management
- Regulatory submissions are managed across immunology expansions, rare disease approvals, and vaccine registrations globally

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Sanofi's **Global Pharmacovigilance Department (GPV)** is the reference center of excellence for health and safety surveillance of all Sanofi medicinal products. GPV processes **over 700,000 adverse event report versions annually**, with case volume growing up to 20% per year -- a scale challenge that has driven aggressive automation investment. GPV operates under a **transversal, multidisciplinary safety governance model** with governance teams comprising empowered senior decision-making individuals with safety, ethics, legal, scientific, medical, clinical, and regulatory expertise. GPV Operations manages both passive and active adverse event collection and processing globally.

**Project ARTEMIS** (Adverse Event Processing using Technology Enabled Medical and Intelligent Solution) is now fully operational on the IQVIA Vigilance Platform (IVP). As of late 2025, ARTEMIS has achieved full legacy system replacement -- Sanofi is now one of the few pharma companies running a single, end-to-end automated PV system. Key ARTEMIS milestones: 15% average cost savings already realized versus pre-implementation baseline, with a target of **50% cost reduction by 2027**. Critically, ARTEMIS has moved beyond intake automation into **AI-driven medical evaluations** -- applying automation to complex medical review activities, not just data entry. This creates a clear **integration opportunity** for a B/R analytics layer: ARTEMIS automates case processing, but the analytical intelligence layer that converts processed safety data into continuous, probabilistic B/R assessment does not yet exist.

**Dupixent COPD approval creates a population-scale safety monitoring challenge.** With COPD approval now live in 45+ countries (including US since September 2024, Japan since March 2025), Dupixent is expanding into a fundamentally different patient population -- older adults with extensive comorbidities (cardiovascular disease, diabetes, renal impairment) and polypharmacy profiles that differ dramatically from the existing atopic dermatitis/asthma patient base. This new population introduces novel drug interaction signals, different baseline event rates, and requires adapted B/R models that account for the COPD comorbidity landscape. The Blueprint Medicines acquisition further compounds the safety monitoring complexity, adding rare disease populations (systemic mastocytosis) with unique safety profiles, limited safety databases, and orphan drug regulatory expectations where probabilistic B/R modeling is especially valuable.

GPV's aspiration is to be a "cutting-edge safety organization," and the combination of ARTEMIS operational maturity, Dupixent COPD population-scale expansion, and Blueprint rare disease integration creates an inflection point where next-generation B/R analytical tools become essential rather than aspirational.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
The pharmacovigilance operating governance model consists of transversal and multidisciplinary safety governance teams with empowered senior decision-makers spanning safety, ethics, legal, scientific, medical, clinical, and regulatory functions. This cross-functional governance structure ensures that B/R decisions incorporate multiple perspectives. GPV's mandate to monitor the "benefit-risk of its therapies" is explicit, with B/R monitoring integrated into the overall pharmacovigilance governance framework. The governance model feeds into executive-level oversight through the Executive Committee (chaired by CEO, meeting twice monthly).

**The B/R governance challenge is intensifying across three dimensions:**
1. **Dupixent population diversification:** Managing B/R across 9 approved indications -- now spanning atopic dermatitis, asthma, CRSwNP, eosinophilic esophagitis, prurigo nodularis, CSU, bullous pemphigoid, and COPD -- with each indication presenting distinct patient populations, comorbidity profiles, and baseline event rates. The COPD population alone introduces ~300,000 adults in the US with fundamentally different risk profiles from existing Dupixent patients. Dupixent peak sales ambition of EUR 22B by 2030 means B/R monitoring scale will continue to expand.
2. **Blueprint Medicines rare disease integration:** Avapritinib (Ayvakit) for systemic mastocytosis, elenestinib (next-gen SM), and BLU-808 (wild-type KIT for broad inflammatory diseases) each bring rare disease safety databases with small population sizes, where probabilistic B/R modeling is both more challenging and more valuable. The Blueprint acquisition also brings allergist/dermatologist/immunologist relationships that overlap with Dupixent's prescriber base, creating cross-product safety monitoring synergies.
3. **Pipeline maturation:** With amlitelimab heading toward global regulatory submissions in H2 2026 (novel anti-OX40L mechanism, including a Kaposi's sarcoma signal flagged in Phase 2 ATLANTIS safety study) and duvakitug in four Phase 3 studies for UC/CD (anti-TL1A mechanism), the B/R governance apparatus must scale to accommodate diverse immunology mechanisms with distinct safety profiles simultaneously.

**RAISE framework intersection:** Any AI-powered B/R analytics tool must pass through Sanofi's RAISE (Responsible AI at Sanofi for Everyone) governance framework, which is fully operational with five pillars: Accountable to Outcomes, Fair & Ethical, Robust & Safe, Transparent & Explainable, and Eco-Responsible. The AI Risk Assessment tool (launched February 2024) standardizes risk classification for all AI use cases. The Interim Responsible AI Governance (IRAG) body reviews high-risk AI use cases and advises on risk mitigation. ArcaScience's BRA platform would likely be classified as high-risk given its role in safety decision-making, requiring IRAG review and demonstration of traceability, explainability, and regulatory compliance (EU AI Act, FDA guidance).

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Head of GPV is now the primary entry point -- Project ARTEMIS is fully operational on IVP, creating a natural "what's next?" moment. The ARTEMIS team has achieved full system replacement and is now focused on expanding AI into medical evaluations, making them receptive to an analytical B/R layer. The R&D Digital Accelerator team remains a secondary entry point with a mandate to deploy AI tools across R&D.
- **Scientific Validator:** GPV leadership and RA leadership jointly; the transversal governance model means both safety and regulatory perspectives are evaluated together. The OpenAI/Formation Bio partnership team may also evaluate AI capabilities. **New consideration:** The Blueprint Medicines safety monitoring team (Cambridge, MA) may need to be included in validation given rare disease safety monitoring requirements for avapritinib/elenestinib/BLU-808.
- **Budget Owner:** Head of R&D/CSO for R&D-wide deployment (EUR 7.45B R&D budget); Head of GPV for safety-specific deployment. **Post-Opella budget availability is at a high-water mark:** EUR 10B net proceeds from Opella, plus improved gross margins (77.5%), plus explicit guidance for "bolt-on acquisitions" and R&D investment as priority uses of proceeds. Sanofi is executing EUR 5B in buybacks in 2025 and EUR 1B in 2026, demonstrating capital availability is not a constraint. FY2025 business EPS growth of 15% CER provides P&L headroom for strategic technology investments.
- **Procurement Cycle:** 9-15 months. Sanofi's "AI-first" culture and Digital Accelerator model create fast evaluation pathways. The OpenAI/Formation Bio precedent shows willingness to engage external AI partners rapidly. However, the RAISE framework and AI Risk Assessment tool add a mandatory governance review step (likely IRAG review for a B/R analytics tool classified as high-risk). French corporate governance and works council requirements may add time for enterprise deployments. **Timing advantage:** The post-ARTEMIS "what's next?" window and the Dupixent COPD safety monitoring urgency create a compressed decision timeline for B/R analytics specifically.
- **Internal Friction Points:** The OpenAI/Formation Bio partnership and IQVIA ARTEMIS platform represent significant existing AI commitments -- ArcaScience must position as complementary (B/R analytics layer on top of ARTEMIS), not competitive. The RAISE framework requires all AI systems to be traceable, explainable, and compliant with stringent regulations -- proactive RAISE alignment is essential. Blueprint Medicines integration is actively consuming organizational bandwidth through 2026 (but also creates urgent need for cross-portfolio B/R harmonization). Sanofi's HEC Paris executive AI training program for 2,000+ executives creates high AI literacy, meaning buyers will be sophisticated evaluators who will probe technical claims rigorously. French data privacy laws (CNIL enforcement) and EU AI Act compliance add regulatory complexity. Paul Hudson has been a vocal critic of "AI washing," so any engagement must demonstrate concrete, measurable outcomes rather than aspirational claims.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Enter through GPV's post-ARTEMIS "what's next?" moment, positioning as the B/R analytical intelligence layer that completes the automation-to-insight journey -- with Dupixent COPD population-scale monitoring as the urgent use case.**

**Why now (Q1-Q2 2026 is the optimal window):**
- **ARTEMIS is fully operational** -- the legacy PV system has been fully replaced by IVP, meaning the case processing automation chapter is closed. GPV leadership is now looking at the next frontier: converting automated AE data into actionable B/R intelligence. This is the exact gap ArcaScience fills.
- **Dupixent COPD launch is scaling** -- with approvals in 45+ countries and US quarterly sales exceeding EUR 3B, the COPD patient population (older, comorbid, polypharmacy) is entering the Dupixent safety database at scale. This creates an urgent, concrete need for B/R analytics that can model the different risk profiles of COPD patients versus the existing atopic/asthma base.
- **Post-Opella focus and budget** -- EUR 10B in proceeds, 77.5% gross margins, and a pure-play biopharma identity mean both strategic attention and capital are available. Sanofi has explicitly stated proceeds will fuel organic R&D growth and bolt-on acquisitions/partnerships.
- **Blueprint integration creates cross-portfolio complexity** -- avapritinib, elenestinib, and BLU-808 add rare disease safety monitoring needs that compound the B/R governance challenge across the immunology franchise.

The narrative should center on:

1. **Completing the ARTEMIS vision** -- ARTEMIS automates case management and is driving toward 50% cost savings by 2027. But automation without analytical intelligence is a half-built bridge. ArcaScience's BRA platform provides the analytical layer that converts 700,000+ processed AE report versions per year into continuous, probabilistic B/R profiles. This is the missing layer between automation and governance decision-making. Position as **complementary to IQVIA** (not competitive) -- IQVIA processes, ArcaScience analyzes.
2. **Dupixent COPD as the flagship use case** -- Dupixent across 9 indications with a peak sales target of EUR 22B by 2030, but the COPD population (~300,000 US adults) introduces novel drug interaction signals, different baseline event rates, and cardiovascular/renal comorbidity considerations that the existing Dupixent B/R model was not built for. Demonstrate how probabilistic B/R modeling can differentiate COPD-specific safety signals from background noise in a population with high baseline event rates.
3. **Aligning with the "AI-first" corporate identity** -- Sanofi's ambition to be the "first biopharma company powered by AI at scale" creates corporate-level receptivity. ArcaScience can position as fulfilling this vision specifically for B/R assessment, a domain not yet covered by the OpenAI/Formation Bio partnership (which focuses on drug development acceleration) or ARTEMIS (which focuses on case processing). **Key differentiation:** B/R analytics is the one major AI gap in Sanofi's otherwise comprehensive AI ecosystem.
4. **Rare disease B/R complexity** -- The Blueprint Medicines acquisition adds rare immunological diseases (systemic mastocytosis via avapritinib, with $479M in 2024 revenue growing 60%+ YoY) with unique safety profiles. Rare disease drugs have limited safety databases, making probabilistic B/R modeling especially valuable for regulatory submissions and lifecycle management. BLU-808's potential expansion into broad inflammatory diseases could further amplify this need.
5. **Proactive RAISE navigation** -- Rather than treating RAISE as a late-stage hurdle, ArcaScience should lead with RAISE alignment from the first conversation. Prepare an AI Risk Assessment pre-submission that maps the BRA platform to all five RAISE pillars (Accountable to Outcomes, Fair & Ethical, Robust & Safe, Transparent & Explainable, Eco-Responsible). Given that Paul Hudson is a vocal critic of "AI washing," the pitch must emphasize measurable outcomes, validated methodology, and regulatory-grade evidence rather than aspirational AI claims.

**Conference and network touchpoints:** DIA Global or Drug Safety Europe Congress remain ideal, where Sanofi's GPV leadership is likely present. Alternatively, leveraging the Accumulus Synergy network could provide a warm introduction, as Sanofi is a founding participant. The Blueprint Medicines acquisition also opens a potential Boston/Cambridge entry point through the rare disease safety monitoring team. The January 2026 Adagene collaboration suggests Sanofi's BD team remains active and receptive to external partnerships.
