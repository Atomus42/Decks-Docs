# Neurocrine Biosciences

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $2.355 billion (up 24.8% YoY); INGREZZA net product sales of $2.3B (up 26% YoY); CRENESSITY launched in late 2024
- **Headquarters:** San Diego, California, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Neuroscience — tardive dyskinesia, Huntington's disease chorea, congenital adrenal hyperplasia (endocrinology), major depressive disorder, schizophrenia, movement disorders (Parkinson's tremor, dystonia), Alzheimer's disease, obesity/metabolic diseases
- **Pipeline Size:** 2 Phase 3 programs (osavampator for MDD, direclidine for schizophrenia); multiple Phase 1-2 programs including next-gen VMAT2 inhibitors, muscarinic agonists (M1, M4, M1/M4 dual), CRF-based therapies for obesity; target of 4+ Phase 1 and 2+ Phase 2 programs per year; approved medicine every 2 years by end of decade
- **Strategic Priorities:** INGREZZA franchise growth ($2.5-2.6B guidance for 2025), pipeline diversification beyond VMAT2, muscarinic portfolio build-out, CRF-based metabolic/obesity platform, CRENESSITY launch execution, R&D engine delivering 4+ Phase 1 programs annually, building a "leading neuroscience company"

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Neurocrine has built a "transformed R&D engine" designed to deliver at least 4 new Phase 1 and 2 Phase 2 programs per year, focusing on genetically or clinically validated mechanisms of action. The company has a rich pipeline spanning neurology, psychiatry, and endocrinology with programs from Phase 1 through commercial. Key Phase 3 programs include osavampator (AMPA receptor potentiator) for MDD and direclidine (M4 muscarinic agonist) for schizophrenia. The muscarinic portfolio includes multiple candidates for different CNS indications (Alzheimer's psychosis, Alzheimer's cognition, Lewy body dementia). Next-generation VMAT2 inhibitors are engineered for long-acting injectable formulations.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs has managed successful FDA approvals for INGREZZA (tardive dyskinesia, Huntington's chorea), CRENESSITY (congenital adrenal hyperplasia, first new treatment in 70+ years), and collaborations with partners (endometriosis, uterine fibroids with AbbVie). The regulatory team navigates neuropsychiatric endpoints (which are inherently subjective and complex) and rare disease pathways (CRENESSITY for CAH). Managing two Phase 3 pivotal programs simultaneously (osavampator, direclidine) requires significant regulatory resources.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV supports INGREZZA (the largest commercial product by far, with 26% revenue growth) and the newly launched CRENESSITY. INGREZZA PV involves monitoring for movement disorder patients who often have complex psychiatric comorbidities and polypharmacy. CRENESSITY PV for CAH requires long-term endocrine safety monitoring. The expanding pipeline of novel mechanisms (muscarinic agonists, CRF modulators, AMPA potentiators) will create growing PV complexity as programs advance.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is central to Neurocrine's strategy given the complexity of neuropsychiatric drug development where subjective endpoints, high placebo response rates, and CNS safety considerations dominate. The company's transformed R&D engine focuses on "genetically or clinically validated mechanisms" as a B/R risk mitigation strategy. Portfolio-level B/R decisions determine which of the multiple muscarinic candidates advance and in which indications. The goal of delivering an approved medicine every 2 years requires systematic B/R optimization.
