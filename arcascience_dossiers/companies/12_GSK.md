# GSK

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $38.4B (GBP 31.4B); long-acting HIV regimens grew 46% in 2025 and now account for 30% of U.S. HIV sales
- **Headquarters:** London, United Kingdom
- **CEO:** Luke Miels (effective 1 January 2026, succeeding Emma Walmsley; former Chief Commercial Officer with deep oncology and specialty medicines background)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Respiratory/immunology & inflammation, oncology (ADCs, Blenrep combinations), HIV (long-acting injectables), infectious diseases (vaccines), immunology (RAPT Therapeutics acquisition for $1.9B)
- **Pipeline Size:** 71 specialty medicines and vaccines in clinical development (19 in Phase III/registration as of early 2025); 14 "scale innovation opportunities" each with >GBP 2B peak sales potential; pipeline bolstered by Hengrui deal (up to 12 medicines), IDRx acquisition ($1B, GIST), Boston Pharma efimosfermin deal ($1.2B upfront, MASH)
- **Dividend/Buyback:** 2025 total dividend 66p; 2026 target 70p; GBP 1.4B of GBP 2B share buyback completed; net debt GBP 14.5B
- **Strategic Priorities:** Pipeline execution to meet 2031 revenue target of >GBP 40B (reaffirmed under new CEO); navigating dolutegravir patent cliff (2028-2030, threatening GBP 5.7B+ in annual HIV revenue); ultra-long-acting HIV regimens advancing (VH499 capsid inhibitor and VH184 third-generation INSTI, both targeting twice-yearly dosing; Q4M cabotegravir+rilpivirine CUATRO registrational study initiating H1 2026, filing 2027, launch 2028); Hengrui scaled collaboration ($500M upfront, up to $12B total, 12 medicines across respiratory, immunology, oncology); ADC pipeline acceleration (GSK'227 B7H3 ADC pivotal trials H2 2025 for ES-SCLC); $2B U.S. manufacturing investment; ViiV Healthcare ownership restructuring (Pfizer exiting 12% stake for $1.9B, Shionogi increasing to 22%); Zantac litigation substantially resolved ($2.2B settlement covering ~80,000 cases)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
GSK operates a centralized R&D organization led by the Chief Scientific Officer, with clinical development organized by therapeutic area across four core disease areas: Respiratory/Immunology & Inflammation, Oncology, HIV, and Infectious Diseases. These four areas receive 80% of pharmaceutical R&D capital allocation. The company invested GBP 6.4B in R&D in 2024, reflecting its transformation into an innovation-driven biopharma company post-Consumer Healthcare demerger (Haleon). Clinical development teams work within a matrix model that integrates early discovery, translational science, and late-stage clinical operations. Under new CEO Luke Miels (January 2026), the clinical development agenda has intensified with pivotal/Phase III trial starts in H2 2025 for GSK'227 B7H3 ADC (ES-SCLC), GSK'981/IDRx-42 (2L GIST), efimosfermin (MASH), and cabotegravir ultra-long-acting + rilpivirine Q4M (HIV treatment). The Hengrui scaled collaboration adds up to 11 additional programs where Hengrui leads development through Phase I completion, after which GSK holds exclusive worldwide development and commercialization options -- creating a novel "pipeline-as-a-service" model that will require robust B/R governance for externally originated assets.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Global Regulatory Affairs operates as a centralized function supporting all therapeutic areas and the vaccines business. Regulatory strategy is tightly linked to the 14 scale innovation opportunities, each requiring coordinated multi-market submissions. The regulatory function manages complex global filing strategies including accelerated pathways (Breakthrough Therapy, Priority Review) -- notably for the oncology pipeline (Blenrep combinations, ADCs) and the HIV franchise (lenacapavir competition response). The ODAC rejection of Blenrep dosing in 2025 highlights the criticality of regulatory strategy optimization.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
GSK's pharmacovigilance organization is a global function supporting both the Specialty Medicines and Vaccines segments. Given the breadth of the marketed portfolio (including high-volume vaccines like Shingrix and biologics like Nucala and Benlysta), the PV function manages a massive safety data stream. The vaccines PV function has distinct requirements for population-level safety monitoring. Drug safety teams are embedded within therapeutic area clinical development organizations while reporting into a global PV governance structure.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance operates through GSK's R&D governance committees, including portfolio review boards that assess clinical programs against the company's ambitious 2031 growth targets. The record 13 positive Phase III readouts in 2024 demonstrates the intensity of B/R decision-making activity. B/R assessments are particularly critical for the oncology pipeline (balancing efficacy against toxicity for Blenrep combinations, ADCs) and for the HIV franchise transition from dolutegravir to next-generation regimens.
