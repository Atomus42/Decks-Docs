# Alnylam Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** ~$3.0 billion total net product revenues (up ~81% YoY), driven by landmark AMVUTTRA ATTR-CM approval; 2025 marked the company's first year of sustained profitability; cash and equivalents of ~$2.8 billion at year-end; FY2024 revenue was $2.248B
- **Headquarters:** Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** RNAi therapeutics across rare disease (transthyretin amyloidosis, acute hepatic porphyria, primary hyperoxaluria), cardiovascular (hypertension, cholesterol), neurology (Alzheimer's disease, CAA), hematology (hemophilia A/B), metabolic (type 2 diabetes), and oncology (hepatocellular carcinoma)
- **Pipeline Size:** 6 approved medicines (ONPATTRO, AMVUTTRA incl. ATTR-CM, GIVLAARI, OXLUMO, Qfitlia/fitusiran with Sanofi, Leqvio with Novartis); clinical programs include zilebesiran (hypertension, Phase 3 KARDIA-3 and ZENITH), mivelsiran (Alzheimer's/CAA, Phase 2 cAPPricorn), nucresiran (hATTR-PN, Phase 3 TRITON-PN), ALN-6400 (bleeding disorders, Phase 2), ALN-KHK (T2DM), ALN-BCAT (HCC); Regeneron partner program cemdisiran (myasthenia gravis, Phase 3 NIMBLE positive, US filing planned Q1 2026); 2026 pipeline goals include 4 clinical readouts, 3 ongoing pivotal studies, 3 Phase 2 initiations, 3+ new IND filings
- **Strategic Priorities:** "Alnylam 2030" vision (replacing prior P5x25 strategy) for scaling RNAi to prevalent diseases, AMVUTTRA ATTR-CM global expansion, zilebesiran cardiovascular franchise buildout, CNS platform expansion (mivelsiran for Alzheimer's/CAA), siRELIS enzymatic-ligation manufacturing platform ($250M investment), RNAi platform innovation (reLNP technology), Novartis/Regeneron/Sanofi partnerships, AI-powered product complaint management (Amazon Bedrock), sustainable profitability and innovation

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Alnylam's clinical development organization is built around the RNAi therapeutic platform, with deep expertise in siRNA design, delivery technology (GalNAc conjugation, lipid nanoparticles, reLNP), and rare disease clinical trial design. Development spans from rare diseases (where the company pioneered RNAi therapeutics) to prevalent conditions (cardiovascular, CNS, hematology). Key programs involve registration-enabling studies (KARDIA-3 and ZENITH for zilebesiran, cAPPricorn-1 for mivelsiran in CAA, TRITON-PN for nucresiran in hATTR-PN) and recently completed label expansions (AMVUTTRA for ATTR-CM approved in 2025 with Priority Review). In 2025, Alnylam's partner Sanofi achieved FDA approval of Qfitlia (fitusiran) for hemophilia A/B, the sixth RNAi therapeutic approved by the FDA. Regeneron reported positive Phase 3 NIMBLE results for cemdisiran monotherapy in generalized myasthenia gravis, with US regulatory filing planned for Q1 2026. 2026 pipeline goals are ambitious: 4 clinical readouts, 3 ongoing pivotal studies, 3 Phase 2 study initiations, and 3+ new IND filings, including a Phase 2 of mivelsiran in Alzheimer's disease and a Phase 2 of ALN-6400 in a second bleeding disorder.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs has successfully navigated FDA approvals for 6 RNAi therapeutics (with the 2025 AMVUTTRA ATTR-CM and Qfitlia approvals), firmly establishing RNAi as a validated drug class. The AMVUTTRA ATTR-CM sNDA achieved FDA approval using a Priority Review Voucher, and the EMA's CHMP issued a positive opinion recommending vutrisiran approval for ATTR-CM, with marketing authorization applications under review by several global health agencies. The team manages complex regulatory pathways including breakthrough therapy designations and global submissions across US, EU, and other markets. The expansion from rare diseases to prevalent conditions (cardiovascular, CNS, hematology) requires adapting regulatory strategies for larger-population indications. Novel delivery technologies (reLNP) and the new siRELIS enzymatic-ligation manufacturing platform present new regulatory considerations for CMC and manufacturing change management.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV now supports 5 commercial products with the addition of AMVUTTRA for ATTR-CM (approved 2025), plus the Sanofi-partnered Qfitlia in hemophilia, significantly expanding the patient population under surveillance beyond the original rare disease base (ONPATTRO, AMVUTTRA for polyneuropathy, GIVLAARI, OXLUMO). The ATTR-CM indication alone represents a substantially larger patient population than all prior rare disease indications combined, creating a step-change in PV volume and complexity. RNAi therapeutics have a distinctive safety profile (injection site reactions, potential off-target gene silencing effects) requiring specialized PV monitoring. Alnylam has deployed generative AI (Amazon Bedrock) for product complaint management, reducing triage time from 3 days to hours and information searching from 15 minutes to 30 seconds — demonstrating appetite for AI-powered safety and quality workflows. The company has published a responsible AI governance policy covering GxP-compliant AI use. The expansion into cardiovascular (zilebesiran) and CNS (mivelsiran) indications will further increase PV scope and patient population size, with cemdisiran (myasthenia gravis, Regeneron partnership) adding yet another therapeutic area.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Alnylam spans the full spectrum from rare disease (small populations, high unmet need, orphan drug framework) to prevalent disease (large populations, competitive landscape, active comparator trials). The successful AMVUTTRA ATTR-CM approval in 2025 validated the company's B/R framework for expanding into a larger cardiomyopathy population with different risk profiles than the original polyneuropathy indication. Under the "Alnylam 2030" strategy, the company is now pursuing even larger prevalent disease markets (hypertension, Alzheimer's), representing the next phase of B/R strategic evolution. Platform-level B/R considerations (RNAi class effects, delivery technology safety, siRELIS manufacturing platform) apply across the entire pipeline, now spanning 6 approved products and 4+ pivotal-stage programs. The positive Phase 3 NIMBLE results for cemdisiran in myasthenia gravis (Regeneron partnership) add autoimmune disease to the B/R governance scope. With $2.8 billion in cash and a profitability milestone achieved, Alnylam has expanded capacity to invest in B/R infrastructure that scales across its diversifying portfolio.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Alnylam at its strategic transition from rare disease to prevalent disease applications of RNAi therapeutics. This transition creates a fundamental B/R challenge: the company must adapt its B/R frameworks from small populations with high unmet need (where regulators accept more risk) to large populations with active comparators (where B/R demonstration is more demanding). The zilebesiran cardiovascular program is the ideal entry point: a Phase 3 trial in hypertension (billions of patients globally) requires demonstrating B/R superiority or differentiation vs. established antihypertensives, a fundamentally different B/R challenge than rare disease. The platform also adds value at the RNAi class level, where Alnylam can systematically track B/R signals across all programs to identify platform-level safety patterns. Position ArcaScience as the bridge between Alnylam's rare disease B/R expertise and its prevalent disease ambitions, enabling seamless B/R assessment that scales from thousands to millions of patients. Engage through the Cambridge headquarters R&D leadership, emphasizing both program-level and platform-level B/R capabilities.
