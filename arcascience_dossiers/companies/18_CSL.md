# CSL

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $13.3B (US$15.6B in FY2025 ending June 2025)
- **Headquarters:** Melbourne, Australia
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Plasma-derived therapies (immunoglobulins, hematology), rare diseases (hereditary angioedema, SCD), cardiovascular & renal (IgA nephropathy, CKD), transplant & immunology, influenza vaccines, iron deficiency/nephrology (CSL Vifor)
- **Pipeline Size:** 59 clinical trials progressed in FY2025; 56 regulatory approvals (43 new registrations, 13 new indications); R&D investment of $1.4B in FY2025
- **Strategic Priorities:** Plasma collection network optimization (vertical integration); gene therapy commercialization (HEMGENIX for hemophilia B); FILSPARI (sparsentan) for IgA nephropathy; garadacimab for HAE; self-amplifying mRNA vaccine platform (sa-mRNA); R&D strategic review focusing on external partnerships; consolidation of R&D sites around six biotech hubs; CSL Vifor integration for iron deficiency/nephrology

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
CSL's R&D is led by an EVP/Head of R&D who also serves as Chief Medical Officer. R&D focuses on four strategic platforms: plasma protein technology, recombinant technology, genetic medicine, and vaccines technology. Clinical development spans five therapeutic areas: Immunoglobulins, Hematology, Cardiovascular & Renal, Transplant & Immunology, and Vaccines. The company operates across four business units (CSL Behring, CSL Plasma, CSL Seqirus, CSL Vifor), each with distinct clinical development needs. CSL Behring focuses on rare disease therapeutics, CSL Seqirus on vaccines, and CSL Vifor on iron deficiency and nephrology. The company is consolidating R&D around six global biotech hubs and increasing reliance on external partnerships following a strategic review in 2025.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Global Regulatory Affairs is led by an SVP/Head of Global Regulatory Affairs, with the function organized globally with regional leads covering North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. The regulatory function includes Global Regulatory Science & Policy, which engages with health authorities on regulatory policy. In FY2025, CSL achieved 56 regulatory approvals globally, demonstrating a high-volume regulatory operation. Key regulatory milestones include FILSPARI's conversion from conditional to standard marketing authorization in the EU, garadacimab FDA/EMA filing acceptance, and ongoing HEMGENIX post-marketing commitments. The diverse product portfolio (plasma products, recombinant proteins, gene therapies, vaccines, small molecules) requires regulatory expertise across multiple modality-specific frameworks.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Global Safety and Pharmacovigilance (GSPV) is led by an SVP and operates across all business units. CSL's PV function adheres to global good pharmacovigilance practice (GPV) and good clinical practice (GCP) standards. The PV organization includes sub-functions for pharmacovigilance & regulatory quality assurance (PVRQA) with regional leads. The plasma-derived therapy portfolio presents unique PV challenges: batch-to-batch variability, pathogen safety, and population-level safety monitoring across millions of patients receiving immunoglobulin products. The gene therapy program (HEMGENIX) requires long-term follow-up for safety (15+ years recommended by regulators). CSL Seqirus vaccine PV requires population-level safety surveillance different from individual product PV. CSL Vifor adds iron product safety monitoring in CKD patients.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is embedded within the R&D leadership structure, with the CMO/Head of R&D providing oversight. B/R decisions are particularly complex for CSL given the modality diversity: plasma-derived products have population-level B/R considerations (batch safety), gene therapies have long-term B/R unknowns (durability, integration risks), and vaccines have population-level public health B/R frameworks. The strategic R&D review in 2025, which resulted in consolidation and increased external partnerships, was itself a portfolio-level B/R decision about where to invest limited R&D resources.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should position around CSL's unique modality diversity challenge. No other company in this cohort operates across plasma-derived therapies, recombinant proteins, gene therapies, vaccines, and small molecules simultaneously. Each modality has fundamentally different B/R frameworks -- batch-level safety for plasma products, long-term integration risk for gene therapies, population-level surveillance for vaccines, individual patient risk for small molecules. ArcaScience can position as the unifying B/R governance platform that brings consistency to B/R decision-making across modalities while accommodating their distinct requirements. The gene therapy angle is particularly compelling: HEMGENIX requires 15+ years of safety follow-up, meaning B/R assessments must be truly dynamic and long-duration. ArcaScience's continuous updating model is perfectly aligned with this need. The strategic R&D review creating a shift toward external partnerships further supports the value proposition: as CSL increasingly relies on external partners for pipeline assets, it needs a platform to rapidly evaluate and integrate B/R data from partner programs. Propose a pilot on the garadacimab HAE program (filing accepted by FDA/EMA) or the FILSPARI IgA nephropathy program, where B/R profiles are being finalized for regulatory interaction.
