# Recordati

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** >EUR 2 billion (revenue growth of +12.4% YoY); Specialty & Primary Care at EUR 1,449M, Rare Diseases at EUR 834M
- **Headquarters:** Milan, Italy
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Rare diseases (Cushing's disease, acromegaly, hyperaldosteronism), specialty & primary care (urology, cardiovascular, GI), with key products including Isturisa (osilodrostat), Signifor (pasireotide), and dinutuximab beta for neuroblastoma
- **Pipeline Size:** Active pipeline spanning endocrinology, metabolic disease, nervous system diseases, neoplasms, immunology; R&D investment of EUR 286M in 2024 (+11.8% YoY); products originated internally and through development agreements
- **Strategic Priorities:** Rare disease franchise expansion (peak year sales targets raised for Isturisa and Signifor), specialty care portfolio growth, strategic acquisitions and licensing, ESG leadership (MSCI A rating, EcoVadis Gold), sustainable profitable growth, geographic expansion across EMEA, Americas, and APAC

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Recordati's clinical development organization combines internal R&D with extensive licensing and acquisition of products from other pharmaceutical companies and research institutes. The company's development of new specialty products is originated either internally or acquired through development agreements. The rare diseases division (Recordati Rare Diseases) operates as a distinct organizational entity with dedicated clinical development capabilities. Key clinical programs include long-term studies for Isturisa (LINC 3 study in Cushing's disease) and ongoing development of the rare disease portfolio.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Recordati operates across approximately 150 countries, requiring a robust global regulatory function. Regulatory strategy spans specialty/primary care products (managed through regional structures in EMEA, Americas, APAC) and rare disease products (managed through Recordati Rare Diseases with orphan drug designations and rare disease regulatory pathways). The company manages regulatory filings across EU (EMA), US (FDA), and other major markets.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV/drug safety spans a diverse portfolio of rare disease and specialty products. Rare disease PV is particularly important given small patient populations and long-term treatment requirements (e.g., Isturisa for Cushing's disease demonstrated long-term cortisol normalization and tolerability in the LINC 3 study). The company's global footprint across 150 countries creates extensive post-marketing surveillance obligations. Products like dinutuximab beta (neuroblastoma) require oncology-specific PV capabilities.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance operates at both the business unit level (Specialty & Primary Care, Rare Diseases) and at the corporate level. For rare diseases, B/R assessment is critical given small patient populations, high unmet need, and the requirement to demonstrate favorable risk profiles for chronic therapies. The company's acquisition strategy (bringing in external products) requires rigorous B/R evaluation during due diligence and portfolio integration.
