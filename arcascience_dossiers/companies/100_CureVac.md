# CureVac

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 535M (~$590M; however, primarily driven by GSK licensing restructuring payment of EUR 400M upfront + milestones; underlying operational revenue is minimal as the company has no commercial products)
- **Headquarters:** Tubingen, Baden-Wurttemberg, Germany (sites also in Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland, US)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** mRNA therapeutics — oncology precision immunotherapies (glioblastoma/CVGBM, squamous NSCLC/CVHNLC, personalized immunotherapies), infectious disease (urinary tract infections/UTI), mRNA technology platform licensing (GSK vaccines: influenza, COVID-19, combinations)
- **Pipeline Size:** Oncology: CVGBM (Phase 1b dose expansion, glioblastoma), CVHNLC (Phase 1, squamous NSCLC — FDA IND cleared April 2025), personalized immunotherapy (Phase 1 expected H2 2026); additional clinical candidate selection expected 2026; infectious disease: UTI program in discovery; all vaccine candidates transferred to GSK under 2024 restructured license agreement; cash runway extended to 2028
- **Strategic Priorities:** Strategic restructuring — ~30% workforce reduction to focus on high-value mRNA oncology programs; GSK licensing deal (EUR 400M upfront + up to EUR 1.05B milestones + royalties); operational expense reduction >30% from 2025; mRNA technology platform advancement; IP assertion against Pfizer/BioNTech; precision immunotherapy leadership (shared-antigen and personalized approaches); cash runway to 2028

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Following the 2024 strategic restructuring (~30% workforce reduction), CureVac's clinical development is now focused exclusively on mRNA oncology precision immunotherapies and select infectious disease programs. The vaccine pipeline (influenza, COVID-19) has been fully transferred to GSK. Clinical development is organized around two oncology approaches: (1) off-the-shelf precision immunotherapies targeting tumor antigens shared across patient populations (CVGBM for glioblastoma, CVHNLC for squamous NSCLC) and (2) fully personalized precision immunotherapies based on individual tumor genomic profiles. The restructured organization is designed to be "leaner and more agile" with reduced headcount focused on technology innovation and R&D.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages FDA and EMA interactions for the mRNA oncology pipeline. IND submissions have been filed for CVGBM (Phase 1b active) and CVHNLC (FDA cleared April 2025). The team has experience with both US and European regulatory pathways. The personalized immunotherapy program will require novel regulatory approaches for individualized mRNA therapeutics. GSK manages all regulatory activities for the transferred vaccine programs.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV is focused on clinical trial safety monitoring for early-stage oncology programs. CVGBM Phase 1b data showed the treatment was well tolerated with no dose-limiting toxicities up to 100mcg; 91% of treatment-related adverse events were mild to moderate. The mRNA platform's characteristic systemic reactions (resolving within 1-2 days post-injection) require mRNA-specific safety monitoring expertise. No marketed products means no commercial PV obligations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is critical for a restructured, resource-constrained organization with a limited number of high-stakes clinical programs. Each program decision (CVGBM advancement to Phase 2, CVHNLC initiation, personalized immunotherapy Phase 1 timing) represents a significant resource allocation from a reduced operational budget. The decision to close vaccine development and focus on oncology was itself a major B/R-driven strategic pivot.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** CMO or Head of Clinical Development — driven by the need to maximize the probability of success for a small number of high-stakes oncology programs post-restructuring
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO and CSO
- **Budget Owner:** CEO or CFO — EUR 481M cash position but must extend runway to 2028; 30%+ operational expense reduction limits discretionary spending
- **Procurement Cycle:** 3-6 months; small biotech with direct C-suite access; restructured organization may have streamlined decision-making
- **Internal Friction Points:** Severe resource constraints post-restructuring (~30% workforce reduction); very early-stage pipeline (Phase 1) may not seem to need sophisticated B/R tools yet; GSK vaccine program transfer means reduced B/R governance scope; novel mRNA modality may require platform adaptation; IP litigation focus (Pfizer/BioNTech) may divert management attention; company may be acquired before tool implementation delivers value

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
CureVac's post-restructuring focus on mRNA precision immunotherapy — a novel modality with limited clinical precedent — creates a unique B/R assessment challenge that ArcaScience can address. The BRA platform should be positioned for: (1) CVGBM Phase 2 go/no-go decision support — the dose expansion data (100mcg recommended dose, favorable safety) will need integrated B/R assessment combining safety, immunogenicity, and preliminary efficacy signals to inform the most critical near-term decision, (2) CVHNLC program optimization — as a multi-epitope mRNA immunotherapy encoding 8 tumor-associated antigens, the B/R complexity of this program benefits from systematic assessment framework, and (3) personalized immunotherapy regulatory strategy — the fully personalized approach (Phase 1 expected H2 2026) will require novel B/R frameworks that regulators have not yet established, giving CureVac an opportunity to lead with a structured approach that facilitates regulatory dialogue. Given severe resource constraints, a risk-sharing commercial model (milestone-based or equity-based) would be more appropriate than upfront licensing. The GSK relationship provides a potential indirect pathway — if ArcaScience supports CureVac's programs well, GSK (which is evaluating the transferred vaccine programs under the same mRNA backbone) may take notice. The pitch should emphasize that for a resource-constrained company making binary program decisions, the value of improved B/R assessment accuracy is proportionally much higher than for large pharma.
