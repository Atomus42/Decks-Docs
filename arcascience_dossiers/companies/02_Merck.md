## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Merck Research Laboratories (MRL), led by Dean Y. Li (EVP and President), oversees all worldwide human vaccines and therapeutics R&D with R&D spend exceeding $13B annually. In March 2026, Merck restructured its Human Health organization into two dedicated business units: an **Oncology Business Unit** (led by Jannie Oosthuizen, EVP and President, Oncology and MSD International) and a **Specialty, Pharma & Infectious Diseases Business Unit**. This restructuring creates separate commercial and operational pathways -- critically, each business unit may develop independent procurement processes, vendor relationships, and technology adoption timelines. The restructuring is designed to sustain long-term oncology leadership while maintaining focus across a growing number of launches in a diversifying portfolio. Clinical development is organized by therapeutic area within MRL, with cross-functional integration spanning data science, translational medicine, and clinical operations. The **GPTeal** generative AI platform has expanded far beyond initial use cases: over 50,000 employees (roughly two-thirds of Merck's workforce) use it monthly across R&D, clinical, regulatory, and corporate functions, with access to ChatGPT, Llama, and Claude models via a secure, encrypted architecture. In partnership with McKinsey's QuantumBlack, Merck deployed AI-generated clinical study reports (CSRs) in live regulatory submissions, reducing drafting time from 2-3 weeks to 3-4 days and halving error rates in data accuracy, citations, and terminology. A team of 80+ specialists across three continents produced the first AI-generated CSRs submitted to regulators. Merck has over 1,600 clinical trials studying KEYTRUDA alone, with extensive Phase III programs across cardiovascular (oral PCSK9 inhibitor enlicitide, CORALreef program with 19,000+ participants, FDA submission expected April 2026), hematology (nemtabrutinib in two Phase III CLL/SLL trials, bomedemstat in Phase III for essential thrombocythemia), oncology next-generation modalities (MK-2870 anti-TROP2 ADC, Daiichi Sankyo ADC partnership, Merck-Moderna personalized cancer vaccines), and infectious diseases (CD388 long-acting influenza antiviral from Cidara acquisition).

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Merck's **Global Regulatory Affairs & Clinical Safety (GRACS)** is a unified function combining regulatory strategy, execution, and compliance across three key therapeutic groupings: **Vaccines & Infectious Diseases**, **Oncology & in Vitro Diagnostics**, and **General Medicine & U.S. Subsidiary**. GRACS supports products from early pipeline development through divestiture or market withdrawal (spanning 10 to 100+ years per product) and is dedicated to optimizing global access through cutting-edge regulatory strategies. The function integrates clinical safety within the same organizational structure, enabling tight coordination between regulatory submissions and safety data. GRACS also encompasses global regulatory compliance and quality assurance.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Merck's **Global Clinical Safety & Pharmacovigilance (GCS&PV)** function operates within GRACS, unifying safety monitoring with regulatory affairs. GCS&PV is responsible for proactive safety assessment, effective risk management, and transparent risk communication across the full product lifecycle. The function aims to improve public health by assuring the safe use of products worldwide. Signal detection, periodic safety reporting, and risk management plans are managed within this integrated GRACS structure, with therapeutic area specialists aligned to the three GRACS groupings. The safety monitoring burden is intensifying across multiple dimensions: (1) **KEYTRUDA combination complexity** -- with 1,600+ active trials spanning dozens of combination partners across different tumor types, each combination generates distinct safety profiles requiring continuous, differentiated B/R assessment at unprecedented scale; (2) **WINREVAIR cardiovascular safety** -- sotatercept's novel mechanism of action (activin signaling trap) in the PAH population requires careful cardiovascular safety monitoring, including effects on hemoglobin/erythropoiesis and potential vascular events in a fragile patient population already on multiple background therapies; (3) **New modality safety profiles** -- Merck's expanding pipeline of ADCs (MK-2870 anti-TROP2, Daiichi Sankyo partnership), T-cell engagers (Harpoon acquisition), and mRNA-based personalized cancer vaccines (Moderna partnership) each introduce novel safety considerations (e.g., cytokine release syndrome, payload toxicity, autoimmune reactions) that differ fundamentally from traditional small molecule and monoclonal antibody pharmacovigilance.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk decisions at Merck are embedded within the integrated GRACS structure, where clinical safety assessment and regulatory strategy are jointly managed. Safety review boards operate at the therapeutic area level, with escalation to senior R&D leadership within MRL. The integrated GRACS model ensures that B/R assessments are directly linked to regulatory submissions and lifecycle management decisions. Enterprise-level risk governance flows through the Board's Audit Committee and Research Committee, which oversees R&D strategy and pipeline risk management. The March 2026 business unit restructuring adds an organizational layer to B/R governance: the dedicated Oncology Business Unit and Specialty/Pharma/ID Business Unit will each develop their own operational cadence, potentially creating two distinct B/R decision-making pathways that still roll up through the unified GRACS structure. This dual-track model increases the surface area for BRA platform adoption, as each business unit may independently evaluate and procure safety analytics tools tailored to its portfolio. The expansion of GPTeal into regulatory document generation also creates a natural integration point: AI-generated CSRs and regulatory submissions require high-quality, structured B/R analytics as input -- a gap that GPTeal does not itself fill.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Enter through the new Oncology Business Unit, positioning around the Keytruda diversification imperative and the B/R complexity of next-generation modalities.**

Merck's defining strategic challenge -- the Keytruda patent cliff (late 2028) -- has become even more urgent. KEYTRUDA generated $31.7B in FY2025 (49% of total revenue), and with biosimilar developers (Samsung Bioepis, Amgen, Indian manufacturers) advancing programs, analysts model 15-30% EU price erosion by Year 3 post-LOE. Keytruda QLEX (SC formulation, approved Sept 2025) extends patent protection to 2030+ but Merck is targeting only 30-40% SC adoption by 2028, meaning substantial IV revenue remains exposed. With 1,600+ trials and dozens of combination studies, the KEYTRUDA franchise generates immense B/R complexity -- different combination partners, different tumor types, different safety profiles. A dynamic BRA platform that can continuously update benefit-risk across this sprawling program would be immediately valuable. The narrative should emphasize:

1. **Scale of oncology B/R management at an inflection point** -- No other company manages as many concurrent combination trials for a single franchise; ArcaScience can provide a unified, probabilistic view across all KEYTRUDA combinations and the expanding pipeline of next-generation oncology modalities. The March 2026 Oncology Business Unit creates a dedicated organizational home for this capability, potentially with faster procurement than enterprise-wide MRL channels. ADCs (MK-2870 anti-TROP2, Daiichi Sankyo $22B partnership), T-cell engagers (Harpoon acquisition), and mRNA-based personalized cancer vaccines (Moderna collaboration, Phase 3 melanoma data expected late 2026) each introduce novel safety profiles -- cytokine release syndrome, payload toxicity, autoimmune reactions -- that demand new B/R frameworks beyond traditional monoclonal antibody pharmacovigilance.
2. **Regulatory acceleration during the diversification sprint** -- With 20+ potential blockbusters in development, line of sight to $70B+ commercial opportunity by mid-2030s, and 80+ Phase III studies, Merck needs tools that accelerate B/R documentation for regulatory submissions, particularly in therapeutic areas where GRACS may have less institutional B/R expertise (e.g., cardiovascular with enlicitide filing in April 2026, WINREVAIR cardiovascular safety monitoring in PAH, HIV with MK-8527). The enlicitide submission timeline creates a near-term, concrete use case: a first-in-class oral PCSK9i with a 19,000-patient Phase III program generating massive B/R data that must be structured for a potentially expedited FDA review (National Priority Voucher candidate).
3. **Complement, not compete, with GPTeal** -- This positioning is now even more critical given GPTeal's expansion to 50,000+ monthly users and the McKinsey/QuantumBlack AI-CSR partnership producing live regulatory submissions. ArcaScience occupies a distinct layer in the analytics stack: GPTeal generates documents, but it does not perform the underlying benefit-risk analysis. ArcaScience provides the structured, quantitative B/R analytics that feed into GPTeal-generated CSRs, PBRERs, DSURs, and RMPs. This complementary positioning avoids triggering the "not invented here" resistance that Merck's $1.2B+ AI investment and Code of Digital Ethics framework might otherwise provoke.

A secondary entry through the **Specialty/Pharma/ID Business Unit** is now equally compelling, given: (a) WINREVAIR's rapid trajectory ($1.4B FY2025, $360M+/quarter run rate in early 2026, on path to $2-5B annually by 2030) requiring continuous cardiovascular safety B/R assessment in a fragile PAH population; (b) enlicitide's imminent FDA submission creating an urgent B/R documentation need; (c) CD388 (from the $9.2B Cidara acquisition) introducing a novel long-acting influenza antiviral modality with its own safety characterization requirements; and (d) the HIV pipeline (MK-8527 monthly oral PrEP) and CAPVAXIVE ($759M FY2025) expanding the infectious disease B/R workload.
