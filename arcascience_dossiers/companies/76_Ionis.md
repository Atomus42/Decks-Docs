- **2025-2026 Key Developments:** TRYNGOLZA became the first FDA-approved FCS treatment; DAWNZERA approved in US (August 2025) and EU (January 2026) for hereditary angioedema; olezarsen sHTG supplemental NDA received FDA priority review with June 30, 2026 PDUFA date (peak sales expectations raised from >$1B to >$2B for combined TRYNGOLZA opportunity); zilganersen NDA filed January 2026 for Alexander disease (received Breakthrough Therapy designation); ION582 for Angelman syndrome received Breakthrough Therapy designation; sapablursen for polycythemia vera received Breakthrough Therapy designation (May 2025); 2026 revenue guidance of $800-825M (~20% YoY growth)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Ionis has completed its evolution from a discovery-focused biotech into a fully integrated commercial-stage organization, with 2025 marking the "defining year" of this transition. Clinical development is organized by therapeutic area (neurology, cardiology, rare disease, hematology) with dedicated program teams for each clinical asset. The company manages both independent programs (where Ionis retains full development and commercial rights) and partnered programs (with Biogen, AstraZeneca, GSK, Novartis, Roche). Independent programs are prioritized with internal clinical operations teams, while partnered programs are co-managed under collaboration agreements. The company has built out a full US commercial field team of approximately 200 representatives to support independent launches. In 2026, the clinical development organization is managing an unprecedented volume of activity: 5 Phase 3 readouts (including pelacarsen for Lp(a) cardiovascular disease and eplontersen for ATTR cardiomyopathy in H2 2026), 4 NDA submissions, and preparation for 3 product launches. Key pipeline designations secured in 2025-2026 include Breakthrough Therapy designations for zilganersen (Alexander disease), ION582 (Angelman syndrome), and sapablursen (polycythemia vera).

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs operates as a centralized function supporting global filings, now managing an expanded portfolio of 8 marketed medicines across the US, EU, and other jurisdictions. The regulatory team has demonstrated strong execution in 2025-2026, successfully securing two independent US approvals (TRYNGOLZA for FCS, DAWNZERA for HAE), EU approval for DAWNZERA (January 2026), and FDA priority review acceptance for olezarsen sHTG (June 30, 2026 PDUFA date). The team is concurrently managing the zilganersen NDA (filed January 2026 for Alexander disease) and coordinating with GSK on worldwide bepirovirsen regulatory submissions for chronic hepatitis B in 2026. The regulatory function has accumulated significant Breakthrough Therapy designation experience, with active designations for zilganersen, ION582 (Angelman syndrome), and sapablursen (polycythemia vera, granted May 2025), alongside existing Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations across the rare disease portfolio.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
With 8 marketed products and rapidly expanding commercial operations (two new independent launches in 2025 alone, with three more anticipated in 2026), Ionis maintains a dedicated pharmacovigilance/drug safety organization under increasing demand. The PV team is responsible for post-marketing safety surveillance, safety signal detection, and aggregate safety reporting across all marketed and investigational products. The rapid pace of new product launches — TRYNGOLZA (2024), DAWNZERA (2025), and anticipated olezarsen sHTG, zilganersen, and bepirovirsen (2026) — has significantly expanded the scope and complexity of post-marketing safety monitoring. The safety team interfaces with both internal clinical operations and external partner safety organizations for co-developed products (Biogen for SPINRAZA, GSK for bepirovirsen).

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk decisions are made at the program level by development committees comprising clinical, regulatory, safety, and commercial leadership. For novel RNA-targeted therapeutics, benefit-risk assessment is especially critical given the platform nature of the technology and the rare disease patient populations. With the transition to independent commercialization now fully underway (2 independent products launched, 2+ more planned in 2026), B/R governance has expanded to encompass the full development-to-market continuum. The simultaneous management of 5 Phase 3 readouts, 4 NDA submissions, and 3 anticipated product launches in 2026 creates unprecedented demand for portfolio-level B/R decision-making across both independent and partnered programs competing for internal resources.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
Ionis is at a pivotal inflection point — transitioning from a partnered discovery company to a fully integrated commercial biotech with 4 independent launches planned over 3 years. This dramatically increases the need for unified benefit-risk governance across a complex portfolio of independent and partnered assets spanning rare diseases, neurology, and cardiology. ArcaScience should position the BRA platform as a portfolio-level decision support tool that helps Ionis: (1) optimize go/no-go decisions across 40+ programs competing for internal resources, (2) support regulatory strategy for simultaneous filings across FDA, EMA, and other agencies, and (3) integrate post-marketing safety data from newly launched products back into the development B/R framework. A compelling pilot would focus on the neurology pipeline cluster (10+ programs) where probabilistic approval prediction and dynamic B/R profiling can demonstrate immediate value in prioritizing the next wave of independent programs. The platform's ability to synthesize clinical trial data with emerging real-world evidence from SPINRAZA and WAINUA would resonate strongly with Ionis's evidence-driven culture.
