# Viatris

- **Strategic Priorities:** Enterprise-wide strategic review (EWSR) completed February 2026 -- restructuring with up to 10% global workforce reduction, $600-700M annual savings when fully implemented, pre-tax charges of $700-850M; $650M total cost savings 2026-2029 with $400M net savings after reinvestment; ophthalmology franchise leadership (Oyster Point/Famy Life Sciences, targeting $1B eye care sales by 2028 with MR-141, MR-142, MR-146 pipeline); complex generics/injectable expansion ($450-550M new product revenue in 2025 and 2026); Indore facility FDA remediation substantially complete as of November 2025, reinspection pending; total debt reduced to $12.5B (from $20B at inception); >$1B returned to shareholders in 2025; >$2.5B cash available for deployment in 2026; ~2% revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth expected in 2026

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Viatris's clinical development has undergone significant evolution since the 2020 Mylan-Upjohn merger, and is now entering a new phase following the February 2026 enterprise-wide strategic review (EWSR) that commits to optimizing R&D, medical affairs, and regulatory activities. The company operates development programs across three distinct tracks: (1) innovative specialty brands dominated by ophthalmology (MR-141 for presbyopia with sNDA accepted and October 2026 PDUFA; MR-142 for mesopic vision with FDA Fast Track and positive LYNX-2 Phase 3 results; MR-146 gene therapy with CORVITA Phase I/II trial initiating H1 2026), plus dermatology, GI, CNS (pitolisant J-NDA accepted in Japan), and women's health (low-dose estrogen weekly contraceptive patch NDA accepted, July 2026 PDUFA); (2) complex generics and injectable products, where 2025 saw four FDA injectable approvals (iron sucrose, paclitaxel, liposomal amphotericin B, octreotide acetate) and new product revenue of $450-550M; and (3) the Botox biosimilar program (clinical study initiated H1 2024, partnered with Revance Therapeutics, potential launch 2026). Note that the Phase 3 trial of MR-139 (pimecrolimus 0.3%) for blepharitis did not meet its primary endpoint, and Viatris is evaluating next steps. Clinical development operates within a global matrix structure organized by geography and product type, supported by approximately 40 global manufacturing sites.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs at Viatris manages an extremely high-volume operation reflecting the company's massive generics, biosimilar, and specialty portfolio across 165+ countries. The regulatory function handles ANDA filings, 505(b)(2) applications, NDAs, BLAs, and international submissions across all major markets. Key 2025-2026 regulatory milestones include: four injectable FDA approvals in 2025 (iron sucrose, paclitaxel, liposomal amphotericin B, octreotide acetate); FDA acceptance of MR-141 sNDA for presbyopia (PDUFA October 17, 2026); FDA Fast Track designation for MR-142 for chronic night driving impairment; IND clearance for MR-146 gene therapy; FDA acceptance of low-dose estrogen weekly contraceptive patch NDA (PDUFA July 30, 2026); PMDA J-NDA acceptance for pitolisant in Japan. The Indore facility remediation is substantially complete as of November 2025, with the company having met with FDA to discuss next steps toward reinspection. The 2025 financial impact of the Indore warning letter and import alert was ~$500M to total revenues and $385M to adjusted EBITDA (lenalidomide representing ~40% of the revenue impact); however, less than 1% of Indore recovery is included in 2026 guidance, suggesting the company has structurally adjusted around the impact. The EWSR restructuring will optimize regulatory activities as part of broader organizational streamlining.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV at Viatris manages safety across one of the broadest product portfolios in the industry, serving ~1 billion patients annually across 165+ countries. The PV function covers generics, complex generics, biosimilars, and branded specialty products. Unique PV challenges include: monitoring safety across extremely high-volume generic products where manufacturing quality is the primary safety variable (the Indore incident demonstrated consequences of quality lapses, with $500M+ in 2025 revenue impact); the expanding injectable generics portfolio (octreotide, iron sucrose, paclitaxel, liposomal amphotericin B) introducing parenteral-specific safety monitoring requirements; specialty product PV for the growing ophthalmology franchise (MR-141 for presbyopia approaching PDUFA, MR-142 for mesopic vision in Phase 3); gene therapy safety monitoring for MR-146 (CORVITA trial initiating H1 2026, requiring long-duration AAV-based gene therapy PV protocols); and the Botox biosimilar program introducing neurotoxin-specific safety comparability requirements. The Indore facility remediation being substantially complete as of November 2025 reflects extensive corrective and preventive actions including engagement of independent third-party subject matter experts. The EWSR restructuring will optimize PV alongside medical affairs and regulatory activities.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Viatris operates at the intersection of two models: (1) the generics model, where B/R focuses on therapeutic equivalence and manufacturing quality; and (2) the emerging specialty/innovative model, where traditional B/R assessment applies to novel assets. The enterprise-wide strategic review in 2025 likely included portfolio-level B/R decisions about which assets to advance and which to deprioritize. The 11 Phase 3 programs represent a significant increase in innovative B/R governance requirements for a company that historically was predominantly generics-focused.

ArcaScience should position around Viatris's transformation from a generics giant to a specialty pharmaceutical company. The pitch: Viatris is investing in 11 Phase 3 programs and a gene therapy candidate, but its B/R governance infrastructure was built for a generics company. The ophthalmology franchise strategy ($1B target by 2028) is the most specific entry point: the MR-146 gene therapy for neurotrophic keratopathy requires B/R assessment capabilities that Viatris has never needed before, making an external platform essential rather than optional.
