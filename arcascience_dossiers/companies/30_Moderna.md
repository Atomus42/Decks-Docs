# Moderna

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** ~$3.0-3.1B (product sales; primarily Spikevax COVID-19 vaccine; ending cash balance ~$9.5B)
- **Headquarters:** Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** mRNA vaccines (COVID-19, RSV, flu, norovirus), immuno-oncology (individualized neoantigen therapy), rare diseases (propionic acidemia, MMA)
- **Pipeline Size:** Largest late-stage mRNA pipeline globally; ~69% probability of success for mid/late-stage programs vs. ~19% industry average; multiple Phase 2 and Phase 3 programs across infectious disease, oncology, and rare disease
- **Strategic Priorities:** Building seasonal vaccine franchise (Spikevax, mRESVIA, flu/COVID combo); pivoting investment into oncology and rare disease; aggressive cost reduction ($1B savings in 2025, additional $500M in 2026); path to cash breakeven by 2028; manufacturing gross margin improvement of 10+ percentage points over three years

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Moderna's clinical development is organized around its mRNA platform with therapeutic area teams spanning infectious disease vaccines, oncology/immuno-oncology, and rare diseases. The company has a highly centralized R&D model given its platform-driven approach — all programs share core mRNA technology. Clinical development for the INT (individualized neoantigen therapy, mRNA-4157) program is co-managed with Merck. The oncology pipeline includes nine ongoing Phase 2 and Phase 3 studies. Rare disease programs (PA, MMA) are entering registrational stages.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages global submissions and interactions with FDA, EMA, and other agencies. The team has significant experience from the rapid COVID-19 vaccine approval process and is now applying this expertise to RSV, flu, and combination vaccine submissions. The FDA review pathway (including priority review voucher use) and regulatory strategy for novel mRNA modalities in oncology and rare disease require specialized regulatory approaches.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV has scaled dramatically given the massive global deployment of Spikevax (billions of doses administered). The pharmacovigilance infrastructure handles an enormous volume of safety reports. As Moderna expands into oncology and rare disease, PV must adapt to very different safety profiles — immunotherapy-related adverse events in oncology and long-term safety monitoring for rare disease replacement therapies.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance operates at the program level and corporate level. Given the platform nature of Moderna's approach, there are cross-program learnings about mRNA safety that inform B/R decisions. The Merck partnership for INT (mRNA-4157) involves joint B/R governance. Corporate-level B/R decisions are particularly important as Moderna prioritizes and deprioritizes programs as part of its cost reduction and R&D prioritization strategy.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Head of Oncology Development or Head of Regulatory Strategy — as novel mRNA modalities in oncology require rigorous, evolving B/R frameworks that regulators will scrutinize
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO / Head of Clinical Development — must validate that the BRA platform can handle the unique B/R considerations of mRNA therapeutics
- **Budget Owner:** CMO or CFO — despite cost reduction focus, Moderna is increasing oncology R&D allocation; the $9.5B cash position provides investment capacity
- **Procurement Cycle:** 6-9 months; Moderna is a relatively lean, fast-moving organization with Silicon Valley-influenced decision-making; less bureaucratic than traditional pharma
- **Internal Friction Points:** Aggressive cost reduction program ($1B in 2025) creates scrutiny on all new spending; platform-centric culture may view external tools as unnecessary given internal capabilities; Merck partnership for INT complicates procurement for oncology programs; revenue decline and net losses in 2024 create cautious spending environment

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Moderna's oncology pipeline, specifically the individualized neoantigen therapy (INT/mRNA-4157) program and the expanding Phase 3 oncology portfolio. The narrative should focus on: (1) how dynamic B/R assessment is uniquely valuable for mRNA cancer vaccines — a completely novel modality where regulators lack historical precedent and will demand rigorous, continuously updated B/R evidence, (2) integrating clinical trial data with emerging real-world evidence as oncology programs move from adjuvant melanoma into multiple new tumor types, and (3) supporting Moderna's R&D prioritization strategy by providing probabilistic approval prediction that helps allocate resources across nine ongoing Phase 2/3 oncology studies. The cost reduction imperative can be turned into an advantage by positioning BRA as a tool that reduces clinical attrition (Moderna already boasts 69% mid/late-stage success vs. 19% industry average) and accelerates decision-making. The approach should align with Moderna's tech-forward culture — position ArcaScience as a platform company speaking the same language as a platform company.
