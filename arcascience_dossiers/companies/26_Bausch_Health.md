## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Bausch Health's clinical development is organized around its five business segments — Salix (GI), Bausch + Lomb (ophthalmology), Solta Medical (aesthetics), Diversified (neurology/dermatology/generics/dentistry), and International. The CMO and Head of R&D (joined December 2024) oversees clinical programs across these segments. The Salix division runs the largest clinical programs, including the RED-C Phase 3 trials for rifaximin SSD in hepatic encephalopathy (topline results expected early 2026). The DURECT acquisition (completed September 2025) added larsucosterol for alcohol-associated hepatitis, with Phase 3 first patient randomized in January 2026 — a registrational, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with 90-day survival as primary endpoint. Bausch + Lomb also expanded its pipeline through the January 2025 acquisition of Whitecap Biosciences (glaucoma and geographic atrophy candidates). The company operates across 398 clinical trial sites in 17 countries for its flagship programs.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs is centralized under the CMO/Head of R&D, with separate regulatory teams supporting each business segment. Bausch + Lomb has its own regulatory function given its scale and the pending potential separation. International regulatory is managed regionally for operations across 90+ countries.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Pharmacovigilance operates as a centralized corporate function under the CMO's organization. Given the breadth of the marketed portfolio (spanning GI, ophthalmology, dermatology, neuroscience, and generics), PV manages safety across both innovative and mature product lines. Post-marketing surveillance is particularly important for Salix products like Xifaxan (rifaximin) with large patient populations. The larsucosterol program adds new PV complexity: the Phase 2b AHFIRM trial results (published in NEJM Evidence, January 2025) require careful safety monitoring in a severe patient population (alcohol-associated hepatitis, ~30% 90-day mortality), and the Phase 3 program under Breakthrough Therapy Designation demands rigorous pharmacovigilance infrastructure. The hepatology PV scope has expanded significantly with two major late-stage programs (rifaximin SSD + larsucosterol) targeting overlapping liver disease populations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk decisions are made at the executive and R&D leadership level, with the CMO/Head of R&D playing a central role. For major pipeline decisions (e.g., RED-C program go/no-go, larsucosterol Phase 3 design), the executive leadership team including the CEO and CFO are involved. The DURECT acquisition introduced an asset with FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation, which involves more intensive FDA interaction and real-time B/R dialogue under the BTD framework. The potential Bausch + Lomb separation adds complexity, as B/R governance may need to be duplicated across two entities. The expanded board (now 12 members, including former Viatris CEO and former BMS General Counsel) strengthens governance oversight of B/R decisions at the corporate level.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Bausch Health's expanding hepatology portfolio, which now includes three major programs: (1) the RED-C Phase 3 program for rifaximin SSD (1,000+ patient, 17-country pivotal program, topline results expected early 2026), (2) the larsucosterol Phase 3 program for alcohol-associated hepatitis (FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation, first patient randomized January 2026), and (3) the existing Xifaxan franchise (16% revenue growth in Q3 2025). The DURECT acquisition creates a compelling dual-program entry point — ArcaScience can position the BRA platform as essential infrastructure for managing B/R across two concurrent late-stage hepatology programs in overlapping liver disease populations. The larsucosterol Breakthrough Therapy Designation is particularly valuable, as BTD requires more intensive FDA interaction and real-time B/R communication where dynamic assessment tools add direct regulatory value. The entry narrative should focus on: (1) reducing regulatory risk for a multi-billion-dollar hepatology franchise, (2) integrating real-world evidence from the large existing Xifaxan patient base with clinical trial data for a continuous B/R profile, (3) supporting the larsucosterol BTD regulatory dialogue with probabilistic approval modeling, and (4) enabling the dual regulatory pathway (FDA + international agencies) across multiple concurrent programs. The return to profitability (FY2025 net income $157M) and growing revenue ($10.27B) indicate improving financial capacity for platform investments. The expanded board with pharma-experienced directors (ex-Viatris CEO, ex-BMS General Counsel) and the CMO's now-established leadership create a favorable decision-making environment. ArcaScience should also highlight how the BRA platform could serve both Bausch Health and a potentially independent Bausch + Lomb, positioning it as infrastructure that adds value regardless of corporate structure changes.
