## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Idorsia was founded with deep drug discovery heritage (25 years), emerging from Actelion. Clinical development covers a diversified portfolio across CNS, cardiovascular, immunology, rare diseases, and now infectious disease/dermatology. The organization was significantly reduced following the July 2023 cost reduction initiative that made up to 500 positions redundant, primarily in R&D and support functions. However, the company is reinvesting in clinical development: three proof-of-concept trials are planned for 2026, including the CXCR7 antagonist in multiple sclerosis (Q1 2026), and studies in psoriasis and C. difficile infection. The pediatric insomnia Phase 2 study for daridorexant — the only dual orexin receptor antagonist in development for children — has results expected Q2 2026. The lucerastat program has achieved FDA alignment on a streamlined registrational strategy comprising two complementary Phase 3 trials focusing on renal pathology outcomes. The Viatris partnership terms were restructured in February 2025, with Idorsia reducing its development cost contributions by USD 100M in exchange for reduced future milestones and expanded Viatris territorial rights for cenerimod.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs has delivered multiple approvals: QUVIVIQ in EU and other markets, TRYVIO in US, JERAYGO in EU (June 2024), UK (January 2025), Switzerland, and Canada — completing a four-market approval sweep for aprocitentan in 2025. The team is now managing the FDA-agreed registrational program for lucerastat in Fabry disease, which includes two complementary trials: a study designed to show decreased renal Gb3 burden after 18 months, and a comparative study (n~74) assessing lucerastat versus enzyme replacement therapies (agalsidase beta, pegunigalsidase alfa). Lucerastat holds orphan drug designation in both the US and EU. Regulatory filing for lucerastat is targeted as early as 2029. The team also manages partner-supported regulatory processes (Viatris for selatogrel/cenerimod) and upcoming IND/CTA filings for new proof-of-concept programs in MS, psoriasis, vitiligo, and C. difficile. Regulatory expertise now spans CNS, cardiovascular, rare disease, immunology, dermatology, and infectious disease pathways.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV manages post-marketing surveillance for QUVIVIQ and TRYVIO/JERAYGO across an expanding geographic footprint (EU, UK, US, Switzerland, Canada). The dual-orexin receptor antagonist (QUVIVIQ) and endothelin receptor antagonist (aprocitentan) mechanisms require specialized safety monitoring. QUVIVIQ sales more than doubling in 2025 significantly increases the volume of post-marketing safety data. The planned entry into pediatric insomnia (Phase 2 results Q2 2026) will add pediatric PV complexity. Robust on-market experience among leading experts has confirmed the aprocitentan tolerability profile across diverse patient groups, with "PRECISION-like" double-digit blood pressure reductions. The lucerastat registrational program will generate new safety data requiring monitoring across rare disease populations. The cost reduction has streamlined PV operations, but growing product sales and pipeline expansion are likely rebuilding PV demands.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is especially critical for Idorsia as the company transitions from survival mode to growth: FY2025 revenue nearly doubled to CHF 221M and operating losses narrowed 67%, but the company still has negative EBIT (guided -CHF 120M non-GAAP in 2026). Every pipeline investment must demonstrate clear B/R value. The decision to invest in three new proof-of-concept trials in 2026 (MS, psoriasis, C. difficile) alongside the lucerastat registrational program reflects an active B/R-driven portfolio expansion strategy. The Viatris partnership restructuring (reduced milestones for reduced development costs) demonstrates B/R trade-off discipline. The lucerastat regulatory strategy — using renal Gb3 biomarkers as a primary endpoint with a head-to-head comparison against established ERTs — is a sophisticated B/R-informed registration approach.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
Idorsia is a constrained-budget account where the value proposition must be extremely sharp: demonstrating that ArcaScience's BRA platform directly increases the out-licensing value of pipeline assets or reduces the development cost of reaching the next inflection point. ArcaScience should position specifically for: (1) out-licensing deal support — providing B/R data packages that increase the perceived value and de-risk perception of pipeline assets offered to potential partners, (2) lucerastat regulatory pathway optimization — the Fabry disease program has complex regulatory considerations where B/R-informed strategy could accelerate the path to approval, and (3) aprocitentan lifecycle management — supporting QUVIVIQ and TRYVIO/JERAYGO market expansion with continuous B/R monitoring. Given severe financial constraints, a risk-sharing commercial model (e.g., milestone-based payment tied to successful out-licensing deals or regulatory approvals) would be more feasible than upfront licensing. The most realistic path is through the partnering/BD team, positioning the BRA output as a component of out-licensing data packages.
