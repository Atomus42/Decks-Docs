# Kyowa Kirin

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** ~$3.2B (gross margin improved to 74%; steady growth from Crysvita and Poteligeo)
- **Headquarters:** Tokyo, Japan (subsidiary of Kirin Holdings Co., Ltd.)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Oncology (AML, CTCL), nephrology (XLH, achondroplasia), central nervous system (Parkinson's disease), immunology/allergy (atopic dermatitis), rare disease (MLD, TK2 deficiency, Hurler syndrome)
- **Pipeline Size:** 15+ clinical-stage programs; key assets include ziftomenib/KOMZIFTI (FDA-approved 2025 for NPM1-mutant AML via Kura Oncology partnership), rocatinlimab/KHK4083 (Phase III for AD via Amgen partnership), OTL-200 (cell/gene therapy for MLD), Crysvita lifecycle expansion
- **Strategic Priorities:** Global Specialty Pharmaceutical Company transformation; advanced antibody technology leadership; cell and gene therapy capabilities expansion; Kura Oncology strategic partnership for hematologic oncology; Amgen collaboration for rocatinlimab; Crysvita lifecycle management; Japan-based R&D with 5 research facilities globally

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Kyowa Kirin's clinical development leverages 70+ years of drug discovery and biotechnology innovation. The organization spans oncology, nephrology, CNS, immunology/allergy, and rare disease with 5 research facilities worldwide. Clinical development is increasingly global — the rocatinlimab (ROCKET) program with Amgen conducts trials across Japan, North America, EU, UK, Middle East, Asia, and Oceania. The Kura Oncology partnership manages ziftomenib development and U.S. regulatory activities. Cell and gene therapy programs (OTL-200 for MLD) represent a new frontier. As a Kirin Holdings subsidiary, Kyowa Kirin has access to the parent company's broader life science resources and biotechnology expertise.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages PMDA (Japan), FDA (U.S.), and EMA (EU) submissions. The team recently navigated FDA approval of KOMZIFTI (ziftomenib) for NPM1-mutant AML — a complex precision medicine regulatory pathway. For Japan, OTL-200 received orphan regenerative medicine product designation for early-onset MLD. Regulatory capabilities span small molecules, biologics (antibodies), and advanced therapies (cell and gene therapy).

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV manages safety across monoclonal antibodies (Crysvita, Poteligeo), small molecules (Nourianz), and emerging cell/gene therapy modalities. The precision oncology products (KOMZIFTI targeting NPM1-mutant AML) require companion diagnostic coordination with safety monitoring. Cell and gene therapy PV (OTL-200) introduces long-term follow-up requirements that are fundamentally different from traditional drug safety monitoring.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance operates within the R&D leadership structure, with partnership governance for co-developed programs (Amgen for rocatinlimab, Kura for ziftomenib). Kirin Holdings parent company provides additional governance oversight. The transition into cell and gene therapy requires B/R frameworks that account for one-time treatment with decades of follow-up.
