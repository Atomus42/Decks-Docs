# Roche

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** CHF 60.5 billion total (~$69B); Pharmaceuticals Division CHF 43.2 billion (~$49.2B); Diagnostics Division CHF 14.5 billion (~$16.5B). **FY2025:** CHF 61.5 billion total (7% CER growth), Pharma CHF 47.7 billion (9% CER growth); core operating profit CHF 21.8 billion (13% CER growth); IFRS net income CHF 13.8 billion (up 58% vs 2024 base effects). CHF 2.2 billion restructuring charges in FY2025 reflecting portfolio prioritization and efficiency initiatives. 2026 outlook: mid-single-digit sales growth (CER), high-single-digit core EPS growth (CER).
- **Headquarters:** Basel, Switzerland
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Oncology (primary), Neuroscience, Immunology, Ophthalmology, Infectious Diseases, Cardiovascular/Renal/Metabolism (expanded emphasis with obesity market entry), Hematology
- **Pipeline Size:** ~75 projects following a major **30% pipeline pruning** announced at JP Morgan 2025 (down from 107); remaining portfolio concentrated on programs with potential to redefine standards of care. Multiple Phase III programs across oncology, neuroscience, immunology, and now obesity (CT-388 Enith1/Enith2 Phase III program launching Q1 2026).
- **Key 2025-2026 Developments:**
  - **CT-388 obesity program:** Phase II results (Jan 2026) showed 22.5% placebo-adjusted weight loss at 48 weeks (24 mg dose), competitive with Eli Lilly's Zepbound; Phase III (Enith1/Enith2) launching Q1 2026
  - **Zealand Pharma deal:** $5.3B collaboration/licensing agreement (Mar 2025) for petrelintide (amylin analog) as standalone and fixed-dose combination with CT-388; co-development and co-commercialization in US/EU; target market launch ~2030
  - **30% pipeline cut:** Deliberate pruning of underperforming programs to sharpen portfolio governance, tighter clinical endpoints, and earlier termination of undifferentiated programs; R&D spend decreased 3% CER to CHF 12.2B reflecting resource optimization
  - **pRED leadership transition:** Hans Clevers retired August 2025; Mark Dawson, M.D., Ph.D. (Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre, cancer biology/epigenetics) appointed Head of pRED effective May 2026
  - **New CDTO role:** Wafaa Mamilli appointed Chief Digital & Technology Officer (Feb 2025), reporting to Group CEO, member of Enlarged Corporate Executive Committee; signals elevated strategic commitment to digital transformation and enterprise AI
  - **Flatiron Health restructuring:** Sold clinical trials business to Paradigm Health (Dec 2025); appointed new Chief Business, Technology, and Product Leaders (Feb 2026); strategic reassessment ongoing rather than full divestiture
- **Strategic Priorities:** Integrated Pharma-Diagnostics model ("The Integrated Doctrine"), AI-driven drug discovery and clinical development, end-to-end disease area organization, digital pathology expansion, personalized healthcare through companion diagnostics, 50% safety case management efficiency target by 2026, regulatory harmonization advocacy, obesity/metabolic franchise buildout via CT-388 + Zealand petrelintide combination strategy, enterprise digital transformation under new CDTO

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Roche's clinical development is organized under Dr. Levi Garraway (Head of Global Product Development and Chief Medical Officer) within an integrated structure that links early research with clinical development. The R&D engine operates through two parallel research organizations: **pRED** (Pharma Research and Early Development, Basel -- **in leadership transition**: Hans Clevers retired August 2025; Mark Dawson, M.D., Ph.D. appointed as successor effective May 2026) and **gRED** (Genentech Research and Early Development, South San Francisco, led by Dr. Aviv Regev). These organizations feed into Global Product Development, which manages clinical programs from proof-of-concept through registration. Core R&D spend decreased 3% CER to CHF 12.2 billion in FY2025, reflecting the 30% pipeline pruning and resource optimization initiatives. A new **Chief Digital & Technology Officer (CDTO)** role was created in January 2025, with Wafaa Mamilli (formerly Zoetis/Eli Lilly) appointed to the Enlarged Corporate Executive Committee, signaling that digital transformation and enterprise AI now have C-suite representation alongside traditional R&D leadership. Clinical development is increasingly organized around **end-to-end disease areas** rather than traditional phase-based structures, enabling therapeutic area teams to own programs from target identification through post-marketing. Roche advanced four potentially practice-changing therapies into Phase III in 2025: NXT007 (hemophilia A), trontinemab (Alzheimer's), prasinezumab (Parkinson's), and zosurabalpin (novel antibiotic). In Q1 2026, the CT-388 obesity Phase III program (Enith1/Enith2) launches, marking Roche's aggressive entry into the competitive obesity/metabolic market.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Roche maintains a global regulatory affairs function that supports both Pharmaceuticals and Diagnostics divisions. The company achieved 56 regulatory events (submissions or approvals) in major markets during 2024, plus 30 pipeline progression events. Roche is a leading advocate for **regulatory harmonization**, supporting single-dossier approaches to avoid duplication across geographies. The company actively participates in the **Accumulus Synergy** platform for real-time regulatory data sharing and supports DEEP (Dynamic Evaluation and Exchange Platform) for cloud-based collaborative reviews. Roche has been vocal about the gap between FDA expedited review utilization (71%) versus EMA (10%) and advocates for broader adoption of accelerated pathways in the EU. Regulatory strategy incorporates companion diagnostics development as a standard element, given Roche's unique Pharma-Diagnostics integration.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Roche is undertaking a radical transformation of its pharmacovigilance technology backbone, targeting a **50% increase in safety case management efficiency by 2026** -- a target that is now immediate, with the deadline year upon us. The company employs NLP to scan scientific literature and real-world data for adverse event signals. Machine learning and large language models (LLMs) are being deployed to automate manual tasks across the safety function, now with elevated organizational support following the creation of the CDTO role under Wafaa Mamilli. Safety monitoring integrates data from both the Pharmaceuticals and Diagnostics divisions, leveraging **Foundation Medicine** (comprehensive genomic data) and a restructured **Flatiron Health** (clinical trials business sold to Paradigm Health in Dec 2025; new leadership appointed Feb 2026; Roche retaining core RWE capabilities while exploring further strategic options). The 30% pipeline pruning intensifies B/R scrutiny on remaining programs: with fewer candidates, each surviving program must demonstrate superior B/R profiles to justify continued investment. The safety function also faces new demands from the **CT-388 obesity program** (Phase III launching Q1 2026) and the **Zealand petrelintide collaboration**, which introduce incretin-based and amylin-based therapies requiring metabolic safety monitoring capabilities that differ significantly from Roche's traditional oncology-centric PV expertise. The safety function operates globally with both centralized signal detection and regional compliance operations. Roche has also piloted a **digital twin platform for autoimmune trial simulation**, reducing virtual scenario iterations by ~50%.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk assessment at Roche is embedded within the Global Product Development function under the CMO. B/R decisions are informed by both clinical trial data and real-world evidence, with the integrated Pharma-Diagnostics model providing unique companion diagnostic data that enriches B/R profiles. Safety governance committees operate at the therapeutic area and portfolio levels, with escalation to the Enlarged Corporate Executive Committee. Roche's end-to-end disease area organization means B/R governance is owned by disease area teams rather than fragmented across functional silos. The company's regulatory harmonization advocacy includes support for dynamic assessment models that align with continuous B/R monitoring approaches. The 30% pipeline pruning has elevated B/R governance to a portfolio-shaping function: remaining programs survived specifically because their B/R profiles justified continued investment, and any B/R signal degradation now carries outsized strategic consequences. The CT-388 obesity program introduces a new B/R paradigm for Roche -- competitive differentiation in the crowded GLP-1/GIP space requires demonstrating not just efficacy parity with Zepbound but superior tolerability (CT-388's Phase II showed only 5.9% discontinuation due to AEs). The Zealand petrelintide combination strategy (CT-388 + amylin analog) creates complex multi-mechanism B/R profiles that will require dynamic, continuously updated assessment frameworks.

- **Interest Initiator:** Three parallel entry paths: (1) Head of Patient Safety driving the 50% safety efficiency target -- this target is **NOW due in 2026**, creating maximum urgency; (2) **CDTO Wafaa Mamilli**, who is building her enterprise AI vendor ecosystem and has explicit mandate + budget authority for digital health platforms; (3) Obesity/metabolic therapeutic area leads who need B/R frameworks for the new CT-388 and Zealand programs
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO's office and/or Global Regulatory Affairs leadership; they would assess the platform's alignment with Roche's regulatory harmonization advocacy and dynamic assessment vision. For obesity-specific entry, the CT-388/Zealand clinical teams would validate B/R framework applicability for incretin/amylin safety monitoring
- **Budget Owner:** CMO (for Global Product Development deployment), Head of Patient Safety (for safety function-specific deployment), or **CDTO** (for enterprise digital platform deployment -- new and potentially larger budget envelope)
- **Procurement Cycle:** 6-12 months (compressed from prior estimate). Three urgency drivers: (1) the 2026 PV efficiency deadline is imminent; (2) the CDTO is in active vendor selection mode for enterprise AI platforms; (3) CT-388 Phase III launch creates immediate need for obesity B/R infrastructure. However, Swiss corporate governance and the dual-structure (pRED/gRED) may require alignment across multiple stakeholders. Roche's corporate strategy function under Silke Hornstein may also weigh in on platform purchases.
- **Internal Friction Points:** The Flatiron situation is evolving -- Roche sold the clinical trials business to Paradigm Health (Dec 2025) but retains core RWE assets, so ArcaScience must position as complementary to the retained Flatiron capabilities and Foundation Medicine. The CHF 2.2B restructuring costs and ongoing portfolio rationalization mean procurement scrutiny is high. Dual R&D organizations (pRED in leadership transition, gRED stable) may have divergent priorities. Swiss data privacy standards (FADP) and EU GDPR compliance add complexity for cloud-based platforms. The new CDTO may centralize vendor decisions previously made at the function level, which could streamline or complicate procurement depending on alignment.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Multi-vector entry leveraging the convergence of three urgent triggers -- the 2026 PV efficiency deadline (NOW), the new CDTO's vendor ecosystem buildout, and the obesity/metabolic franchise launch.**

Roche has publicly committed to a 50% increase in safety case management efficiency by 2026 through AI/ML/LLM deployment. **This target year has arrived**, creating a now-or-never buying window. Simultaneously, the new CDTO (Wafaa Mamilli) is actively building her enterprise AI platform strategy, and the CT-388/Zealand obesity programs are launching into Phase III with entirely new B/R requirements. The narrative should emphasize:

1. **The 2026 PV efficiency target is NOW** -- ArcaScience's continuous B/R profiling automates the analytical layer that sits between raw signal detection and B/R governance decisions, precisely the bottleneck Roche is trying to solve. With the deadline year upon them, Roche needs solutions that can demonstrate immediate efficiency impact, not 18-month implementation timelines
2. **CDTO as a new, high-leverage entry point** -- Wafaa Mamilli is building her enterprise AI vendor ecosystem from scratch. Her mandate from CEO Schinecker explicitly includes leveraging AI across all business functions. Position ArcaScience's BRA platform as a flagship AI deployment that spans R&D, safety, and regulatory -- demonstrating the cross-functional digital transformation value that justifies her C-suite role
3. **Obesity market entry creates new B/R infrastructure needs** -- CT-388's Phase III launch (Enith1/Enith2, Q1 2026) and the Zealand petrelintide combination strategy introduce incretin and amylin-based therapies with metabolic safety profiles fundamentally different from Roche's traditional oncology expertise. Competitive differentiation vs. Zepbound requires demonstrating superior tolerability (CT-388's 5.9% AE discontinuation rate is a key selling point that needs robust, continuous B/R documentation). The fixed-dose CT-388 + petrelintide combination creates multi-mechanism B/R complexity that demands dynamic assessment frameworks
4. **Pipeline pruning amplifies B/R value of surviving programs** -- With 30% of the pipeline cut, each remaining program carries outsized strategic weight. B/R signal degradation on any surviving candidate has portfolio-level consequences. ArcaScience can position continuous B/R monitoring as a portfolio risk management tool that protects Roche's concentrated bets
5. **Complement to Roche's restructured data ecosystem** -- Position the BRA platform as the analytical engine that synthesizes inputs from retained Flatiron RWE capabilities, Foundation Medicine (genomics), clinical trial databases, and literature monitoring into a unified, continuously updated B/R view. The Flatiron restructuring (clinical trials business sold to Paradigm Health) creates a gap in Roche's data integration stack that BRA can partially fill
6. **Support for regulatory harmonization advocacy** -- Roche is championing dynamic assessment and real-time regulatory data sharing (Accumulus, DEEP); a dynamic BRA platform provides the continuous B/R evidence that these regulatory models require

A secondary entry through neuroscience remains compelling given the Phase III advancements (trontinemab for Alzheimer's, prasinezumab for Parkinson's) -- these CNS programs have complex, long-duration B/R profiles that benefit from continuous monitoring.
