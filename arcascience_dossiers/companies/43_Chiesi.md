# Chiesi

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 3.41B (~$3.7B; +13% at CER; record year)
- **Headquarters:** Parma, Italy
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Respiratory (AIR franchise — asthma, COPD; 54% of revenue), neonatology/special care (CARE franchise — 24% of revenue), rare diseases (RARE franchise — 22% of revenue; +41% growth driven by Amryt Pharma integration)
- **Pipeline Size:** 43 projects across three therapeutic areas; Phase III trials for U.S. respiratory market entry; rare disease product launches following 3 FDA approvals; EUR 829M R&D spend (24.3% of revenue — top pharmaceutical R&D investor in Italy and 11th in Europe)
- **Strategic Priorities:** U.S. market expansion (now 20% of revenue, +24% growth); rare disease franchise scaling (Amryt integration); respiratory pipeline for U.S. entry; Biotech Center of Excellence in Parma (EUR 380M investment 2024-2030); carbon-minimal inhaler program; Oak Hill Bio neonatology collaboration; PAH/PH-ILD partnership with Gossamer Bio

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Chiesi's clinical development is organized around three global business units: AIR (respiratory), CARE (neonatology/special care), and RARE (rare diseases). With record R&D investment of EUR 829M (24.3% of revenue), Chiesi ranks among Europe's top pharmaceutical R&D investors. The 43-project pipeline spans all three therapeutic areas. Phase III clinical trials are a primary driver of R&D spending, particularly for respiratory products seeking U.S. market entry. The RARE business unit was significantly strengthened by the Amryt Pharma acquisition (2023), integrating rare disease clinical development capabilities. Chiesi also operates a new Biotech Center of Excellence in Parma for biological medicine production from the R&D pipeline.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages EMA, FDA, and global submissions. The team is managing a major strategic initiative: entry into the U.S. respiratory market through Phase III clinical trials. Three FDA approvals were obtained in the previous year for rare disease products, demonstrating strong FDA regulatory capability. The rare disease regulatory team handles orphan drug designations and accelerated pathways. The carbon-minimal inhaler program requires regulatory submissions for reformulated propellant-based products.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV manages safety across respiratory products (inhalers with decades of post-marketing data), neonatology products (administered to extremely vulnerable patient populations — premature infants), and rare disease products (small populations, limited safety databases). The neonatology portfolio demands the highest PV vigilance given the patient vulnerability. The Amryt integration brought new rare disease safety monitoring requirements. The Gossamer Bio PAH collaboration introduces cardiovascular safety considerations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is managed through the family-owned governance structure. As a B Corp certified, privately held company with strong ESG commitments, Chiesi's B/R framework explicitly incorporates patient-centricity and sustainability considerations alongside traditional clinical and regulatory factors. The three distinct therapeutic areas (respiratory, neonatology, rare disease) each have specialized B/R considerations.
