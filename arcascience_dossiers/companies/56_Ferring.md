# Ferring Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 2,343 million (~$2.5 billion; up 7% YoY); reproductive medicine franchise exceeded EUR 1 billion for the first time
- **Headquarters:** Saint-Prex, Switzerland (privately held)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Reproductive medicine and maternal health (world leader in ART/fertility), uro-oncology gene therapy (Adstiladrin for bladder cancer), gastroenterology/microbiome (Rebyota for C. diff), urology
- **Pipeline Size:** Active clinical programs in reproductive medicine, uro-oncology (Adstiladrin label expansion, combination therapy with Theralase), and microbiome platform; R&D in reproductive health peptides (barusiban, follitropin delta Phase 3 RITA trials), gene therapy expansion; clinical collaboration agreement with Theralase Technologies for bladder cancer combination therapy (January 2026)
- **Strategic Priorities:** Reproductive medicine franchise growth, Adstiladrin US commercialization and global expansion (Japan NDA under PMDA review since September 2025, Phase 3 showing 75% CR rate in Japanese patients), microbiome platform development (REBYOTA real-world data and long-term safety from CDI-SCOPE Phase 3b), new Enterprise Model (Core Business + Portfolio) with operational optimization reducing ~500 roles globally (October 2025), gene therapy manufacturing expansion (new Kuopio, Finland facility operational; Parsippany, NJ second facility FDA-approved), partnership-driven pipeline expansion (Evotec, PharmaBiome, Theralase Technologies)
- **Key 2025-2026 Developments:** CEO transition (Jean-Frederic Paulsen appointed CEO while retaining Board Chairman role, February 2026); new CTO appointed (Andre Overmeyer from Merck Life Science, effective March 2026); global operational restructuring with up to 500 role reductions; Adstiladrin Japan Phase 3 results presented at Japanese Urological Association; REBYOTA real-world evidence presented at IDWeek 2025 and DDW 2025

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Ferring conducts clinical research worldwide in reproductive medicine, uro-oncology, and gastroenterology. Clinical development is led by the Chief Medical Officer / Chief Science Officer (dual role held since March 2024), reflecting integration of medical and scientific leadership. The company's Enterprise Model (announced September 2025) separates "Core Business" (strengthening current portfolio) from "Portfolio" (driving future growth through clinical development and external innovation with a focus on mid-to-late-stage opportunities). A new Science, Medicine and Development Council, chaired by board member Dr. Michael Rosenblatt, provides advice and oversight for clinical development, medical affairs, regulatory, and pharmacovigilance. In January 2026, Ferring signed a clinical development agreement with Theralase Technologies to investigate combination therapy (Theralase's Ruvidar followed by Adstiladrin) in bladder cancer, expanding the uro-oncology clinical program. The Phase 3 RITA trials for follitropin delta in reproductive medicine continue to generate data on age-based dosing and tolerability. The October 2025 operational optimization (up to 500 role reductions globally) is intended to sharpen strategic focus and free resources for reinvestment in innovation.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
In the Enterprise Model, Technical Operations encompasses Global Regulatory Affairs. Armin Metzger (EVP and Chief Technical Operations Officer) is stepping down at end of March 2026, succeeded by Andre Overmeyer (from Merck Life Science), signaling a leadership transition in the regulatory-adjacent function. Regulatory complexity spans reproductive health products (globally regulated differently), gene therapy (Adstiladrin, navigating FDA and PMDA approval pathways), and microbiome therapeutics (novel regulatory pathway for Rebyota as first FDA-approved fecal microbiota product). The PMDA accepted the Adstiladrin NDA for review in September 2025, supported by Japan Phase 3 data showing a 75% complete response rate at 3 months across 25 centers, with all treatment-related adverse events limited to Grade 1-2 severity. FDA approved a second drug product manufacturing facility for Adstiladrin (Parsippany, NJ) in 2025, expanding the manufacturing footprint to three sites globally (including the new Kuopio, Finland facility for drug substance). Regulatory affairs supports commercial operations across 50+ countries and marketing in 100+ countries.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV is overseen through the Science, Medicine and Development Council structure (chaired by Dr. Michael Rosenblatt). The PV function manages safety monitoring for reproductive health products (used in millions of fertility treatments globally), gene therapy (Adstiladrin, requiring long-term follow-up as a non-replicating gene therapy), and a first-in-class microbiome product (Rebyota). The diversity of modalities (peptides, gene therapy, microbiome) creates unique PV challenges. New 2025-2026 PV data: REBYOTA long-term safety data from the Phase 3b CDI-SCOPE trial demonstrated microbiome restoration through six months; real-world studies presented at IDWeek 2025 confirmed consistent efficacy and safety in preventing recurrent C. diff infection; DDW 2025 data showed safety and treatment success with colonoscopy administration along with quality-of-life improvements. Adstiladrin Japan Phase 3 safety data (zero Grade 3+ adverse events in 20 patients) strengthens the global safety profile as the company prepares for potential PMDA approval. The Theralase combination therapy agreement (January 2026) will generate new PV requirements for the combination of photodynamic therapy followed by gene therapy in bladder cancer.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
The Science, Medicine and Development Council (chaired by Dr. Michael Rosenblatt) provides oversight for B/R decisions across clinical development, medical affairs, regulatory, and pharmacovigilance under the Board's authority. This governance structure centralizes B/R oversight at a time of significant organizational change: the CEO transition (Jean-Frederic Paulsen assuming dual CEO/Chairman role in February 2026), operational restructuring (up to 500 role reductions), and CTO succession all increase the importance of stable B/R governance. The separation of Core Business from Portfolio in the Enterprise Model implies distinct B/R governance for existing products vs. pipeline candidates. The Theralase combination therapy collaboration introduces a new B/R dimension: evaluating the sequential use of two distinct modalities (photodynamic therapy + gene therapy) requires integrated B/R frameworks that span both partners' data.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Ferring at a critical inflection point of organizational transformation and leadership transition. The Enterprise Model with its separate "Portfolio" function focused on external innovation and clinical development creates a natural home for BRA technology, and the ~500-role restructuring (October 2025) means remaining teams need efficiency tools to maintain output. The Science, Medicine and Development Council (chaired by Dr. Michael Rosenblatt) is the key B/R governance body that would benefit from a structured BRA platform. The most compelling entry points are: (1) the Adstiladrin franchise, now expanding via the Theralase combination therapy agreement (January 2026) and approaching potential PMDA approval in Japan (NDA under review since September 2025, Phase 3 showing 75% CR rate with excellent safety), where multi-modality combination B/R assessment and multi-market regulatory coordination are critical; (2) REBYOTA, where accumulating real-world evidence (IDWeek 2025, DDW 2025) and long-term Phase 3b CDI-SCOPE safety data create an expanding B/R dataset that needs systematic integration; (3) the reproductive medicine franchise, where Phase 3 RITA trials for follitropin delta are generating new B/R data on age-based dosing. The CEO transition (Paulsen assuming dual CEO/Chairman role, February 2026) and CTO succession (Overmeyer from Merck Life Science, March 2026) create a window where new leadership may be receptive to tools that demonstrate operational efficiency. Position ArcaScience as a tool that supports the Science, Medicine and Development Council's mandate to provide integrated oversight across clinical development, medical affairs, regulatory, and PV during a period of organizational lean-down and leadership change.
