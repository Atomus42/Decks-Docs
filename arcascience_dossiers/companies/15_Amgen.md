## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Amgen's R&D organization is led by an EVP of Research and Development, with clinical development organized by therapeutic area. The company invested a record $7.0B in R&D in 2025 (22% increase over 2024's $6.0B record), reflecting the massive scale of clinical programs underway. Clinical development is structured across major franchises: Obesity & Related Conditions (led by a dedicated EVP), Oncology, Inflammation, Cardiovascular, Bone Health, and Rare Disease. The MARITIME Phase 3 program for MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide) is one of the largest clinical trial programs in Amgen's 45+ year history, with six Phase 3 studies now underway: MARITIME-1 (obesity without T2D), MARITIME-2 (obesity with T2D), plus four additional studies announced in November 2025 in ASCVD with obesity, heart failure with preserved/mildly reduced ejection fraction and obesity, and two obstructive sleep apnea populations. Phase 2 data (presented at ADA June 2025, published in NEJM) showed up to ~20% weight loss at 52 weeks with no weight loss plateau, and second-year data confirmed weight maintenance with well-tolerated quarterly dosing. Phase 3 features an optimized three-step dose escalation over 8 weeks followed by a 64-week target dose period. A separate Phase 2 study of MariTide for T2D completed its 24-week timepoint in January 2026. The Horizon Therapeutics acquisition ($27.8B, closed October 2023) added clinical development capabilities in thyroid eye disease (TEPEZZA, with Japan and EU expansion underway) and gout (KRYSTEXXA), though Amgen acknowledges integration synergies and benefits may take longer than expected.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Global Regulatory Affairs is a centralized function within R&D, managing filings across the FDA, EMA, and global markets. The regulatory function has been intensely active, with five FDA approvals secured in 2025 alone, including the landmark conversion of IMDELLTRA from accelerated to full FDA approval (November 2025) based on the Phase 3 DeLLphi-304 study showing 40% reduction in risk of death versus standard of care in ES-SCLC. IMDELLTRA is now the NCCN Category 1 preferred treatment for relapsed ES-SCLC. Other 2025 regulatory milestones include broader U.S. Repatha labeling, multiple new UPLIZNA indications, new TEZSPIRE indications, and EU approval of TEPEZZA. The regulatory organization faces intensifying competing demands: accelerating filings for novel pipeline assets (MariTide Phase 3 program, oncology expansion, olpasiran for cardiovascular disease, biosimilar antibodies) while managing the patent cliff fallout -- Sandoz biosimilar Prolia/Xgeva launched May 2025, with Samsung Bioepis, Celltrion, and AryoGen Pharmed also developing biosimilar denosumab. Enbrel faces additional pressure from IRA-driven Medicare Part D net price declines (sales fell 48% in Q4 2025). The creation of a dedicated EVP for Obesity & Related Conditions confirms that regulatory strategy for MariTide receives its own governance structure.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Pharmacovigilance at Amgen supports a massive marketed portfolio of 14 blockbuster products plus clinical programs spanning biologics, biosimilars, small molecules, T-cell engagers, and peptide-antibody conjugates. IMDELLTRA's full FDA approval in November 2025 solidifies its post-marketing PV requirements, including management of cytokine release syndrome (42% Grade 1, 13% Grade 2, 1% Grade 3 CRS in DeLLphi-304, primarily after the first two doses). The PV function has expanded significantly with the Horizon acquisition, adding rare disease safety monitoring for TEPEZZA and KRYSTEXXA, and UPLIZNA's expansion into multiple new indications increases the monitoring scope. MariTide's MARITIME Phase 3 program will generate the largest safety dataset in Amgen's history given six concurrent Phase 3 studies targeting a generally healthy obesity population where the safety bar is exceptionally high. Phase 2 data showed GI adverse events (nausea, vomiting) were the most common, mostly mild-to-moderate, with tolerability improving over time and at quarterly maintenance doses. The patent cliff also creates PV implications: as biosimilar versions of Prolia, Xgeva, and Enbrel enter the market, Amgen must coordinate pharmacovigilance with biosimilar manufacturers and maintain reference product safety reporting.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance operates through R&D portfolio review committees, with strategic oversight from the executive leadership team. The MariTide program represents the highest-stakes B/R decisions in Amgen's current portfolio -- demonstrating an acceptable safety profile in a large, relatively healthy obesity population across six concurrent Phase 3 studies is essential. Phase 2 data showed up to 20% weight loss with no plateau and acceptable GI tolerability, but Wall Street has noted MariTide's weight loss figures appear slightly below Eli Lilly's Zepbound, making the B/R differentiation story critical. B/R committees must balance the needs of the massive obesity franchise (safety bar in essentially well patients) against the oncology portfolio (where higher risk tolerance applies for T-cell engagers in cancer patients -- IMDELLTRA's full approval validates this framework). The Horizon integration has added rare disease B/R governance where patient populations are small and alternative treatments are limited. The patent cliff adds urgency: with 42% of 2025 revenue at risk from LOE, Amgen's growth story depends entirely on new product B/R profiles being favorable enough to drive rapid market uptake.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should lead with the MariTide obesity story. MariTide's MARITIME Phase 3 program is one of the largest clinical trials in Amgen's history, targeting a generally healthy obesity population where the safety bar is exceptionally high (unlike oncology, where patients accept more risk). The FDA will scrutinize the benefit-risk profile intensely -- overweight/obese patients must have near-perfect safety for a chronic treatment. ArcaScience's dynamic BRA platform can continuously update the B/R profile as MARITIME data accrues across multiple studies (obesity, ASCVD, heart failure, sleep apnea), giving Amgen real-time visibility into whether the aggregate safety signal supports filing. The second angle is the novel modality challenge: Amgen now operates across biologics, biosimilars, T-cell engagers, and bispecific antibodies -- each with distinct B/R frameworks. ArcaScience can position as the unifying BRA layer across modalities. Frame the ROI around the $6B R&D spend: even a 5% improvement in clinical attrition rates represents hundreds of millions in saved R&D investment. Propose a pilot on the MARITIME cardiovascular outcomes study, where integrating obesity, CV, and metabolic B/R endpoints in a single dynamic model could differentiate MariTide from competitors.
