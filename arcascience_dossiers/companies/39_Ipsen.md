# Ipsen

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 3.4B (~$3.7B; +9.9% at CER; growth across all three therapeutic areas)
- **Headquarters:** Paris (Boulogne-Billancourt), France
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Oncology (NETs, pancreatic cancer, HCC, glioma), rare disease (Alagille syndrome, PFIC, FOP), neuroscience (neurotoxin franchise, rare neurological diseases)
- **Pipeline Size:** 30+ programs added since 2020 across all three therapeutic areas; 8 new assets added in 2024; ~2,000 patients across 800 sites in ongoing clinical programs; R&D investment of EUR 687M in 2024
- **Strategic Priorities:** Oncology pipeline expansion (ADCs via Sutro and Foreseen, RAF inhibitor tovorafenib from DayOne); rare disease franchise growth (67.4% revenue increase in 2024); neuroscience expansion (Long-Acting Neurotoxin, rare neurological diseases); external innovation-driven model (30+ programs sourced externally since 2020); profitability maintenance (32.6% core operating margin)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Ipsen's clinical development is organized around three therapeutic areas with global hubs in the U.S., France, and the U.K. The company has teams in 40+ countries with products reaching 100+ countries. The clinical development model is heavily externally sourced — since 2020, Ipsen has brought in 30+ programs from external partners across all development stages. Key partnerships include Sutro Biopharma (ADCs), Foreseen Biotechnology (ADCs), Marengo Therapeutics (T-cell engagers), Biomunex (TCEs), DayOne Biopharmaceuticals (tovorafenib), and Skyhawk Therapeutics (RNA-modulating small molecules). Internal capabilities include ~100 years of development expertise and clinical operations across 800 sites globally.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages EMA, FDA, and global submissions. Recent milestones include FDA approval of Onivyde for first-line PDAC, U.S. and European approval of Iqirvo (elafibranor) for PBC, and EU approval of Kayfanda (odevixibat) for Alagille syndrome. The regulatory team handles both traditional small molecule pathways and emerging biologic/ADC pathways. The tovorafenib European filing represents navigation of pediatric oncology regulatory requirements.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV manages safety across a diverse portfolio: neurotoxin products (Dysport — long-term safety in established markets), oncology products (Somatuline, Onivyde, Cabometyx), and rare disease products (Iqirvo, Kayfanda, Sohonos). The rare disease portfolio requires intensive PV with small patient populations and limited long-term safety data. The expanding ADC and T-cell engager pipeline will introduce new immunotherapy-related safety monitoring requirements.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance sits within the R&D leadership, supported by nearly 100 years of institutional development experience. The external innovation model means B/R assessment must often begin mid-stream when in-licensing assets at various development stages. The balanced portfolio across oncology, rare disease, and neuroscience requires therapeutic area-specific B/R expertise.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Head of Oncology Development or Head of Rare Disease Development — both segments have complex B/R requirements and are growing rapidly
- **Scientific Validator:** Head of R&D / CSO — must validate that BRA platform can handle the diversity of externally sourced assets at different development stages
- **Budget Owner:** Head of R&D or CFO — EUR 687M R&D budget and 32.6% operating margin provide investment capacity
- **Procurement Cycle:** 6-12 months; French mid-cap pharma with European procurement norms; lean enough for relatively fast decisions
- **Internal Friction Points:** External innovation model means many assets arrive with partner-generated B/R data that must be integrated; multiple partnership structures (licensing, co-development, ex-U.S. rights) create complexity; three therapeutic areas with very different B/R needs may challenge platform generalizability; neurotoxin franchise (Dysport) is mature and may resist new tool adoption

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Ipsen through its external innovation model. With 30+ programs sourced from external partners since 2020, Ipsen needs a systematic B/R framework that can rapidly onboard and assess in-licensed assets at various development stages. The pitch should emphasize: (1) ArcaScience as a "B/R integration engine" for externally sourced assets — when Ipsen in-licenses a Phase 1 asset from Sutro or a Phase 3 asset from DayOne, the BRA platform can immediately establish and continuously update the B/R profile using all available data, (2) for the rare disease portfolio (67.4% growth), dynamic B/R assessment is particularly valuable because small patient populations mean every data point matters and regulators require sophisticated B/R communication, (3) probabilistic approval prediction can enhance Ipsen's business development due diligence, improving the quality of in-licensing decisions. The key differentiator for Ipsen is that ArcaScience enables the external innovation model to work better — reducing the time and risk of integrating externally sourced clinical data into Ipsen's development framework. Engage through ESMO, ASCO, or rare disease conferences (ECRD, NORD) where Ipsen is active.
