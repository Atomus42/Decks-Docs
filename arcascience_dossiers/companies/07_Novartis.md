# Novartis

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** $54.5 billion (+8% cc), up from $50.3B in FY2024; core operating income $21.9B (+14% cc); core margin 40.1% (+210bps YoY); core EPS $8.98 (+17% cc); free cash flow $17.6B (+8%). Q1 2025 was $13.23B (+11.9% YoY) with 42.1% core margin. FY2026 guidance: low single-digit net sales growth, low single-digit core OI decline -- reflecting the largest patent expiry in Novartis history (Entresto, Tasigna, Promacta/Revolade LOE in late 2025)
- **Headquarters:** Basel, Switzerland
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Cardiovascular/Renal/Metabolic (CRM), Immunology, Neuroscience, Oncology -- four core therapeutic areas as a pure-play innovative medicines company (post-Sandoz demerger fully realized)
- **Key Growth Brands:** Kisqali (breast cancer; FY2024 $3.03B, +46%; Q2 2025 $1.18B, +64% YoY; peak sales upgraded to $10B+ with early breast cancer expansion), Pluvicto (radioligand therapy; Q3 2025 $564M, +45%; pre-chemo mCRPC label expansion ~3x eligible patients; mHSPC Phase III PSMAddition positive), Scemblix (CML; +87% cc FY2025; first-line FDA approval broadens population ~4x; peak sales raised to $4B+), Kesimpta, Cosentyx
- **Pipeline Size:** 30+ potentially high-value NME candidates in Phase I-III; 8 de-risked blockbuster drugs with $3-10B peak sales potential each; 10% pipeline pruning completed to sharpen focus on high-value programs
- **Strategic Priorities:** Pure-play innovative medicines focus, four-pillar therapeutic area strategy, AI-driven decision making (IDS platform on AWS), fast-to-IND strategy, $23B five-year US manufacturing/R&D investment, responsible AI governance framework (Code of Ethics updated 2024; AI Risk & Compliance Management Framework aligned with EU AI Act), 5-6% cc sales CAGR guidance 2025-2030
- **AI Partnerships:** Isomorphic Labs (DeepMind spin-off; small molecule discovery collaboration expanded February 2025 with 3 additional programs; chemical material found for previously intractable targets), Generate:Biomedicines ($65M upfront + $1B+ milestones; AI-designed protein therapeutics across multiple disease areas), Microsoft, Schrodinger (computational chemistry, November 2024)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Novartis's clinical development is led by Shreeram Aradhye, M.D. (President, Development and Chief Medical Officer), who brings experience across multiple therapeutic areas including innovative therapeutics, cell and gene therapies. The Development function manages clinical programs from Phase I through registration, running global clinical trials to confirm safety and efficacy. The **Translational Research & Development (TRD)** team comprises ~2,500 associates across 4 continents and 8 countries, developing virtually the entire Novartis portfolio across all disease areas. A newly created **Data & Digital** team within Global Drug Development aims to enhance medicine value through data and digital capabilities. Biomedical Research, led by Fiona H. Marshall (President), serves as the separate innovation engine focused on turning scientific breakthroughs into development candidates. Novartis employs a **fast-to-IND strategy** for preclinical assets, reflecting a culture of accelerated development. The company has committed a $23 billion investment over five years to expand US manufacturing and R&D (announced early 2025, building/expanding 10 US facilities). In 2025, Novartis secured multiple FDA approvals and positive Phase III readouts across Pluvicto (pre-chemo mCRPC), Scemblix (first-line CML), and ianalumab, while the PSMAddition trial delivered a third positive Phase III readout for Pluvicto in mHSPC. Novartis also advanced its radioligand therapy (RLT) pipeline beyond prostate cancer, exploring new isotopes, ligands, and combination therapies targeting breast, colon, lung, and pancreatic cancers -- a novel modality that introduces unique radiopharmaceutical safety monitoring requirements into the Development function.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory Affairs at Novartis is a **unified, company-wide team** (post-Sandoz demerger simplification) focused on regulatory excellence and innovation. The RA function collaborates cross-functionally with R&D, clinical development, pharmacovigilance, quality assurance, and marketing. Given Novartis's presence in 150+ countries, the regulatory team manages a robust multi-jurisdiction strategy, including participation in early scientific advice meetings with health authorities and leveraging accelerated pathways such as **FDA Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy Designation, and EMA PRIME**. The function aligns regulatory submissions with local market requirements while maintaining global consistency. Novartis also operates a **Regulatory Affairs Postgraduate Training Program**, indicating deep investment in regulatory talent development. RA is integrated with the broader Quality & Regulatory function, which includes audit programs and regulatory inspection readiness (900+ audits per year).

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Novartis operates a dedicated **Patient Safety and Pharmacovigilance** unit within the Development function, with the centralized organization (Hyderabad operational hub + Basel strategic hub) now **fully operational** following the completion of the country-level PV and Device Vigilance integration into a single organization with centralized governance and regional execution. The **Surveillance function** within PV creates Development Safety Update Reports (DSURs) for clinical-stage drugs and Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Reports (PBRERs) for marketed drugs. Novartis researchers have published work on risk-based approaches to ICSR follow-up procedures, indicating thought leadership in PV methodology innovation. Quality oversight includes pharmacovigilance within the 900+ annual audit program covering GMP, GCP, GLP, and PV.

**Radiopharmaceutical PV Complexity (Pluvicto):** The Pluvicto radioligand therapy franchise introduces unique pharmacovigilance requirements not present in conventional small molecule or biologic portfolios. These include: radiation safety monitoring across ~600 US treatment sites (and 25+ countries globally), management of radiation-specific adverse events (myelosuppression, renal toxicity) alongside conventional oncology AEs, coordination with nuclear medicine departments for safety reporting, causality assessment challenges specific to radiopharmaceuticals (disentangling radiation effects from disease progression), and underreporting risk due to the multidisciplinary administration model (nuclear medicine, oncology, radiology, urology). As Pluvicto's label expands to pre-chemotherapy mCRPC (~3x more patients) and potentially mHSPC (~2x more again), the PV case volume and complexity will scale significantly.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Novartis operates within the Development function under the CMO:
1. **Product Safety Teams** -- Asset-level B/R monitoring and signal assessment
2. **Surveillance function** -- DSUR/PBRER generation with formal B/R evaluation
3. **Development leadership** -- CMO-led portfolio-level B/R governance
4. **Executive Committee** -- CEO Vas Narasimhan and direct reports provide enterprise B/R oversight for major decisions

The AI-powered **Intelligent Decision System (IDS)**, built on AWS, uses digital twins to simulate clinical workflows and forecast outcomes. The IDS platform has expanded significantly: the underlying next-generation data platform (built with AWS and Accenture) now ingests 9TB+ from 80+ internal/external data sources across Development, Commercial, Manufacturing/Quality, and Corporate Business Services -- at 20% faster rates than legacy systems. Protocol generation use cases show 83-87% acceleration in producing compliance-standard protocols. The platform's GenAI roadmap targets connecting all generative AI use cases, with Amazon Bedrock and Lambda being explored for automated compliance documentation and IQ/OQ/PQ test generation. This expanding IDS capability creates a natural integration point for B/R-specific analytics -- the BRA platform would serve as the benefit-risk module within this broader digital twin ecosystem.

**Kisqali B/R Complexity:** The expansion of Kisqali from metastatic to early-stage breast cancer (FDA-approved September 2024 for HR+/HER2- stage II/III eBC based on NATALEE trial; 28.5% recurrence risk reduction at longer follow-up) creates growing B/R evaluation complexity. The adjuvant setting involves a fundamentally different risk-benefit calculus than metastatic disease -- treating patients with no active cancer to prevent recurrence, where long-term safety tolerability weighs more heavily. With Kisqali now the broadest-label CDK4/6 inhibitor (including node-negative patients, unlike Verzenio), B/R assessments must span multiple subtypes, risk categories, and treatment settings across 150+ markets, each with distinct regulatory expectations. Global regulatory expansion is ongoing (EU, China pending; NICE approved; Health Canada approved June 2025).

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Enter through the Surveillance/PBRER function, positioning around continuous B/R monitoring that transforms periodic reporting into dynamic assessment.**

Novartis's Surveillance function (responsible for DSURs and PBRERs) is the most direct organizational target for a dynamic BRA platform. The narrative should center on:

1. **Transforming PBRER from periodic to continuous** -- Regulatory authorities increasingly expect continuous safety monitoring rather than periodic snapshots. ArcaScience's dynamic BRA enables Novartis to maintain a living B/R profile that generates PBRERs as outputs, rather than conducting separate periodic assessments
2. **Complementing the IDS platform** -- Position ArcaScience as the B/R-specific module within Novartis's broader Intelligent Decision System. The IDS uses digital twins for clinical workflow simulation; the BRA platform adds B/R-specific simulation that feeds into IDS scenario modeling
3. **Supporting 8 blockbuster candidates through the patent cliff** -- Each of the 8 de-risked drugs with $3-10B peak sales potential (Kisqali at $10B+, Scemblix at $4B+, Pluvicto, Kesimpta, ianalumab, remibrutinib, etc.) requires rigorous, continuous B/R monitoring across multiple indications and geographies. As Novartis navigates its largest-ever patent cliff (Entresto, Tasigna, Promacta LOE), the pressure to maximize value from growth brands through flawless regulatory execution intensifies. A dynamic BRA platform directly supports the 5-6% CAGR target through 2030 by reducing B/R-related regulatory friction for these assets.
4. **Enabling the 150-country regulatory footprint** -- Novartis submits regulatory documents across 150+ markets. A centralized, dynamic B/R platform ensures consistency of B/R messaging across jurisdictions while accommodating local regulatory requirements. This is especially critical as Kisqali undergoes simultaneous global regulatory expansion for early breast cancer (EU, China pending; NICE, Health Canada approved) and Pluvicto rolls out to 25+ countries with jurisdiction-specific radiopharmaceutical regulations.
5. **Radioligand therapy as a novel-modality B/R challenge** -- Pluvicto represents a fundamentally different safety monitoring paradigm. Radiation-specific AEs (myelosuppression, renal toxicity), multidisciplinary administration (nuclear medicine + oncology + radiology + urology), causality assessment complexity (distinguishing radiation from disease effects), and administration across ~600 specialized US sites create PV demands that conventional safety databases were not designed to handle. As the RLT pipeline expands beyond prostate cancer into breast, colon, lung, and pancreatic tumors, this complexity will multiply. ArcaScience can position the BRA platform as purpose-built for novel-modality B/R assessment where traditional tools fail.

A secondary entry through Ronny Gal's strategy function is increasingly viable: positioning the BRA platform as a portfolio valuation tool that quantifies risk-adjusted pipeline value with dynamic B/R data, supporting his aggressive M&A due diligence cadence ($12B Avidity deal, Anthos, Regulus in 2025 alone) and the broader 5-6% CAGR commitment.

The pure-play innovative medicines focus (post-Sandoz) simplifies vendor relationships -- there is a single decision-making chain without consumer health or generics stakeholders to navigate.
