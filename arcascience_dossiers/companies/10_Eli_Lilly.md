# Eli Lilly

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** ~$63.0-63.5 billion (45% YoY growth); tirzepatide franchise (Mounjaro + Zepbound) generated $36.5B combined, becoming the world's best-selling drug; Mounjaro ~$23B, Zepbound ~$13.5B
- **Headquarters:** Indianapolis, Indiana, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Metabolic/Obesity (GLP-1 franchise: Mounjaro, Zepbound; oral GLP-1: orforglipron; triple agonist: retatrutide), Diabetes, Oncology (radiopharmaceuticals, PI3Ka inhibitors via Scorpion Therapeutics), Neuroscience (Alzheimer's/Kisunla, non-opioid pain via SiteOne Therapeutics), Immunology (NLRP3 inhibitors via Ventyx Biosciences), Cardiovascular (gene editing via Verve Therapeutics), Gene Therapy (Adverum Biotechnologies)
- **Pipeline Size:** 11 obesity drugs in pipeline; orforglipron NDA under FDA Priority Review (PDUFA target: April 10, 2026 for obesity); retatrutide in 7 Phase III TRIUMPH trials (first readout December 2025: 28.7% weight loss, NDA submission expected late 2026); extensive Phase III programs across oncology (POINT Biopharma radiopharmaceuticals), neuroscience (Kisunla), and immunology; multiple Phase III readouts expected throughout 2026
- **M&A Activity (2025-2026):** Scorpion Therapeutics (~$2.5B, oncology PI3Ka, Jan 2025), SiteOne Therapeutics (~$1B, non-opioid pain, May 2025), Verve Therapeutics (~$1.3B, CRISPR-based gene editing for cardiovascular, mid-2025), Adverum Biotechnologies (~$262M, gene therapy, Oct 2025), Ventyx Biosciences (~$1.2B, oral immunology/NLRP3 inhibitors, Jan 2026) -- totaling ~$6.3B+, spanning five novel modalities
- **Strategic Priorities:** GLP-1 franchise dominance and supply scaling ($1.5B pre-launch inventory for orforglipron), oncology portfolio building (radiopharmaceuticals, Scorpion Therapeutics, Orna Therapeutics), neuroscience expansion (Alzheimer's, ALS, non-opioid pain), immunology entry (Ventyx), cardiovascular gene editing (Verve), AI-native enterprise transformation, first Chief AI Officer fully operational, NVIDIA supercomputer partnership, TuneLab AI platform monetizing externally, $4.5B research/manufacturing center

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Eli Lilly's R&D is led by Dr. Daniel M. Skovronsky (EVP, Chief Scientific Officer, and President of Lilly Research Laboratories and Immunology, since 2024). Lilly Research Laboratories is the primary R&D engine, organized by therapeutic area (Metabolic/Diabetes, Oncology, Neuroscience, Immunology). Clinical development operates within a global matrix structure with functional departments including clinical pharmacology, biostatistics, clinical operations, and translational medicine. The company appointed Thomas J. Fuchs as its first **Chief AI Officer** in October 2024, reflecting the strategic importance of AI across all R&D functions. Diogo Rau (EVP, Chief Information and Digital Officer) provides the technology infrastructure backbone. Lilly's AI initiatives have already saved approximately 1.4 million hours of human work, with a target to double to 2.4 million hours. The company launched **TuneLab** in September 2025, an AI/ML platform providing external biotech companies access to Lilly's drug discovery models built through $1B+ in research investment, indicating that Lilly views its AI capabilities as a competitive differentiator to be monetized.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory Affairs at Lilly operates as **Global Regulatory Affairs (GRA)** within the broader R&D organization. The function manages a high-volume regulatory pipeline across multiple therapeutic areas and modalities, including novel mechanisms (GLP-1 agonists, dual/triple agonists, anti-amyloid antibodies, radiopharmaceuticals) and, increasingly, entirely new modality classes acquired through M&A (CRISPR gene editing from Verve, gene therapy from Adverum, NLRP3 inhibitors from Ventyx). The **Global Medicines Quality Organization (GMQO)** for Regulatory and Safety provides integrated quality oversight across GRA, Global Patient Safety (GPS), HEOR, and Global Scientific Communications (GSC), ensuring alignment between regulatory submissions and safety data. Lilly has demonstrated regulatory execution capability with the full FDA approval of Kisunla (donanemab) for Alzheimer's disease (July 2024), European Commission marketing authorization, and multiple metabolic/obesity regulatory milestones. The regulatory function is now navigating its most complex period ever: orforglipron's NDA is under FDA review via the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher program (PDUFA target: April 10, 2026), compressing a typical 10-12 month review into months; retatrutide's 7 Phase III TRIUMPH trials are reading out throughout 2026, with NDA submission anticipated late 2026; and each of the five 2025-2026 acquisitions brings distinct regulatory pathways (gene editing, gene therapy, and novel small molecule modalities) that GRA must integrate into its regulatory strategy.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Lilly's **Global Patient Safety (GPS)** function takes a comprehensive and rigorous approach to pharmacovigilance designed to maintain and evaluate each product's benefit-risk profile. GPS communicates important safety issues to doctors, patients, and regulatory agencies. By law, Lilly reports safety information according to specific timelines including expedited individual case safety reports, periodic aggregate safety reports, clinical study reports, and other relevant communications. GPS operates under the quality oversight of GMQO (which integrates Regulatory and Safety quality), ensuring alignment between safety monitoring and regulatory compliance. The function communicates safety findings -- whether favorable or unfavorable -- to regulators, patients, and healthcare providers in an accurate, objective, and balanced manner. GPS is positioned within the organizational structure alongside GRA under GMQO oversight, creating natural integration between safety and regulatory functions.

GPS faces a uniquely layered set of pharmacovigilance challenges in 2026:
- **Population-scale GLP-1 monitoring:** The tirzepatide franchise ($36.5B, tens of millions of patients) generates an unprecedented volume of real-world safety data. Even extremely rare adverse events (cardiovascular, pancreatitis, thyroid, suicidality concerns, muscle loss) will surface statistically at this scale, requiring continuous B/R recalibration.
- **Oral GLP-1 launch:** Orforglipron's expected approval (PDUFA April 10, 2026) introduces a new formulation with distinct pharmacokinetics and potentially distinct safety profiles versus injectable tirzepatide. Post-marketing surveillance for the oral formulation must be stood up rapidly at launch.
- **Retatrutide dysesthesia signal:** The Phase III TRIUMPH-4 trial revealed dysesthesia (abnormal touch sensation) in 8.8% (9 mg) and 20.9% (12 mg) of patients versus 0.7% placebo -- a signal not seen in Phase 2 and unique to this triple agonist mechanism. GPS must characterize this novel signal across 6 additional TRIUMPH readouts in 2026, determine whether it is dose-dependent and mechanism-related (likely glucagon receptor activity), and develop a B/R framework for a safety concern with no precedent in the GLP-1 class.
- **Novel modality PV paradigms:** The Verve (CRISPR gene editing), Adverum (gene therapy), and Ventyx (NLRP3 inhibitors) acquisitions each introduce safety monitoring paradigms fundamentally different from small molecule and biologic PV. Gene editing and gene therapy require long-duration (potentially lifetime) safety follow-up, off-target editing surveillance, and immunogenicity monitoring -- capabilities GPS must build or acquire.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Lilly operates through:
1. **Global Patient Safety** -- Asset-level B/R profile maintenance and evaluation
2. **GMQO (Regulatory and Safety)** -- Integrated quality oversight ensuring B/R assessments meet regulatory standards
3. **Therapeutic Area Leadership** -- CSO and TA heads provide portfolio-level B/R governance
4. **Board and Executive Management** -- Annual extended strategy review includes product pipeline progress and risk assessment, with ongoing revisiting throughout the year

The metabolic/obesity franchise (Mounjaro/Zepbound, orforglipron, retatrutide) creates a uniquely high-stakes B/R environment -- these drugs are prescribed to tens of millions of patients, making even rare safety signals enormously consequential for regulatory standing and market access. In 2026 this environment intensifies on three fronts simultaneously: (1) **orforglipron's PDUFA decision** (April 10, 2026) makes oral GLP-1 B/R assessment the single most commercially consequential regulatory milestone in pharma -- the B/R profile must be quantified precisely for the FDA advisory process and for competitive positioning against Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide (Wegovy pill launched at $149/month); (2) **retatrutide's dysesthesia signal** (20.9% at 12 mg in TRIUMPH-4) requires B/R governance to adjudicate a novel, dose-dependent safety finding across 6 remaining TRIUMPH readouts before NDA submission, with no analogous precedent in the class; (3) the **five acquisitions across novel modalities** (gene editing, gene therapy, NLRP3 inhibitors, PI3Ka oncology, non-opioid pain) each bring fundamentally different B/R frameworks -- gene editing (Verve) requires multigenerational off-target assessment, gene therapy (Adverum) requires decade-scale durability and immunogenicity monitoring, and NLRP3 inhibitors (Ventyx) require cardiometabolic-inflammatory B/R integration. The breadth and novelty of Lilly's current B/R challenge set is arguably unmatched in the industry.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Enter through the convergence of three simultaneous, time-pressured B/R challenges -- orforglipron launch, retatrutide dysesthesia signal management, and novel modality integration -- positioning ArcaScience as the only platform capable of handling Lilly's B/R complexity at this scale and velocity.**

Eli Lilly faces the most complex B/R environment in the pharmaceutical industry in 2026. The metabolic/obesity franchise ($36.5B from tirzepatide alone) is prescribed to tens of millions of patients. Orforglipron's PDUFA target (April 10, 2026) makes oral GLP-1 B/R assessment an imminent regulatory decision. Retatrutide's Phase III dysesthesia signal (20.9% at 12 mg) requires novel safety characterization across 6 remaining TRIUMPH readouts. And five acquisitions across gene editing, gene therapy, oncology, pain, and immunology each demand fundamentally different B/R frameworks. The narrative should emphasize:

1. **Orforglipron launch B/R readiness** -- The PDUFA target of April 10, 2026 makes this the most commercially consequential B/R assessment in pharma right now. ArcaScience's continuous BRA platform can support the B/R evidence package for the FDA decision, the competitive positioning against Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide (Wegovy pill at $149/month), and the post-marketing surveillance infrastructure needed for immediate launch ($1.5B pre-launch inventory already stockpiled). The compressed timeline from the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher program means Lilly cannot afford gaps in its B/R assessment capability.
2. **Retatrutide dysesthesia signal as a proof case** -- The dysesthesia signal (8.8% at 9 mg, 20.9% at 12 mg, vs. 0.7% placebo) is a textbook use case for dynamic B/R monitoring: a novel safety finding not seen in Phase 2, unique to the triple agonist mechanism (likely glucagon receptor-mediated), dose-dependent, and requiring characterization across 6 remaining Phase III trials before NDA submission (expected late 2026). ArcaScience can demonstrate value by providing a quantified, continuously-updated B/R framework for this specific signal -- showing exactly how dysesthesia trades off against retatrutide's 28.7% weight loss and 75.8% OA pain reduction at each dose level.
3. **Population-scale franchise B/R coherence** -- With 11 obesity drugs in the pipeline spanning different mechanisms (GLP-1, GIP/GLP-1 dual, GIP/GLP-1/glucagon triple) and delivery modes (injectable, oral), Lilly needs a unified B/R assessment platform that can compare and contrast safety profiles across the franchise, detect class-level signals, and differentiate molecule-specific risks.
4. **Regulatory defense of a $36.5B franchise** -- Any safety signal affecting the GLP-1 class (cardiovascular, pancreatitis, thyroid, suicidality concerns, muscle loss) demands immediate, data-driven B/R response. A dynamic BRA platform enables Lilly to present quantified B/R evidence to regulators in real time, protecting the franchise. With Medicare coverage beginning as early as April 1, 2026 ($50/month for Zepbound and orforglipron), the patient population will expand further, increasing PV volume exponentially.
5. **Novel modality B/R framework gap** -- The five acquisitions (Verve gene editing, Adverum gene therapy, Ventyx NLRP3, Scorpion PI3Ka, SiteOne non-opioid pain) create an entirely new category of B/R challenge. Gene editing (VERVE-102) requires long-duration off-target surveillance with no established PV playbook. Gene therapy requires decade-scale monitoring. NLRP3 inhibitors require integration of cardiometabolic and inflammatory B/R frameworks. ArcaScience can position as the platform that scales across all these modalities, providing Lilly with a single B/R infrastructure for its entire portfolio evolution.
6. **Non-obesity portfolio support** -- CSO Skovronsky has stated explicit focus on oncology, neuroscience, and immunology investment. These programs (Kisunla for Alzheimer's, POINT radiopharmaceuticals, Orna in vivo CAR-T) have complex safety profiles that require sophisticated B/R tools distinct from the metabolic franchise.

**Primary entry point: Chief AI Officer (Fuchs).** He provides the centralized, enterprise-level evaluation pathway for AI-powered tools. The NVIDIA collaboration, TuneLab platform, and stated 2.4M AI-hours-saved target create natural touchpoints. Position ArcaScience as a specialized BRA complement to TuneLab (which focuses on drug discovery), not a competitor.

**Secondary entry point: Head of Metabolic/Obesity Development** via the retatrutide dysesthesia signal. Offer a targeted pilot to demonstrate dynamic B/R characterization of the dysesthesia signal across TRIUMPH readouts -- this creates immediate, tangible value and a proof case that can expand to the broader franchise and portfolio.

**Tertiary entry point: GPS/Novel Modalities leadership** via the gene editing/gene therapy B/R framework gap. The Verve and Adverum acquisitions create a need for long-duration safety monitoring capabilities that GPS has not historically required. ArcaScience can offer to co-develop the B/R monitoring framework for these novel modalities, establishing deep integration early.
