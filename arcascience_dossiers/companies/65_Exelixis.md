# Exelixis

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $1.81 billion in net product revenues (up from $1.63B in 2023); additional milestone revenues from Ipsen partnership
- **Headquarters:** Alameda, California, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Oncology — renal cell carcinoma (RCC), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), medullary thyroid cancer (MTC), neuroendocrine tumors (NET), colorectal cancer (CRC), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), ovarian/prostate/breast cancer (BRCA-mutant tumors)
- **Pipeline Size:** Cabozantinib franchise (CABOMETYX/COMETRIQ) with multiple indication expansions; zanzalintinib (next-gen TKI) in 6 Phase 3 pivotal trials; XL309 (USP1 inhibitor, Phase 1); XB010 (Phase 1); XL495 (Phase 1); partnerships with Merck, Bristol Myers Squibb, Ipsen
- **Strategic Priorities:** Multi-product/multi-franchise oncology business build-out, zanzalintinib development acceleration, cabozantinib label expansion (NET approval March 2025), cabozantinib patent estate protection through 2030, partnership leverage (Merck, BMS), early-stage pipeline optimization, corporate restructuring for efficiency

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Exelixis has built integrated research, development, and commercial capabilities focused exclusively on oncology. Clinical development centers on two major programs: the cabozantinib franchise (ongoing label expansions) and zanzalintinib (next-gen TKI with 6 Phase 3 pivotal trials). The company employs a strategy of rapidly profiling early-stage compounds and advancing only those with the highest probability of success. Key clinical collaborations include Merck (zanzalintinib + pembrolizumab/belzutifan in RCC and HNSCC) and BMS (cabozantinib + nivolumab/ipilimumab). The 13% workforce reduction in early 2024 was aimed at prioritizing pipeline advancement.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages FDA, EMA, MHRA, and PMDA interactions for cabozantinib and the expanding pipeline. The FDA approved CABOMETYX for neuroendocrine tumors in March 2025 with orphan drug designation. The regulatory team manages sNDA/sBLA filings for label expansions and coordinates global regulatory strategy with partners (Ipsen for ex-US cabozantinib territories). Zanzalintinib's 6 Phase 3 trials require significant regulatory planning for multiple indication-specific submissions.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV supports CABOMETYX and COMETRIQ across multiple approved indications worldwide. Cabozantinib's broad oncology use (RCC, HCC, MTC, NET) creates diverse safety monitoring requirements. The Ipsen partnership means PV coordination across territories. Zanzalintinib's Phase 3 programs generate expanding safety databases. Combination therapy trials (with checkpoint inhibitors) require monitoring for overlapping toxicity profiles.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance at Exelixis is driven by the multi-indication development strategy for cabozantinib and zanzalintinib. Each new indication expansion requires B/R evaluation specific to the tumor type, line of therapy, and combination partner. The early-stage pipeline strategy of "advancing only those with the highest probability of success" is an explicit B/R-gated portfolio approach. Zanzalintinib's 6 concurrent Phase 3 trials require portfolio-level B/R resource allocation decisions.
