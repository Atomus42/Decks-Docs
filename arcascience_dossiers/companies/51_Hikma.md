# Hikma Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2025):** $3.35 billion (up 6% YoY from $3.13B in FY2024); FY2026 guidance of 2-4% group revenue growth with core operating profit of $720-770M
- **Headquarters:** London, United Kingdom (founded in Amman, Jordan)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Injectables (generic and specialty), generics (oral solids, nasal sprays), branded pharmaceuticals (MENA region), oncology, anti-infectives, CNS, metabolic diseases, biosimilars (denosumab, ustekinumab, liraglutide), ready-to-use (RTU) hospital products
- **Pipeline Size:** 84 products launched in 2025; ~15 RTU products in pipeline (launches expected early 2028); active pipeline in biosimilars, long-acting injectables, complex generics, nasal sprays, and nasal epinephrine; first generic GLP-1 (liraglutide) approved and launched
- **Strategic Priorities:** Differentiated generics portfolio expansion, biosimilar franchise build-out (STARJEMZA, Enoby/Xtrenbo denosumab biosimilars, liraglutide), US manufacturing investment ($1B by 2030 across Ohio and New Jersey sites), MENA branded growth (became #1 pharma company by sales in MENA in 2025), RTU platform as long-term injectables growth driver, digital transformation of manufacturing and supply chain, TYZAVAN launch, $250M share buyback program
- **Recent Leadership Overhaul (Dec 2025-Feb 2026):** CEO Riad Mishlawi stepped down Dec 2025; Executive Chairman Said Darwazah resumed CEO role, subsequently relinquished chairmanship to focus on CEO for two years; Victoria Hull appointed Chair; Mazen Darwazah named Deputy CEO (MENA); Khalid Nabilsi named Deputy CEO (North America & Europe) while stepping down as CFO; company withdrew medium-term guidance and Injectables margin targets pending strategic review

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Hikma's clinical development is organized within a centralized R&D function under the Chief Scientific Officer (CSO) & Global Head of R&D, currently Shahin Fesharaki. R&D is segmented across the three business lines: Injectables R&D (with a new Zagreb R&D centre), Generics R&D (focused on complex and differentiated products, headquartered at the Columbus, Ohio facility), and Branded R&D (focused on first-to-file opportunities in MENA). Hikma operates 8 R&D centres worldwide. Clinical development primarily involves bioequivalence studies and regulatory filings for generic/biosimilar products rather than large-scale novel drug clinical trials. **2025-2026 Update:** The RTU (ready-to-use) platform has emerged as a significant clinical development priority, with ~15 RTU products in the pipeline and launches expected beginning early 2028. An additional FDA-required study for nasal epinephrine is ongoing, with US submission expected in 2026 and European filings in progress. The company is also leveraging AI in drug discovery, manufacturing, and supply chain operations, with particular focus on generative AI for de novo drug design and real-world evidence (RWE) in regulatory submissions.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs is embedded within each business segment (Injectables, Generics, Branded) with centralized strategic oversight. The company submitted 137 regulatory filings across all markets in 2024 and manages regulatory interactions with the FDA, EMA, and MENA health authorities. Given the generics/biosimilars focus, regulatory strategy centers on ANDA/505(b)(2) filings in the US, and local regulatory submissions in MENA and Europe. **2025-2026 Update:** The regulatory function secured three major FDA biosimilar approvals in 2025: STARJEMZA (ustekinumab-hmny, biosimilar to Stelara, May 2025), Enoby and Xtrenbo (denosumab-qbde, biosimilars to Prolia and Xgeva respectively, September 2025), and the first approved ANDA for a generic GLP-1 (liraglutide, referencing Victoza). The accelerating pace of biosimilar and complex generic approvals across multiple therapeutic areas (immunology, oncology/bone health, metabolic) significantly expands the regulatory affairs workload and multi-jurisdictional coordination requirements. Nasal epinephrine was filed in the UK and will be filed in Europe, with out-licensing discussions underway.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Hikma maintains a global pharmacovigilance function supporting its portfolio of marketed products across approximately 150 countries, with PrimeVigilance providing global-scale PV services. Safety monitoring is particularly important for its biosimilar products and its growing injectables franchise, which includes hospital-critical products. PV operations span post-marketing surveillance, adverse event reporting, and periodic safety update reports across multiple regulatory jurisdictions. **2025-2026 Update:** The PV burden has materially expanded with three new biosimilar FDA approvals in 2025 (STARJEMZA/ustekinumab, Enoby and Xtrenbo/denosumab, and liraglutide), each bringing distinct post-marketing safety monitoring requirements across different therapeutic areas (immunology, oncology/bone health, metabolic/GLP-1). The TYZAVAN (vancomycin RTU) launch adds hospital-setting PV complexity. With 84 products launched in 2025 and the company now the largest pharmaceutical company in MENA by sales, the PV function faces scaling challenges across an increasingly diverse and global product portfolio.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk decision-making at Hikma is integrated within the product development lifecycle, particularly for biosimilar and complex generic programs where demonstrating equivalence is central. The Board and its Audit Committee provide oversight on risk management. Given Hikma's expansion into biosimilars and complex injectables, benefit-risk governance is becoming increasingly important, though it may not yet be formalized as a standalone governance body.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Hikma's biosimilar and complex injectable development programs, where benefit-risk assessment is most critical for regulatory success. The entry point is the growing biosimilar portfolio (STARJEMZA approval as proof of need) and the 137+ annual regulatory filings across multiple jurisdictions. Position the BRA platform as a tool that can standardize and accelerate benefit-risk assessments across Hikma's multi-jurisdictional filing strategy (FDA, EMA, MENA authorities), reducing regulatory risk and time-to-market. The $1B US manufacturing investment signals long-term commitment to complex products that will require increasingly sophisticated regulatory and safety infrastructure. A pilot project focused on the biosimilar pipeline or the nasal spray franchise (where clinical evidence integration matters) would demonstrate value before scaling across the enterprise. Engage through the Zagreb or Columbus R&D centers where biosimilar and complex generic development is most active.
