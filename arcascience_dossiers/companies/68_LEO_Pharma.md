# LEO Pharma

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** DKK 12.4 billion (~$1.7 billion; reported growth 9%, dermatology portfolio +12% CER); North America +35% CER; adjusted EBITDA up 43% to DKK 895M
- **Headquarters:** Ballerup (Copenhagen area), Denmark (co-owned by LEO Foundation and Nordic Capital)
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Medical dermatology — atopic dermatitis, chronic hand eczema, psoriasis, congenital ichthyosis, generalized pustular psoriasis (GPP), inflammatory skin conditions; also thrombosis and coagulation
- **Pipeline Size:** Adbry/Adtralza (tralokinumab, IL-13 inhibitor, now largest product), Anzupgo (delgocitinib, JAK inhibitor for CHE, EU approved, US NDA filed), TMB-001 (congenital ichthyosis), temtokibart (IL-22RA1 for AD, Phase 2A), Gilead partnership (oral STAT6, up to $1.7B), Boehringer partnership (spesolimab for GPP, $105M upfront)
- **Strategic Priorities:** Multi-year transformation toward sustainable profitable growth, dermatology pipeline advancement, Adbry/Adtralza global expansion (20 markets, 69% revenue growth CER), Anzupgo US launch, profitability improvement (EBITDA margin from -17% in 2021 to 7% in 2024, targeting 15-18% in 2025), strategic partnerships (Gilead, Boehringer, ICON)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
LEO Pharma's R&D is organized into two distinct areas: Research and Early Development, and Development, designed to optimize both early-stage and late-stage pipeline activities. This structure reflects a strategic decision to separate early innovation from late-stage execution. The company has a strategic CRO partnership with ICON for clinical trial execution in medical dermatology. Clinical development focuses on innovative biologics (tralokinumab, temtokibart), small molecules (delgocitinib), and topical therapies (TMB-001). Partnerships bring additional programs: Gilead (oral STAT6 for AD) and Boehringer (spesolimab for GPP).

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs has delivered significant recent milestones: EC approval of Anzupgo (delgocitinib) for chronic hand eczema, FDA NDA filing for delgocitinib, Adbry/Adtralza regulatory management across 20+ markets. The team navigates dermatology-specific regulatory considerations including topical formulation requirements, steroid-free positioning, and patient-reported outcome endpoints. The EADV congress program (5 late-breaking abstracts, 23 posters) demonstrates active engagement with the scientific and regulatory community.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV supports a portfolio serving close to 100 million patients annually in over 70 countries. The commercial portfolio includes both chronic therapies (dermatology) and acute products (thrombosis). Adbry/Adtralza's rapid growth (69% revenue growth, expanding to 20 markets) creates scaling PV requirements. Novel mechanisms (IL-13 inhibition, JAK inhibition, IL-22RA1 targeting) require mechanism-specific safety monitoring. The Anzupgo (JAK inhibitor) program requires particular PV attention given the FDA's boxed warning requirements for JAK inhibitors.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is critical for LEO Pharma given the competitive dermatology landscape where multiple biologics and JAK inhibitors compete for market share. Differentiation is driven by B/R profiles: Adbry positions on IL-13 selectivity vs. dupilumab (IL-4/IL-13), and Anzupgo (topical JAK inhibitor) must demonstrate favorable B/R vs. systemic JAK inhibitors and other CHE treatments. The DELTA FORCE trial data showing delgocitinib superiority over systemic treatment in severe CHE exemplifies B/R-driven competitive positioning.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target LEO Pharma's competitive positioning challenge in the crowded dermatology biologic market. With Adbry/Adtralza competing against dupilumab (Dupixent), JAK inhibitors, and emerging IL-13/IL-31/OX40 therapies, B/R differentiation is the key competitive lever. The BRA platform can provide continuous B/R profiling that supports market access dossiers and payer engagement, demonstrating Adbry's B/R advantages. The Anzupgo (topical JAK inhibitor) US launch is another high-value entry point where the FDA's heightened scrutiny of JAK inhibitors (boxed warning context) makes rigorous B/R documentation essential. Position ArcaScience as enabling LEO Pharma to win on B/R data in a market where multiple similar mechanisms compete. The Gilead partnership (oral STAT6 for AD, up to $1.7B deal) represents a future pipeline program where BRA could be embedded from Phase 2 onward. Engage through the Development organization or the EVP Global Therapeutic & Value Strategy, who drives competitive B/R positioning.
