# Regeneron

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $14.2B (8% increase YoY)
- **Headquarters:** Tarrytown, New York, USA
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Immunology/inflammation (Dupixent), ophthalmology (Eylea/Eylea HD), oncology/hematology (Libtayo, bispecific antibodies), cardiovascular/metabolic, rare diseases, obesity (entering via partnership), genetic medicine
- **Pipeline Size:** ~45 product candidates in clinical development; 18 additional Phase 3 studies initiated; pursuing therapies across 30+ cancer types; $5.1B R&D investment in 2024 (~$6B planned for 2026)
- **Strategic Priorities:** Dupixent franchise expansion (COPD, CSU, bullous pemphigoid); Libtayo expansion (adjuvant CSCC); bispecific antibody platform (linvoseltamab for myeloma, odronextamab for lymphoma, fianlimab for melanoma); obesity market entry (GLP-1/GIP agonist licensed from Hansoh); Regeneron Genetics Center (23andMe acquisition); Regeneron Cell Medicines; complement-mediated diseases (pozelimab/cemdisiran); massive capital investment ($7B U.S. capex for manufacturing/research)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Regeneron's R&D organization is founder-led, with the company's Chief Scientific Officer and co-founder providing strategic R&D direction. Clinical development is organized across six therapeutic areas with approximately 45 clinical-stage programs. The company operates through a highly integrated "bench-to-bedside" model where its proprietary technology platforms (VelocImmune, Regeneron Genetics Center) feed directly into clinical development. The Regeneron Genetics Center (RGC), which has sequenced nearly 3 million samples and acquired 23andMe, provides genetic validation for clinical targets. Clinical operations span immunology, oncology/hematology, ophthalmology, cardiovascular, rare diseases, and now obesity. The company recently created Regeneron Cell Medicines through the acquisition of 2seventy bio.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages a diverse filing portfolio across multiple therapeutic areas. The regulatory function has managed significant recent approvals: Dupixent in COPD, CSU, and bullous pemphigoid; Libtayo in adjuvant CSCC; and multiple BLA resubmissions (linvoseltamab, odronextamab). The bispecific antibody platform creates unique regulatory challenges -- each program targets different receptor combinations with distinct safety profiles. Regulatory strategy must also navigate the Sanofi partnership for Dupixent (shared regulatory responsibilities) while managing fully owned programs. The entry into obesity (via Hansoh partnership) introduces new regulatory territory for Regeneron.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV at Regeneron manages safety across a portfolio anchored by Dupixent ($14.15B in global sales), one of the most widely prescribed biologics in the world, plus Eylea/Eylea HD (ophthalmology), Libtayo (oncology), and emerging bispecific antibodies. Dupixent's expansion into COPD adds a large new patient population requiring safety monitoring. The bispecific antibody programs (linvoseltamab, odronextamab) have distinct safety profiles including CRS risk. The Sanofi partnership for Dupixent means PV governance involves inter-company coordination. The 23andMe acquisition and RGC data create potential for genetically informed pharmacovigilance -- understanding which patient subpopulations may be at higher risk for adverse events.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance is embedded within the R&D leadership structure, with the science-driven founder culture ensuring deep scientific rigor in B/R assessments. The company's genetic medicine capabilities (RGC, 23andMe) enable uniquely data-driven B/R analysis by linking genetic risk to clinical outcomes. B/R decisions span the full spectrum from high-safety-bar immunology (Dupixent in mild allergic conditions) to high-risk-tolerance oncology (bispecific antibodies in refractory cancers). The entry into obesity adds another high-safety-bar indication where population-level B/R is critical.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should position around Regeneron's unique genetics-driven R&D model. The combination of the Regeneron Genetics Center (3M+ samples), 23andMe data, and clinical development data creates an unprecedented opportunity for genetically informed benefit-risk assessment. The pitch: ArcaScience can integrate genetic risk stratification data from RGC with clinical trial and real-world B/R data to create the first truly precision B/R platform -- enabling Regeneron to predict which patient subpopulations will derive the greatest benefit and which may face elevated risk. For the Dupixent franchise ($14B+), this means moving from population-average B/R to subgroup-specific B/R as the drug expands into increasingly diverse patient populations (COPD, CSU, bullous pemphigoid). For the bispecific antibody platform, genetically informed B/R can help predict CRS risk and optimize patient selection. The immediate entry point should be the bispecific antibody B/R challenge: Regeneron is developing three CD3-engaging bispecific antibodies simultaneously (linvoseltamab, odronextamab, fianlimab+cemiplimab), each with distinct but related safety profiles. ArcaScience can model the platform-level B/R of CD3 engagement to optimize dosing and patient selection across all three programs.
