# Kedrion Biopharma

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 1.578B (~$1.7B; 10% YoY growth; EBITDA EUR 232M up 28%; adjusted EBITDA EUR 279M up 20%)
- **Headquarters:** Barga (Castelvecchio Pascoli), Tuscany, Italy
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Plasma-derived therapies — immunodeficiencies (immunoglobulins including Yimmugo), coagulation disorders/hemophilia (factor concentrates), neurological disorders, Rh sensitization (Anti-D Immunoglobulin/RhoGAM), critical care (Albumin), ultra-rare diseases (aceruloplasminemia — EMA Orphan Drug Designation)
- **Pipeline Size:** Expanding pipeline including Yimmugo (FDA-approved June 2024, US launch underway), orphan drug for aceruloplasminemia (EMA Orphan Drug Designation), novel proteins from plasma waste fractions, proteomics-driven rare disease discovery; manufacturing: 68 plasma collection centers in US, 8 in Czech Republic, 7 production facilities across 5 countries; ~5,200 employees; presence in 100+ countries
- **Strategic Priorities:** Double-digit revenue growth continuation; Yimmugo global rollout (EU + US launch); orphan disease portfolio expansion; Net Zero environmental plan (SBTi alignment); unified brand identity across affiliates; plasma collection expansion; US market penetration; Permira/Marcucci family ownership driving growth investment

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Kedrion's clinical development focuses on plasma-derived product development, which involves fractionation process optimization, clinical safety and efficacy studies for new products, and lifecycle management for existing therapies. The Yimmugo program (developed by partner Biotest, commercialized by Kedrion in the US) demonstrates ability to manage complex clinical-to-commercial transitions. The orphan drug program for aceruloplasminemia represents a move into ultra-rare disease clinical development. Novel protein discovery from plasma waste fractions and proteomics-driven approaches indicate expanding R&D ambitions.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages filings across FDA, EMA, and 100+ country markets. The team navigated both FDA approval for Yimmugo (June 2024) and EMA Orphan Drug Designation for aceruloplasminemia. Plasma-derived product regulatory pathways involve specialized requirements including donor screening, manufacturing process validation, and viral safety assurance. The global regulatory portfolio spanning 100+ countries requires multi-jurisdictional capability.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV covers a portfolio of plasma-derived therapies used in critical care and chronic treatment settings. Plasma product safety monitoring has unique requirements including traceability from donor to patient, viral transmission risk monitoring, and batch-specific safety tracking. The expanding global footprint (100+ countries) adds multi-jurisdictional safety reporting complexity. Yimmugo US launch creates new post-marketing surveillance obligations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance for plasma-derived products involves unique considerations: limited ability to conduct traditional randomized controlled trials, reliance on manufacturing process consistency for product quality/safety, and donor pool management affecting supply and product characteristics. The decision to pursue orphan drug development (aceruloplasminemia) reflects B/R-driven portfolio expansion into ultra-rare indications where plasma-derived therapies have novel applications.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
Kedrion's growth ambitions (10%+ revenue growth, Yimmugo US launch, orphan disease expansion, 100+ country footprint) require increasingly sophisticated B/R governance for a specialized plasma-derived product portfolio. ArcaScience should position the BRA platform for: (1) Yimmugo global launch support — integrating clinical evidence, post-marketing safety data, and real-world evidence across the multi-country rollout to continuously optimize the B/R profile, (2) orphan disease portfolio expansion — aceruloplasminemia and future ultra-rare indications identified through proteomics require robust B/R assessment where patient populations are extremely small and traditional clinical evidence is limited, and (3) plasma product safety governance — adapting the platform to incorporate the unique safety dimensions of plasma-derived products (donor pool management, viral safety, batch traceability) alongside traditional clinical evidence. The Permira PE ownership suggests investment appetite for tools that support the growth thesis. A pilot focused on the Yimmugo US launch (where FDA post-marketing expectations are well-defined) would demonstrate platform value in a concrete, time-bound context.
