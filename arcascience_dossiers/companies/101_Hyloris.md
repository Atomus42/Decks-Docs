# Hyloris Pharmaceuticals

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 9.76 million (+160% YoY); total revenue and other income exceeding EUR 10 million (+138%). H1 2025 royalties: EUR 2.9 million (+30% YoY). TTM revenue (June 2025): ~$7.96M. Net loss FY2024: EUR 6.3 million (lowest since IPO, down 59% from 2023). Pre-profitability — net losses since inception.
- **Headquarters:** Legiapark, Liège, Belgium (Wallonia, French-speaking region). Part of the BioWin health cluster.
- **Stock:** Euronext Brussels: HYL. Market cap ~$157M (January 2025). IPO June 2020 (raised ~EUR 61.8M).
- **Cash Position:** EUR 18.6M as of June 30, 2025. Equity base EUR 28.9M. No dividend. Board believes cash is sufficient with commercialized product revenues + expected out-licensing proceeds.
- **Employees:** ~50 people, 11 nationalities represented.
- **Corporate Identity:** Name stands for **"High Yield, Lower Risk"** — reflecting the 505(b)(2) regulatory strategy.
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Cardiovascular (core: atrial fibrillation, heart failure, acute coronary syndrome), Pain Management (non-opioid analgesics), Anti-Infectives/Antiviral, Women's Health, Rare Disease (HAT/sleeping sickness, orphan drugs), Neurology/CNS (ADHD), Urology/Bladder, Veterinary.
- **Pipeline Size:** 26 products and candidates (including 3 commercialized) as of mid-2025, targeting 30 by year-end 2025.
- **Business Model:** Value-added medicines via 505(b)(2) regulatory pathway. Reformulate and repurpose well-known pharmaceuticals with established safety/efficacy profiles. Target development cost: <EUR 7M per product. Capital-light: does not manufacture or commercialize directly — out-licenses to partners. R&D lab at Legiapark (operational since late 2022) for formulation and analytical work.
- **Key 2025 Developments:** (1) Tranexamic Acid RTU FDA approved June 2025 (partner: Avenacy); (2) Kuvatris/Suramin IV partnership June 2025 (19.75% equity, PRV potential ~$150M+); (3) Orion partnership for TXA RTU Europe October 2025; (4) Aspirin IV positive pivotal results late 2025; (5) Ondansetron ER (RHB-102) licensed from RedHill February 2025; (6) Stijn Van Rompay appointed sole CEO November 2025.
- **Strategic Priorities:** Shift toward proprietary, internally-originated, patent-protected reformulated and repurposed products (moving up from high-barrier generics); build toward 30 diversified assets; self-commercialization under evaluation for cardiovascular portfolio (though current preference is out-licensing); expand royalty base from commercialized products; strengthen governance post-2024 forensic review.

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Hyloris operates as a **lean clinical trial sponsor**, outsourcing execution to CROs and development partners. With ~50 employees, the company does not run clinical trials in-house. Clinical programs are designed to be capital-efficient under the 505(b)(2) framework: bioequivalence studies, PK bridging studies, and limited efficacy trials rather than full Phase 1-3 programs. Clinical trial scale is typically small (e.g., Alenura Phase 2: 120 patients, 4-arm adaptive design).

**Key clinical leadership:**
- **Dr. Dietmar Aichhorn (COO):** Oversees technical development, clinical development, and regulatory affairs across US, EU, and other geographies. 20+ years at Sandoz, Mylan, Innovacell, ViraTherapeutics, Polpharma Biologics. Chemical engineer (Linz), Doctorate in Strategic Management (WU Vienna).
- **Christophe Lyssens (VP Clinical Affairs):** Oversees clinical trial execution. Background: Optimapharm (SVP Strategy), Covance (Country Head Greece), Ablynx, SGS Life Sciences. Founded Simak bv (drug development consulting). Expert in CRO management, clinical outsourcing, ICH-GCP.

Co-development partnerships for clinical execution: AFT Pharmaceuticals (HY-091 VLS, HY-090 Burning Mouth Syndrome, HY-094 injectable iron), Rhoshan Pharmaceuticals (Aspirin IV pivotal study organized by Rhoshan), Kuvatris Therapeutics (Suramin IV Phase 3).

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Hyloris's regulatory strategy centers on the **505(b)(2) NDA pathway** in the US and **hybrid applications (Article 10(3) of Directive 2001/83/EC)** in the EU. The company relies on existing RLD safety/efficacy data to reduce clinical burden. Secondary pathway: ANDA for select products (Podofilox Gel, Tranexamic Acid RTU). Dual-track strategy: US first (505(b)(2) NDA), then international filings. Some products (HY-088) target EU first.

No dedicated large-scale regulatory affairs division — regulatory strategy is managed by the COO's team with external support. Regulatory interactions include pre-NDA meetings with FDA. Manufacturing is outsourced to CMOs; the Valacyclovir experience (Greek manufacturer rated OAI by FDA, blocking approval beyond October 2025 PDUFA date) highlights vulnerability to partner manufacturing site inspections.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
**Hyloris does not have a dedicated pharmacovigilance organization.** With only 3 commercialized products and a small employee base, PV responsibilities are managed through commercial partners (Hikma, AltaThera, Padagis, Avenacy, Orion) who handle post-market safety surveillance for their respective territories. This is standard for a specialty pharma company of this size using an out-licensing model.

**Implication for ArcaScience:** Traditional PV/BRA positioning is irrelevant. ArcaScience must be positioned as BD decision infrastructure — the analytical engine for asset identification and prioritization — not as a safety monitoring or signal detection tool.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Following the 2024 governance restructuring, Hyloris has established a formal **Product Selection Committee** as part of enhanced corporate governance. This committee evaluates new product candidates for inclusion in the pipeline. The committee requires structured rationale for candidate selection decisions — creating a natural fit for ArcaScience's structured B/R assessment outputs.

Board composition includes Non-Executive Chairman Stefan Yee (30+ years audit, M&A, corporate finance), Dr. Melanie Mestdagt (CEO of EyeD Pharma, Board member of AWEX, VP of BioWin), and Leon Van Rompay (40+ years pharma, founder of Docpharma).

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Christophe Maréchal (CFO) has facilitated the introduction and existing relationship. Nicolaj Donskov Nielsen (VP Portfolio Management) is the operational buyer who will evaluate day-to-day value. The entry conversation goes through Maréchal to Nielsen.
- **Scientific Validator:** Christophe Lyssens (VP Clinical Affairs) for clinical trial design value; Dr. Dietmar Aichhorn (COO) for regulatory pathway alignment and technical evaluation.
- **Budget Owner:** CEO Stijn Van Rompay for strategic vendor decisions; CFO Christophe Maréchal for financial approval. At EUR 75K-100K PoC level, this likely requires CEO + CFO joint approval given Hyloris's cash-conscious posture.
- **Procurement Cycle:** 4-8 weeks. Hyloris is a small organization with short decision chains. No formal procurement department. The Product Selection Committee and Board would likely be informed but not directly involved in vendor selection. Key risk: capital conservation instinct may delay discretionary technology spending.
- **Internal Friction Points:** (1) Prior negative feedback ("not ready for clinical trial setup") creates skepticism barrier — must be addressed directly. (2) Cash-conscious posture — EUR 18.6M cash with ongoing R&D burn means every expenditure is scrutinized. (3) Small team bandwidth — even evaluating a new vendor consumes scarce management attention. (4) Hyloris's out-licensing model means much of the downstream regulatory/PV work is handled by partners — the BD scouting/prioritization use case must be clearly articulated as distinct from PV/regulatory tools. (5) Potential "not invented here" resistance from BD team accustomed to manual processes.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
**Recommended approach: Enter through CFO Christophe Maréchal's existing relationship, positioning as BD decision infrastructure for Hyloris's proprietary development pivot — with the glioblastoma drug screening case study as the primary proof point and cardiovascular portfolio as the recommended PoC area.**

**Why now (Q1-Q2 2026 is the optimal window):**
- **Strategic pivot in progress:** Stijn Van Rompay's appointment as sole CEO (November 2025) consolidates leadership for the proprietary development shift. The organization is actively building internal asset identification capabilities.
- **Portfolio expansion pressure:** Target of 30 assets requires continuous identification of new candidates. The BD team's manual processes do not scale to this target.
- **Product Selection Committee governance:** The new committee requires structured rationale for candidate selection — ArcaScience provides exactly this.
- **Multiple NDA filings approaching:** Dofetilide IV (in preparation), Aspirin IV (targeted 2026), Atomoxetine (H2 2025) — the 505(b)(2) benefit-risk evidence structuring capability has immediate application.
- **Post-governance-crisis professionalization:** Hyloris is in a "reset and professionalize" phase following the 2024 forensic review. Structured, evidence-based decision-making tools align with this organizational maturation.

**The narrative should center on:**

1. **The proprietary development enabler** — As Hyloris shifts from licensing pre-identified assets to internally identifying reformulation candidates, the analytical burden shifts to the BD team. ArcaScience provides the systematic screening and evidence structuring capability that large pharma BD teams have — without requiring Hyloris to hire 10 additional analysts.
2. **The glioblastoma proof point** — ArcaScience screened 100 marketed drugs and identified 3 candidates for glioblastoma repurposing (2 now in Phase 2). This is structurally identical to Hyloris's BD scouting: screening marketed drugs for reformulation potential using AE data as the screening signal.
3. **The 505(b)(2) evidence thread** — ArcaScience outputs map directly to eCTD Module 2.5.6 requirements. The scouting evidence becomes the filing evidence — no rework, no disconnected processes.
4. **Capital-efficient partner, not enterprise vendor** — EUR 75K-100K PoC is less than two months of a senior BD analyst's fully-loaded cost. Frame as operational expense, not technology investment.
5. **Addressing the prior objection honestly** — "We were positioning as clinical trial support; we now recognize the highest-value application for Hyloris is upstream, in asset identification and prioritization."

**Conference and network touchpoints:** BioWin health cluster events (Hyloris is a member), Belgian pharma networking (Liège-based ecosystem — both Hyloris and Mithra/Neuralis where Maréchal has connections), Euronext Brussels small-cap pharma community. The Christophe Maréchal relationship is the primary entry — no cold outreach needed.
