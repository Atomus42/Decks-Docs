# Servier

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** EUR 6.9B (~$8.2B; FY2024-25 fiscal year)
- **Headquarters:** Suresnes (Paris area), France
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Oncology (32% of revenue, 55% growth), cardiovascular/cardiometabolic (43% of revenue), neurology (emerging pillar), generics (Biogaran subsidiary, potential divestiture)
- **Pipeline Size:** 62 projects total (40 in clinical development, 22 in research); 30 oncology R&D projects including 11 potential first-in-class treatments; Biogaran covers 80% of upcoming patent expirations in generics
- **Strategic Priorities:** EUR 10B revenue ambition by 2030; oncology as main growth engine (70% of R&D budget); neurology franchise establishment (rare neurological diseases); Biogaran potential sale to refocus on innovation; AI partnership with Insilico Medicine; Bio-S biologics production unit; CAR-T and cell therapy development (Cellectis/Allogene partnerships)

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Servier's clinical development is organized primarily around oncology (70% of R&D budget) with growing neurology and cardiometabolic programs. The company invests ~20% of brand-name medicine revenue in R&D. As a privately held, foundation-governed company, Servier can take longer-term R&D investment perspectives without quarterly earnings pressure. Clinical programs span hematologic malignancies (Cema-Cel CAR-T with Allogene), solid tumors (RAF/RAS inhibitors from Black Diamond, PKC inhibitor darovasertib from Ideaya), and IDH inhibitors (Voranigo for glioma). The company operates R&D across France, the U.S., and multiple international sites with ~20,000 employees.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages submissions primarily through EMA and FDA, with the U.S. becoming the largest single-country market by brand-name revenue (EUR 879M). The regulatory team navigates both small molecule and emerging biologic/cell therapy pathways. Recent regulatory milestones include Voranigo launch in the U.S. for low-grade glioma, demonstrating strong FDA regulatory capability.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV manages safety across a diverse portfolio spanning cardiovascular (decades of post-marketing data for products like Daflon and hypertension treatments), oncology (growing portfolio with novel mechanisms including IDH inhibitors and CAR-T), and emerging neurology assets. The transition from a cardiovascular-heavy portfolio to an oncology-dominant pipeline requires PV to build new capabilities in oncology-specific safety monitoring (immune-related AEs, cell therapy toxicities).

### Benefit-Risk Governance
As a privately held, foundation-governed company, B/R governance benefits from long-term strategic thinking without public market pressure. B/R decisions are made by R&D leadership with board oversight from the Foundation. The company's dual transformation — from cardiovascular to oncology, and potential Biogaran divestiture — creates complex portfolio-level B/R decisions.

## 4. Buying Pathway Hypothesis
- **Interest Initiator:** Head of Oncology R&D or Head of Regulatory Affairs — 30 oncology programs and 11 first-in-class candidates create strong demand for dynamic B/R tools
- **Scientific Validator:** CMO / Head of R&D — must validate scientific approach for oncology and cell therapy B/R

- **Procurement Cycle:** 6-12 months; as a private French company, Servier can move faster than public companies on strategic investments; European procurement norms apply
- **Internal Friction Points:** Ongoing strategic transformation (Biogaran potential sale, oncology pivot) may consume management attention; traditional cardiovascular heritage culture may resist rapid adoption of novel tools; cell therapy programs (Cellectis/Allogene) involve partner coordination; 70% R&D concentration in oncology means other therapeutic areas may feel underserved

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should target Servier's oncology transformation directly, focusing on the 11 potential first-in-class oncology candidates. The pitch should emphasize: (1) for first-in-class mechanisms (IDH inhibitors, RAF/RAS inhibitors, PKC inhibitors), dynamic B/R assessment is especially valuable because there are no predicate drugs to benchmark against — regulators will demand rigorous, continuously updated B/R evidence, (2) supporting the emerging cell therapy portfolio (CAR-T via Cellectis/Allogene) with B/R frameworks designed for advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), where safety profiles are fundamentally different from small molecules, and (3) enabling probabilistic approval prediction across the 30 oncology programs to optimize R&D resource allocation. Servier's partnership with Insilico Medicine for AI-driven drug discovery creates a natural receptivity to AI-powered tools. The private/foundation governance structure means Servier can make long-term investments without quarterly earnings pressure — ArcaScience should pitch a multi-year strategic partnership rather than a transactional software sale. The EUR 10B by 2030 ambition creates urgency for tools that accelerate pipeline advancement. Engage through ESMO, ASCO, or French pharmaceutical industry events.
