# Sandoz

## 1. Company Overview
- **Revenue (FY2024):** $10.4B (9% growth in constant currencies; biosimilars $2.9B up 30% cc; generics $7.5B up 2% cc)
- **Headquarters:** Basel, Switzerland
- **Therapeutic Focus Areas:** Generics (~1,300 products spanning common cold to cancer), biosimilars (immunology: adalimumab/Hyrimoz, natalizumab/Tyruko; oncology: pembrolizumab and nivolumab in late-stage clinical trials; endocrinology: somatropin/Omnitrope; ophthalmology: ranibizumab/Cimerli; bone: denosumab/Wyost/Jubbonti), antibiotics
- **Pipeline Size:** 28 biosimilar molecules in pipeline; ~450 generic pipeline products; targeting $43B in biosimilar LoE value and $40B in generics LoE value (2024-2028); pembrolizumab and nivolumab biosimilars in late-stage clinical trials; GLP-1 strategy in development; 20,000+ employees; 900M+ patient treatments annually
- **Strategic Priorities:** #1 global biosimilar provider (already #3 in US); biosimilars driving 50% of growth over next 5 years; multi-year transformation program to simplify organization; manufacturing network optimization (from 18 to 15 internal facilities, new biosimilar plant in Slovenia); Novartis spin-off independence (October 2023); GLP-1 biosimilar/generic strategy; pipeline targeting combined $83B in LoE value

## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Sandoz operates a major clinical development organization focused on biosimilar development programs requiring analytical characterization, PK/PD studies, and confirmatory clinical trials. With 28 biosimilar molecules in the pipeline and pembrolizumab/nivolumab in late-stage clinical trials, the clinical team manages multiple concurrent development programs. Clinical development is supported by the new biosimilar development capabilities being built in Slovenia and Germany. The company also manages generic development programs (~450 in pipeline) requiring bioequivalence studies. Post-spin-off from Novartis, Sandoz is building independent clinical development infrastructure.

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Regulatory affairs manages a massive global filing portfolio across generics (~1,300 products) and biosimilars (launched and pipeline). The team has demonstrated regulatory excellence with successful biosimilar approvals across FDA (Hyrimoz, Cimerli) and EMA (Tyruko, Pyzchiva). Regulatory strategy for pembrolizumab and nivolumab biosimilars — targeting >$40B in combined originator sales — represents the most commercially significant biosimilar regulatory programs globally. Multi-market filing capability spans Europe (HQ), North America (#3 US biosimilar), and international markets.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
PV monitors safety for a massive portfolio serving 900M+ patient treatments annually. Biosimilar safety monitoring requires reference product comparison and post-marketing safety surveillance across multiple jurisdictions. The expanding biosimilar portfolio (new launches plus pipeline) increases PV complexity. The antibiotics portfolio (manufacturing in Kundl, Austria) adds specialized antimicrobial resistance monitoring considerations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
B/R governance spans the entire generics and biosimilars portfolio, with strategic importance for biosimilar pipeline investment decisions. The decision to enter the oncology biosimilar space (pembrolizumab, nivolumab) represents a major B/R-driven strategic commitment given the complexity and cost of oncology biosimilar development. Portfolio-level B/R assessment informs the allocation of development resources across 28 biosimilar molecules and 450 generic products.
