## 2. Clinical & Regulatory Organizational Mapping

### Clinical Development Structure
Novo Nordisk completed a major R&D reorganization effective 7 August 2025, merging its Research & Early Development and Development functions into a single combined R&D unit under Chief Scientific Officer Martin Holst Lange (MD, PhD; 20+ year Novo veteran, previously EVP Development). This replaced the prior two separate EVP-level organizations; former CSO Marcus Schindler retired in connection with the restructuring. The reorganization was part of a broader leadership overhaul that also installed Mike Doustdar as CEO, accompanied by DKK 9B in one-off restructuring costs to streamline operations. Concurrently, Novo Nordisk narrowed its R&D strategy to focus on diabetes, obesity, and associated comorbidities, discontinuing several early-stage programs (DNA immunotherapy, oncology, stem cell platforms). Clinical development is organized by therapeutic area (Diabetes & Obesity, Cardiovascular, Rare Disease) with global trial operations run centrally. In the U.S., Clinical Development, Medical & Regulatory Affairs is led by a single SVP (Anna Windle) who integrates drug development, clinical operations, medical affairs, regulatory, HEOR, and safety functions. Internal governance is handled by a Research Portfolio Committee (early-stage decisions) and a Product Development Committee (late-stage decisions).

### Regulatory Affairs Structure
Global Regulatory Affairs is a centralized function headquartered in Denmark, with regional VP-level leads in North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. The U.S. regulatory function reports into the SVP of Clinical Development, Medical & Regulatory Affairs. Regulatory strategy is tightly linked to clinical development planning, particularly for the massive semaglutide franchise requiring coordinated global filings across multiple indications (obesity, cardiovascular risk reduction, CKD, MASH, HFpEF). Key regulatory milestones achieved or in progress: FDA approved Ozempic for CKD risk reduction (January 2025, based on FLOW trial showing 24% risk reduction in kidney disease progression); EMA CHMP positive opinion for Ozempic CKD label update (December 2024); FDA accepted Rybelsus sNDA for MACE/CKD risk reduction (decision expected October 2025); oral semaglutide 25mg (oral Wegovy) FDA decision expected Q4 2025; Wegovy for MASH FDA decision expected Q3 2025; CagriSema NDA submitted December 2025 (FDA decision expected late 2026). The Alzheimer's program (EVOKE/EVOKE+ Phase III trials) missed primary endpoints in 2025, though exploratory research continues with a Phase 2 study initiated in January 2026. The Disclosure Committee oversees capital market communications related to regulatory milestones.

### Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety Structure
Pharmacovigilance sits within the R&D Global Safety Department, now reporting into the consolidated R&D organization under CSO Martin Holst Lange. PV specialists are embedded across product teams and are responsible for safety data analysis, medical evaluations, development safety update reports (DSURs), and periodic benefit-risk evaluation reports (PBRERs). Findings are presented to an internal committee of senior safety experts for validation. The PV function faces an unprecedented signal detection workload driven by massive global semaglutide exposure (Ozempic/Wegovy/Rybelsus across 10+ million patients). Key safety monitoring areas in 2025-2026 include: (1) neuropsychiatric signals -- FAERS/VigiBase disproportionality analyses show semaglutide-specific (not class-wide) signals for depressive disorders; the Australian TGA aligned product warnings across the GLP-1 RA class for suicidal ideation risk in December 2025; (2) neurological adverse events -- population-scale FAERS analyses identify 19 distinct neurological AE signals for GLP-1 RAs, with semaglutide showing higher NAE proportions (16.97%) than tirzepatide (6.06%); (3) gastrointestinal safety -- 187,757 GLP-1 RA AEs reported in FAERS 2007-2023, with semaglutide showing broader GI AE range than comparators; (4) route-of-administration differences between injectable and oral semaglutide; (5) ongoing EMA/PRAC scrutiny (e.g., NAION risk assessment). The evidence increasingly supports drug-specific rather than purely class-level monitoring, adding complexity to the PV function. Off-label and misuse monitoring is also a growing concern as semaglutide prescriptions expand beyond indicated populations.

### Benefit-Risk Governance
Benefit-risk decisions are governed through the Product Development Committee at the enterprise level. At the product level, B/R assessments are embedded in clinical risk management plans (RMPs) required for marketing authorization and clinical trial applications. The internal committee of senior safety experts serves as a B/R validation body. B/R governance is under exceptional strain given: (1) the CagriSema REDEFINE 4 trial failure to demonstrate non-inferiority vs. tirzepatide (23.0% vs. 25.5% weight loss at 84 weeks) creates a complex B/R narrative for FDA review -- Novo must articulate differentiated benefit despite the head-to-head loss; (2) CagriSema NDA under FDA review with a mixed efficacy story (strong vs. placebo at 22.7% in REDEFINE 1, but falling short of market expectations and tirzepatide benchmark); (3) ongoing multi-indication expansion of semaglutide across diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular, renal, MASH, and HFpEF indications; (4) Alzheimer's program B/R reassessment after EVOKE Phase III failure; (5) amycretin entering Phase 3 in 2026 with both subcutaneous and oral formulations requiring parallel B/R governance tracks. The consolidated R&D organization under CSO Lange is expected to tighten B/R decision-making cycles.

## 5. Strategic Entry Angle
ArcaScience should position its BRA platform as a critical enabler for Novo Nordisk at a moment of acute strategic vulnerability. The company faces a convergence of B/R challenges unlike any in its history: (1) **CagriSema's mixed B/R narrative** -- the REDEFINE 4 failure to demonstrate non-inferiority vs. tirzepatide (23.0% vs. 25.5% weight loss) means the pending FDA review requires an exceptionally sophisticated benefit-risk argument; ArcaScience can demonstrate how dynamic, quantitative BRA modeling would build a stronger differentiation case (e.g., dual mechanism benefits, tolerability advantages, flexible dosing) that static assessments cannot capture; (2) **population-scale semaglutide pharmacovigilance** -- with 10M+ patients exposed globally, drug-specific safety signals (neuropsychiatric, neurological, GI) are emerging that require real-time, probabilistic signal detection rather than retrospective FAERS mining; (3) **amycretin dual-formulation Phase 3** -- as both subcutaneous and oral amycretin enter Phase 3 in 2026, Novo needs parallel B/R governance for two formulations across obesity and T2D, a complexity multiplier that dynamic BRA was designed for.

Target CSO Martin Holst Lange's mandate to "quickly advance innovation" by framing ArcaScience as the decision-support layer that enables faster, data-driven B/R decisions in the newly consolidated R&D organization. The Catalent manufacturing expansion (14 fill-finish sites) will dramatically increase semaglutide patient exposure, further amplifying PV complexity. The company's cost-consciousness following its stock decline and 2026 guidance cut means ROI demonstration is essential -- frame ArcaScience as protecting pipeline value (CagriSema projected at $16.4B peak revenue) rather than adding cost.
